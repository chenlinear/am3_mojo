# What makes am3_mojo faster

`benchmark.py` and `profile_stages.py` measure am3_mojo against the C++ AM³, as their docstrings describe. The main README gives the results under [Performance](../../README.md#performance-am3_mojo-4-threads-vs-the-c-original-serial). This file explains where the gain comes from, and the docstrings of the code it names give the details.

Most of the gain comes from better algorithms, not only from threads. On one thread, am3_mojo is already $2.8\text{–}4.1\times$ faster than the C++ AM³.

1. **Inverse-Compton emission from a 4.36 MB table instead of a 236.1 MB kernel** (the "photon coeff: pair compton em." stage). AM³ stores one dense float32 matrix per outgoing photon bin and streams those of the emitted bins (198 MB) through matrix–vector products every step, limited by memory bandwidth. On AM³'s logarithmic grids every branch of the cross section has the form $1/(\gamma_j^2 \varepsilon_i) \cdot \Phi(i + j, k - j)$ within the kinematic limits. The 3D kernel therefore factorises into a 2D table $\Phi$, which stays in cache, and the emission becomes a Hankel product for each $d = k - j$. At initialisation, am3_mojo compares the table with every entry of the original kernel and stores the 2,745 of 49.5 million entries it does not reproduce to $10^{-12}$ exactly (`ICKernel2D` in `src/am3_mojo/physics/inverse_compton.mojo`).
2. **Decay and photo-pion kernels as Toeplitz/Hankel products** (the "calc. coeff. neutrino", "calc. coeff. muon" and "calc. coeff. proton/neutron" stages). Pion and muon decay kernels depend only on an energy ratio, and photo-pion kernels only on an energy product ($\gamma_p \varepsilon$). On a logarithmic grid their matrices are therefore constant along diagonals (Toeplitz) or anti-diagonals (Hankel). am3_mojo detects this once per decay matrix of AM³ and multiplies with the $\sim \mathrm{rows} + \mathrm{cols}$ diagonal values as a SIMD convolution (`MatD` and `diagonal_gemv` in `src/am3_mojo/utils/linalg.mojo`). The 51 photo-pion channels and the kaon decay kernels are built in this form directly, and the channels are computed in parallel.
3. **Bethe–Heitler pair emission and the pair sources** (the "calc. coeff. pairs" stage, $17\text{–}32\times$ faster). am3_mojo packs the kernel into windows of its non-zero rows, read sequentially, and sums only over the proton bins that hold particles (`src/am3_mojo/physics/bethe_heitler.mojo`). The muon-decay pair sources use the Toeplitz products of point 2. With the full $\gamma\gamma$ kernel instead of AM³'s 14-bin approximation, the pair source sums each kernel row with a SIMD dot product that keeps four independent partial sums.
4. **Cooling rates once per species** (the "evolve pion" and "evolve muon" stages). AM³ recomputes the synchrotron and inverse-Compton cooling rates in each solver call, 6 for the pions and 12 for the muons, although they are the same for all components of a species. am3_mojo computes them once per step and species and shares them, and runs the species updates in parallel (`evolve_group` in `src/am3_mojo/simulation/manager.mojo`, `share_cooling` in `src/am3_mojo/solver/solver.mojo`, `src/am3_mojo/simulation/evolve.mojo`).
5. **SIMD everywhere.** The matrix–vector products, the IC and photo-pion kernels and the IC cooling rates use explicit SIMD vectors of $8 \times$ float64, or $16 \times$ float32 for the Bethe–Heitler kernel, and accumulate blocks of output rows in registers.
6. **Parallelism on the Mojo worker pool**, for IC and Bethe–Heitler emission, the photo-pion channels, the full pair-production source, all independent species updates and the construction of the kernels. Exactly one worker computes each output element, in a fixed order, so results are bitwise identical for any thread count. Kernel set-up is $3.3\text{–}3.7\times$ faster than in C++ for the hadronic models.
7. **Memory: 0.25 GB instead of 2.88 GB** (blazar model). The IC table and the Toeplitz/Hankel forms replace the large dense kernels. The full $\gamma\gamma$ pair kernel is allocated only when that treatment is on, and AM³'s copy of it, 1.9 GB, is most of AM³'s memory (`src/am3_mojo/physics/pair_production.mojo`). The Bethe–Heitler kernel (120 MB as float32) is the largest part of am3_mojo's peak. The arrays are allocated once and updated in place, and Mojo's ownership model avoids copies.
8. **Hadron inverse-Compton emission** (the "photon coeff: proton/pion/muon compton em." stages, $3.3\text{–}10\times$ faster). Instead of AM³'s loop over every pair of photon and hadron bins, am3_mojo adds the shifted target photons of each hadron bin that is not empty over the photon bins it reaches (`HadIC` in `src/am3_mojo/physics/decays.mojo`).
9. **Interpolation between the grids** (the "photons -> had. grid" stage, $37\text{–}39\times$ faster). AM³ copies the whole photon spectrum for each bin of the hadron grid it fills, every step. am3_mojo reads it in place, with weights computed once per grid (`grid_lep2had` in `src/am3_mojo/model/arrays.mojo`).

The grid sizes come from the physics, not from SIMD: 314 hadron, 340 lepton, 464 neutrino and 620 photon bins, at 10 bins per e-fold. With 8-wide tail blocks, a $314 \times 314$ product runs within about $20\text{％}$ of a padded $320 \times 320$ one per element, so the arrays are not padded.
