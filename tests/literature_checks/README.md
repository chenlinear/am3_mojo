# Literature checks

These scripts check am3_mojo's equations and input data against the papers they come from. They cover the four processes that am3_mojo adds to AM³, and AM³'s inverse-Compton loss and quantum-synchrotron cooling. The main README summarises them under [Checks against the literature](../../README.md#checks-against-the-literature), and their findings are under [Results](#results) below. They are not part of the test suite (`make test`).

## Derivation checks

The derivation checks (`derivations/`) prove that each equation of the main README is that of the source it cites, or follows from it, in the README's notation. Each check reads the equation from the README, and the source's form from the paper's TeX or from a transcription of its scan. It proves the two equal, exactly with sympy or to 50 digits at random points, and a control with one term broken must fail. For a modelling choice, the check derives what follows from it: a count, a conservation law, a limit or a continuity. The checks also recompute the figures that this file and [`tests/reference/README.md`](../reference/README.md) quote, and check the code's inputs and tables against the main README. An edited equation or figure therefore fails its check.

There is one module per added process (`annihilation.py`, `triplet.py`, `neutron_decay.py`, `kaon_production.py`, `kaon_evolution.py`, `kaon_decays.py`), and one each for AM³'s solver and cooling (`am3_cooling.py`) and kernels (`am3_kernels.py`). `run.py` runs them all, and `inventory.py` lists every equation with its source and its check. A module's docstring names its sources, and each check's docstring says what it proves.

The checks found these misprints in the sources, which the main README corrects:
- Svensson's eq. 6, in the argument of its logarithm (under [Results](#electronpositron-annihilation));
- Lee et al.'s eq. 4.5, which lacks the factor $\alpha^3$ of their exact cross section, and their eq. 4.3, which lacks a factor 2 in $J_{11}$;
- Hümmer et al.'s eq. 42, whose second and third steps do not follow from their eq. 27;
- Lipari et al.'s eq. 19, which prints $\mu_\mathrm{R}^+$ for $\mu_\mathrm{R}^-$, and Kelner et al.'s eq. 76, which prints the letter k for $\kappa$.

The misprints of the AM³ paper are in [`tests/reference/README.md`](../reference/README.md).

## Independent checks

The other scripts check what a derivation cannot. They print numbers to be read, not pass/fail results. They take the code's values from the code itself, through the harnesses of `tests/harness/` or the tables of `src/`, and every formula of the README or of a paper from `derivations/`. No script or test compares the code with a Python transcription of it. The Monte Carlo scripts are seeded, so their output is reproducible. Each script's docstring says what it checks, from which data, and with which options.
- `triplet/`: the QED code of `tools/triplet` (`machinery.py`), the code's cross section against the exact one and Haug's values (`compare_exact.py`), the inelasticity and the energy loss against the literature (`inelasticity.py`), the code's lab energies against the exact ones (`x_scaling.py`), and the extrapolation above $k = 10^{10}$ (`extrapolation.py`).
- `kaon_production/`: the two-body channels from the measured data (`two_body.py`), and the kaons of the multi-pion types against measured strange-particle photoproduction (`multibody_strangeness.py`, `m4_steps.py`). `code_tables.py` reads the code's tables for them.

The papers are not part of the repository. The scripts and modules name the tables and equations they transcribe.

## Running them

`make checks` (`tests/run_checks.py`) runs every script, together with the [solver checks](../solver_checks/README.md), and writes the outputs to `build/checks/`. The scripts need scipy, mpmath, sympy and vegas, which `uv sync` installs with the rest of the [development set-up](../../README.md#development-set-up). To run one script, from the repository root:

```bash
.venv/bin/python tests/literature_checks/derivations/run.py
```

```bash
.venv/bin/python tests/literature_checks/triplet/compare_exact.py --mc 7 45
```

## Results

What the checks found (October 2026), by process. The main README's sections of the added processes name the approximations whose size is given here. Script docstrings quote these figures as "README: …", and the derivation checks read many of them from this file.

### Electron–positron annihilation

- The equations cited from [Svensson (1982)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1982ApJ...258..321S), eqs. 18, 19b, 33a–d, 41 and 55–59, were checked against the paper (its scan in the NASA ADS). The treatment attributed to ATHEνA was checked against §3.9 of [Mastichiadis & Kirk (1995)](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/9408077). Svensson's eq. 6, Dirac's cross section (the main README's $\sigma_\mathrm{rest}$), prints the argument of its logarithm as $(1 + \beta_\mathrm{cm})/(1 + \beta_\mathrm{cm})$. His eq. 18, its integral (eq. 16), follows from $(1 + \beta_\mathrm{cm})/(1 - \beta_\mathrm{cm})$.
- $\sigma_\mathrm{rest}$ is the cross section of $\mathrm{e}^+\mathrm{e}^- \to \gamma\gamma$, from the Dirac trace of its two diagrams. $\langle\sigma v\rangle$ is its angle average with the Møller flux factor, through the primitive $G(x)$. Both hold exactly (`derivations/annihilation.py`).
- The photon spectrum (eqs. 55–58) has the normalisation $\int v\ d\sigma/dk\ dk = 2\langle\sigma v\rangle$ and $\int k v\ d\sigma/dk\ dk$ $= (\gamma_+ + \gamma_-)\langle\sigma v\rangle$ (`derivations/annihilation.py`). The normalisation of the spectrum (to $1.4 \times 10^{-30}$, in 30-digit quadrature), its symmetry and peaks, and Svensson's closed form at $k = \gamma_+ = \gamma_-$ were checked too. Eq. 41 is the limit $\gamma_- \to 1$ of this spectrum. Its primitives $N(k)$ and $E(k)$ (below) were checked by differentiation.
- The treatments of the other codes were compared with the exact result (`derivations/annihilation.py`). ATHEνA's rate is the fit of [Coppi & Blandford](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/245.3.453) that [Mastichiadis & Kirk (1995)](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/9408077) give in their eq. 59, $3/(8\gamma)\thinspace[\gamma^{-1/2} + \ln\gamma]$ (in $\sigma_T c$). This rate is $-14\text{％}$ at $\gamma = 2.2$ and $+6\text{％}$ at $\gamma \approx 100$. The photon spectrum of [Böttcher & Schlickeiser](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/9506112) (1996, their eq. 16) has $3\text{–}15\text{％}$ too many photons for Lorentz factors from 5 to $10^4$. The rates of their eqs. 6 and 33 are $-12\text{％}$ and $-17\text{％}$ at $\gamma = 5$.
- **The code's rate:** where $p_+p_- \lt 10^{-2}\ \gamma_r^+$, the difference $G(\gamma_r^+) - G(\gamma_r^-)$ would cancel. There the code computes the rate as $c\langle G'\rangle/(\gamma_+\gamma_-)$, with $\langle G'\rangle$ the 8-point Gauss–Legendre mean of $G' = \sqrt{x^2 - 1}\thinspace \sigma_\mathrm{rest}(x)$ over $[\gamma_r^-, \gamma_r^+]$. It computes $\gamma_r^-$ as $(\gamma_+^2 + \gamma_-^2 - 1)/\gamma_r^+$.
- **One lepton at rest:** the code integrates eq. 41 over each bin in closed form. With $D = 1 + \gamma$, $B = 3 + \gamma$, $A = -B/D$, $C = 3\sigma_T/(8p\gamma)$ and $L = \ln k - \ln(D - k)$, the photon number and energy have the primitives $N(k) = C[1/k + (B - 2/D) L - 1/(D - k) - 2k]$ and $E(k) = C[A D^2 \ln(D - k) - L - D/(D - k) - k^2]$.
- **Three-photon annihilation**, which the code neglects, is a fraction $4(\pi^2 - 9)\alpha/(3\pi) \approx 1/371$ of two-photon annihilation for slow pairs ([Berestetskii, Lifshitz & Pitaevskii](https://doi.org/10.1016/C2009-0-24486-2), §89).
- **Regime 4 of AM³'s solver:** where the solver's regime 4 (cooling only) applies and annihilation is below $1\text{％}$ of the cooling, the solver neglects the sink. The photon source still counts those leptons.

### Triplet pair production

- **Expansions and interpolation** (`compare_exact.py`): the threshold and high-energy expansions agree with the tables to $4 \times 10^{-5}$ ($k = 4.02$) and $2 \times 10^{-4}$ ($k \ge 10^3$). The cubic spline between the nodes is within $1.8 \times 10^{-4}$ of the exact cross section.
- **QED machinery** of `tools/triplet` (`machinery.py`): Compton scattering computed with the same spinors and gamma matrices agrees with the Klein–Nishina formula to $10^{-15}$. The triplet amplitude satisfies the Ward identity: with the photon's polarisation replaced by its momentum, $\vert M\vert^2$ is $\lesssim 10^{-16}$ of $\vert M\vert^2$ up to $k = 10^7$. It is invariant under longitudinal boosts, to $2 \times 10^{-15}$ at $k = 5$, $10^{-12}$ at $10^4$ and $10^{-9}$ at $10^7$. The faster implementation of $\vert M\vert^2$ that made the tables agrees with it to $6 \times 10^{-10}$ in that range.
- **Cross section:** [Haug](https://doi.org/10.1515/zna-1975-0901) gives numerical values of the exact lowest-order cross section (1975, Table 1: 58 values for $4 \lt k \le 5000$, to three or four digits; `compare_exact.py`, `derivations/triplet.py`). The Monte Carlo agrees with them to Haug's precision. At the 14 of his energies that are table nodes, it is within $7 \times 10^{-4}$ (at two of them, $k = 4.02$ and $10^3$, the code uses the expansions). At $k = 4.03, 7, 10, 12$ and 45, which are not nodes, it is within $5.2 \times 10^{-4}$. The expansions the code uses also agree to Haug's precision (within $6 \times 10^{-4}$ for $k \le 4.02$ and $k \ge 10^3$). Between the nodes the code's cubic spline is within $8 \times 10^{-4}$ of Haug's values (and within $1.6 \times 10^{-4}$ of the Monte Carlo at those five energies).
- **Exact cross section:** [Lee et al.](https://doi.org/10.1007/JHEP01%282021%29144) give the exact total cross section through one-fold integrals (their eqs. 4.3–4.4). These are evaluated here in 30-digit arithmetic (`derivations/triplet.py`), and `compare_exact.py` checks the code against this evaluation. Lee et al.'s eq. 4.3 as printed, in the journal version as in arXiv v1, lacks a factor 2 in $J_{11}$. Their own differential system (eq. 4.2) requires this factor: of the integrals $J_3 \ldots J_{12}$ of eq. 4.3, only $J_{11}$ does not solve eq. 4.2, by a factor $1/{2}$. Without the factor, eq. 4.4 is $-28$ times the threshold expansion at $k = 4.001$. With it, eq. 4.4 agrees with the threshold expansion to $3 \times 10^{-10}$ there and with the high-energy expansion to $O(k^{-2})$. Against this corrected cross section, the Monte Carlo nodes agree to $1.8 \times 10^{-4}$ (rms $8 \times 10^{-5}$ above $k = 10^3$). The code's $\sigma(k)$ agrees to $2 \times 10^{-6}$ in the threshold expansion, $1.8 \times 10^{-4}$ along the spline and $2.3 \times 10^{-5}$ in the high-energy expansion. The threshold expansion is $10^{-3}$ below the exact cross section at $k = 4.2$ and $8 \times 10^{-3}$ at 4.5. At these energies the series' next, unknown term is of this size. [Haug](https://doi.org/10.1515/zna-1975-0901)'s values are $2.4 \times 10^{-4}$ low on average (46 of the 58 lie below the exact cross section, by up to $8 \times 10^{-4}$). Most of the code's deviation from them is therefore theirs. The triplet cross section exceeds the Klein–Nishina cross section above $k = 272$.
- **Inelasticity:** $k\langle x_+ + x_\mathrm{low}\rangle/\ln^2(2k)$ from the tables falls from 0.44 at $k = 11$ to 0.349 at $k \approx 500$ and rises again to 0.393 at $k = 10^{10}$.
  - The fit of [Anguelov et al. (1999)](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0001252) is $0.195 \ln^2(2k)/k$ per produced particle (their eq. 9; 0.39 for the pair). It is $9\text{–}12\text{％}$ high for $10^2 \le k \le 10^4$ and within $2\text{％}$ above $10^7$. Their numerical values before the fit (their Table 2: 0.100, 0.0202 and 0.00354 for the pair at $k = 10^2, 10^3$ and $10^4$) agree with the tables within $1\text{％}$.
  - [Mastichiadis](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/253.2.235)' fit as [Lee (1998)](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/9604098) quotes it, $\eta = 1.768(s/m_e^2)^{-3/4}$ (his eq. 25, with $s = m_e^2(1 + 2k)$), is 3.4 times too low at $k = 10^3$ and 1.8 times at $10^7$. It crosses the exact value near $10^9$ and is 1.5 times too high at $10^{10}$.
  - [Mastichiadis](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/253.2.235)' own fit (his eq. 26, isotropic photons) gives a mean energy of $2.5(\gamma\varepsilon)^{1/4}/\varepsilon$ per produced particle, so the pair takes $5(\gamma\varepsilon)^{-3/4}$ of the lepton's energy. The fit is 1.6 times too high at $\gamma\varepsilon = 10^3$ and $10^4$, 1.8 times at $10^5$, 2.15 at $10^6$ and 3.7 at $10^8$. His loss rate (eq. 27) is 1.55 and 1.54 times too high at $10^3$ and $10^4$, 1.73 at $10^5$, 2.12 at $10^6$ and 3.70 at $10^8$.
- **Energy loss** in monoenergetic isotropic photons: the analytic rate of [Dermer & Schlickeiser (1991)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1991A%26A...252..414D), $-\dot\gamma$ $= (4/3)\alpha_f c\sigma_T n\ (\gamma/\varepsilon)^{1/2}$ (their eq. 7), overestimates the exact one at every $\gamma\varepsilon$. The overestimate is $1.25\text{–}3$ for $30 \lesssim \gamma\varepsilon \lesssim 3 \times 10^5$, and 10 at $\gamma\varepsilon = 10$ and 8 at $10^7$ (exact/DS: 0.10, 0.34, 0.60, 0.80, 0.66, 0.43, 0.25, 0.13 at $\gamma\varepsilon = 10, 30, 10^2, \ldots, 10^7$). They quote a factor 2 over four decades and 3 over five and a half against [Mastichiadis](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/253.2.235)' numerical results.
- **Against inverse Compton:** the exact Klein–Nishina loss rate (`derivations/am3_cooling.py`) is within $0.22\text{％}$ of its Klein–Nishina limit, $-\dot\gamma$ $= (3/8) c\sigma_T n\varepsilon^{-1}$ $[\ln(4\gamma\varepsilon) - 11/6]$, for $\gamma\varepsilon \ge 10^3$. The limit comes from eq. 2.57 of [Blumenthal & Gould 1970](https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.42.237), $-dE/d{t}$ $= \pi r_0^2 m^2c^5 \int d\varepsilon\ n(\varepsilon)$ $\varepsilon^{-1}$ $[\ln(4\varepsilon\gamma/mc^2) - 11/6]$, which [Mastichiadis](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/253.2.235) writes as $\pi r_e^2 c n\varepsilon^{-1}$ $[\ln(4\gamma\varepsilon) - 11/6]$, his eq. 28. This was checked against both papers. The triplet loss is 0.32 of the inverse Compton loss at $\gamma\varepsilon = 10^5$ and 0.48 at $10^6$, and exceeds it above $\gamma\varepsilon = 2.6 \times 10^8$ ([Anguelov et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0001252) estimate $1.6 \times 10^8$). Mastichiadis' loss rate (eq. 27) exceeds the inverse Compton loss above $\gamma\varepsilon = 9 \times 10^5$ (he gives $(4\alpha_f)^{-4} = 1.4 \times 10^6$).
- **Why the tables stop at $k = 10^{10}$** (`machinery.py`): above it, the gauge cancellations between the diagrams approach the limit of double precision. The Ward identity holds to $10^{-11}$ of $\vert M\vert^2$ at $k = 10^{10}$, $2 \times 10^{-8}$ at $10^{12}$ and $10^{-4}$ at $10^{14}$. The amplitude is therefore accurate to about the square root of that: $3 \times 10^{-6}$, $1.5 \times 10^{-4}$ and $10^{-2}$. At $10^{12}$ the mean energies of the products still continue those below smoothly. At $10^{14}$, with the adaptive maps of `tools/triplet`, the positron's mean energy and the recoil's energy loss come out about $10^6$ times too large. The lower-energy electron's mean energy comes out 30 times too large. The total cross section, however, still agrees with [Lee et al.](https://doi.org/10.1007/JHEP01%282021%29144).
- **The energy the returning lepton loses per interaction** (`inelasticity.py`): its mean in isotropic photons, $\langle\xi\rangle$, is 0.085 at $\gamma\varepsilon = 10^2$, 0.018 at $10^3$ and $7 \times 10^{-5}$ at $10^6$. At the highest of these energies, the process takes half as much energy as inverse Compton. The code's continuous loss neglects the spread of this loss. In interactions where the lepton loses more than a grid bin ($\xi \gt 0.095$), it loses $41\text{％}$ of the total at $k = 10^3$, $24\text{％}$ at $10^5$ and $20\text{％}$ at $10^6$. In interactions where it loses more than half of its energy, the share is $3\text{–}6\text{％}$.
- **Lab energies** (`x_scaling.py`): spectra are compared by half the L1 distance between them, in bins of the grid's 0.1 in $\ln E$. Against the exact lab energies of the QED events, boosted for isotropic photons, the binned positron spectrum changes:
  - for $\varepsilon \le 0.1\ m_e c^2$ by about as much as the comparison resolves: $0.1\text{–}0.9\text{％}$ (mean energy $\le 6 \times 10^{-4}$), against $0.1\text{–}0.8\text{％}$ between two exact spectra differing only in the random azimuth. Yet $2.1\text{–}2.4\text{％}$ (at $0.01\ m_e c^2$) and $22\text{–}24\text{％}$ (at $0.1\ m_e c^2$) of the positrons land in a neighbouring bin, in both directions;
  - by $2.5\text{–}2.8\text{％}$ at $0.3\ m_e c^2$ (mean energy $0.1\text{–}0.4\text{％}$);
  - by $19\text{–}23\text{％}$ at $m_e c^2$ (mean energy $1\text{–}3\text{％}$);
  - the recoil's spectrum by less than $0.03\text{％}$.
- **Products below the lepton grid** (`tests/solver_checks/triplet_below_grid.py`): on target photons above about $0.25\ m_e c^2$, some products get lab energies $x\gamma$ below the lowest bins of the lepton grid ($\gamma \lt 1.16$). This happens for any $\gamma$. These products go into bin 0, which AM³'s solver empties every step (as it empties bin 1). Their photons are still absorbed, though, and the lepton still loses their energy. Of the pairs, $1\text{％}$ are lost on photons of $0.3\ m_e c^2$, $9\text{–}10\text{％}$ at $0.5\ m_e c^2$ and $30\text{–}34\text{％}$ at $m_e c^2$ (with up to $0.1\text{％}$, $1.4\text{％}$ and $6\text{％}$ of the pairs' energy). In `leptohadronic_all`, at the end of the run, $69\text{％}$ of the triplet interactions are on photons above $0.25\ m_e c^2$, and $15\text{％}$ of the triplet positrons are lost this way.
- **AM³'s solver** integrates the continuous loss as it integrates AM³'s other continuous losses:
  - In its Chang & Cooper regime (slow cooling), the leptons lose $(1 - e^{-\Delta})/\Delta = 0.952$ of the pairs' energy at time steps much shorter than the energy-loss time. With synchrotron cooling the same regime removes $0.95\text{–}0.96$ of the energy the synchrotron photons carry at such time steps.
  - Where the loss is below $1\text{％}$ of the escape rate, the solver neglects it, as it neglects AM³'s own losses there.
- **Time stepping** (`tests/solver_checks/triplet_steady_states.py`): the pairs of a step come from the leptons at its start, as AM³'s sources do. Number and energy are kept exactly in a steady state (on photons below about $0.25\ m_e c^2$; see products below the lepton grid above). Outside a steady state they are kept to first order in the time step. Both hold up to the accuracy of AM³'s solver. In steady states of electrons in photons of $3 \times 10^{15}$ to $10^{17}$ cm⁻³ at $0.03\ m_e c^2$, the process dominates the cooling. There the triplet loss reaches 38 to 1250 times the escape rate. In these states, at time steps of 0.3 to 1 escape time, the positrons that escape equal the photons absorbed to $0.4\text{％}$. The escaping power equals the injected power to $0.7\text{％}$ at these steps. At 10 escape times both hold to $3\text{％}$. At shorter steps the solver's Chang & Cooper regime takes over, in which the leptons lose only 0.952 of the pairs' energy (above). The escaping power then exceeds the injected power by up to $2.5\text{％}$ at 0.1 escape times and by $4\text{–}12\text{％}$ at 0.03. In the densest field the positrons then exceed the photons absorbed by $1\text{％}$ and $6\text{％}$. Each generation of a cascade takes one step.
- **Extrapolation above $k = 10^{10}$:** fitted to fewer nodes, the asymptotic laws reproduce the nodes left out as follows.
  - One decade beyond the fitted range: within $1\text{％}$ in the distributions (L1; $1\text{–}3\text{％}$ weighted with energy) and $0.1\text{–}2\text{％}$ in the mean energies.
  - Three decades beyond: within $2.5\text{–}6.4\text{％}$ in the mean energies (mostly the recoil).

### Neutron decay

- **Inputs** (`derivations/neutron_decay.py`): the code uses the masses and the mean life of the [PDG 2026](https://pdg.lbl.gov/2026/) listings of the neutron, the proton and the electron. It takes $\alpha$ from the PDG 2026 table of constants. With these inputs the proton recoils with at most 751.2 eV ([Dubbers & Schmidt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.3694): 750 eV).
- **The spectrum** (`derivations/neutron_decay.py`): with the masses and $\alpha$ of PDG 2026, the electron's mean kinetic energy is 0.3011 MeV and the antineutrino's mean energy 0.4805 MeV. For an ultra-relativistic neutron the antineutrino takes $5.11 \times 10^{-4}$ of the energy on average ([Lipari et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/0704.0718): $\simeq 5.1 \times 10^{-4}$), up to $x = 2(E_0 - m_e)/m_n = 1.664 \times 10^{-3}$. Lipari et al. give $E_{\bar\nu}^{\max}/E_n$ $\simeq 2(m_n - m_p - m_e)/m_n$ $\simeq 1.66 \times 10^{-3}$. On average the electron takes $8.64 \times 10^{-4}$ and the proton 0.99862. The Fermi function raises the normalised spectrum by $37\text{％}$ at 1 keV and $8.5\text{％}$ at 10 keV, and lowers it by $0.85\text{％}$ near the end point. Without the Fermi function the mean kinetic energy would be $0.44\text{％}$ higher.
- **Recoil** (`derivations/neutron_decay.py`): the exact three-body decay is the Dalitz plot with the matrix element of the vector and axial couplings, using $\lambda = g_A/g_V = -1.2753$ of [PDG 2026](https://pdg.lbl.gov/2026/). This matrix element is the V−A trace with explicit Dirac matrices, exactly. Against this exact decay, the normalised spectra differ by up to $2 \times 10^{-3}$ below $90\text{％}$ of the end point. The electron's mean kinetic energy and the antineutrino's mean energy differ by $-4.1 \times 10^{-4}$ and $-5.6 \times 10^{-4}$. With [PDG](https://pdg.lbl.gov/2026/)'s convention for $\lambda$, the trace gives PDG's $A = -2\lambda(\lambda + 1)/(1 + 3\lambda^2)$ and $a = (1 - \lambda^2)/(1 + 3\lambda^2)$ at zero recoil. The proton's mean kinetic energy is 357 eV, whereas the code gives every proton 751 eV. This changes the proton's energy fraction by $4 \times 10^{-7}$.
- **Radiative corrections** (`derivations/neutron_decay.py`): the outer radiative correction of [Sirlin](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.164.1767) ([Hayen et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.07530), eq. 50) would change the electron spectrum. The change would range from $+0.4\text{％}$ at 10 keV to $-1.4\text{％}$ at 750 keV, and the electron's mean kinetic energy would change by $-1.5 \times 10^{-3}$. The photons of the radiative decays are not made. The relativistic and finite-size terms of the Fermi function (Hayen et al.'s eq. 6 with $\gamma_1$ and the proton's radius) change the spectrum by at most $2.4 \times 10^{-4}$. Bound decay into a hydrogen atom, $4.2 \times 10^{-6}$ of the decays (Hayen et al.), is left out.
- **Other codes** (`derivations/neutron_decay.py`): ATHEνA's protons, with the neutron's Lorentz factor, take $m_p/m_n$ of its energy, $8 \times 10^{-7}$ less than in the code. The electrons and antineutrinos that ATHEνA leaves out carry $1.38 \times 10^{-3}$. Its $\tau = 881.5$ s ([PDG 2010](https://doi.org/10.1088/0954-3899/37/7A/075021) and its [2011 update](https://pdg.lbl.gov/2011/)) gives decay rates $0.36\text{％}$ lower than [PDG 2026](https://pdg.lbl.gov/2026/)'s. [Hümmer et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1002.1310)'s line at $\chi = 5.1 \times 10^{-4}$, $Q_{\bar\nu}(E) = Q_n(E/\chi)/\chi$ (their eq. 50), gives the antineutrinos of decays $\propto E_n^{-2}$ (per unit energy) to $3 \times 10^{-3}$. For steeper spectra it gives too few: decays $\propto E_n^{-2.5}$, $E_n^{-3}$ and $E_n^{-3.5}$ make 1.19, 1.50 and 1.97 times as many antineutrinos as the line.

### Kaon production

- **Equations and data:** the photo-hadronic equations were checked against the TeX source of [Hümmer et al. (2010)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1002.1310). [Koers et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ph/0611219)'s eqs. 16–18 were checked against the TeX source of their paper. So were the 60 values of their Table III that the code uses (the $\mathrm{K}^+$, $\mathrm{K}^-$, $\mathrm{K}^0$, $\bar{\mathrm{K}}^0$ and $\pi^0$ columns). Integrating the formulas again reproduces the multiplicity and energy tables of the $\mathrm{pp}$ section. The two-body cross sections and angles were rebuilt, to the last digit, from the HEPData records of SAPHIR, CLAS and A2 and from the published CLAS $\mathrm{K}^0\Lambda$ tables.
  - **Two-body channels** (`two_body.py`):
    - *The data used:* only the bins measured over most of the angular range are kept. The measured range must reach from $\cos\theta \le -0.6$ to $\ge 0.6$ ($\pm 0.7$ for A2, $-0.5$ for the CLAS $\mathrm{K}^0\Lambda$ tables and $-0.1$ for CLAS $\mathrm{K}^+\Sigma^-$). Above 2.85 GeV the CLAS $\mathrm{K}^+\Sigma^-$ data reach backward only to $\cos\theta = -0.4 \ldots {-0.1}$. $\gamma\mathrm{n} \to \mathrm{K}^0\Sigma^0$ is measured from its threshold on. A2's first bin, $W = 1675\text{–}1705$ MeV, is centred 0.5 MeV below the threshold in photon energy, so the cross section starts at that bin's 0.15 μb.
    - *The angular distributions:* for $\mathrm{K}^+\Lambda$, $\langle\cos\theta\rangle$ rises from 0.1 near threshold to 0.7 at 3.5 GeV. This forward peaking lowers $\chi$ for $\mathrm{p} \to \mathrm{K}^+$ below its isotropic value, by $5\text{％}$ near threshold and by more than half at $3\text{–}5$ GeV. Near threshold the $\mathrm{K}^0\mathrm{Y}$ reactions are backward-peaked. The peaking is clear for $\gamma\mathrm{n} \to \mathrm{K}^0\Lambda$. For $\mathrm{K}^0\Sigma^0$ and $\mathrm{K}^0\Sigma^+$ it holds only within their errors, as it does for the lowest CLAS $\mathrm{K}^+\Sigma^0$ bin. This backward peaking raises the $\mathrm{n} \to \mathrm{K}^0$ $\chi$ by $7\text{–}8\text{％}$ below 1.25 GeV.
    - *The analogous reactions:* the factor 1.7 is measured near threshold. Pure $\mathrm{K}^\ast$ exchange would make it 0.5 at high energies. This would lower the $\mathrm{n} \to \mathrm{K}^0$ cross section above 1.65 GeV by up to $40\text{％}$.
    - *The experiments:* SAPHIR and CLAS differ by up to 0.15 ($\mathrm{K}^+\Lambda$) and 0.18 ($\mathrm{K}^+\Sigma^0$) in $\langle\cos\theta\rangle$ at $1.6\text{–}2.4$ GeV. This makes $\chi$ uncertain by about $\pm 0.02$. For $\mathrm{p} \to \mathrm{K}^0$ above 2.2 GeV, where only SAPHIR's sparse $\mathrm{K}^0\Sigma^+$ data are used, the uncertainty is about $\pm 0.04$. The experiments also differ in normalisation. CLAS's $\mathrm{K}^+\Lambda$ cross sections are $27\text{–}39\text{％}$ above SAPHIR's at $1.25\text{–}1.9$ GeV (averaged over SAPHIR's bins). Below 1.25 GeV, CLAS's $\mathrm{K}^0\Lambda$ cross sections are $1.25\text{–}2.2$ times A2's. Taking either experiment alone would therefore change the $\mathrm{p} \to \mathrm{K}^+$ cross sections of the steps that both cover ($1.05\text{–}3.0$ GeV) by up to $11\text{％}$. The $\mathrm{n} \to \mathrm{K}^0$ cross sections of the first two steps would change by $-29 \ldots {+42}\text{％}$ and $\pm 17\text{％}$.
    - *Hümmer et al.'s $\mathrm{K}^+$ channel* (`derivations/kaon_production.py`): the first three steps of the main README's table refine [Hümmer et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1002.1310)'s $\mathrm{K}^+$ channel (2.0 μb at $1.0\text{–}1.2$ GeV and 3.7 μb at $1.2\text{–}1.65$ GeV, their eqs. 41–42). The refinement uses the measured energy dependence ($17\text{％}$ more $\int\sigma\thinspace d\epsilon_r$ over $0.91\text{–}1.65$ GeV). Above 1.65 GeV, the measured fall-off replaces their constant 2.7 μb to the highest energies. Hümmer et al.'s printed response function for their $\mathrm{K}^+$ channel (eq. 42) does not follow from their eq. 27 for the second and third step. Eq. 27 gives 3.7 μb $[1 - 1.20\ \mathrm{GeV}^2/(2y)^2]$ and 2.7 μb $[1 - 0.64\ \mathrm{GeV}^2/(2y)^2]$, against the printed 0.3 and 0.16 GeV². The code's steps use the exact integral, the closed form of the main README.
  - **Kaons of the multi-pion types against the two-body reactions** (`multibody_strangeness.py`): the two-body channels share the energy ranges of M2 and M3. Do the multiplicities of these types repeat the two-body reactions? They were compared with measured strange-particle photoproduction on protons below 10 GeV, and those of M4 with the measurement at 20 GeV (`m4_steps.py`).
    - *M2 ($0.9\text{–}1.5$ GeV).* A kaon can be made with a third particle only above 1.15 GeV. The threshold of $\gamma\mathrm{N} \to \mathrm{K}\Lambda\pi$ is $1.152\text{–}1.168$ GeV for the four charge states, and $\mathrm{K}\Sigma\pi$ starts at 1.30 GeV. An antikaon comes only with a kaon pair, from 1.51 GeV on ($\gamma\mathrm{N} \to \mathrm{N}\mathrm{K}\bar{\mathrm{K}}$), so M2 makes none (these thresholds: `derivations/kaon_production.py`). Below 1.15 GeV the two-body channels hold all kaons, so the kaons of M2 start there. Between 1.15 and 1.5 GeV the measured three-body cross sections add up to $0.5\text{–}1.4$ μb for protons. Their error is about $40\text{％}$, nearly all of it the statistical error of ABBHHM's $\mathrm{K}^0\Lambda\pi^+$. The sum uses $\gamma\mathrm{p} \to \mathrm{K}^+\Sigma^\pm\pi^\mp$ of SAPHIR ([Schulday et al. 2010](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.0823)), $\mathrm{K}^0\pi^0\Sigma^+$ of CBELSA/TAPS ([Nanova et al. 2008](https://arxiv.org/abs/0803.2146)) and $\mathrm{K}^0\Lambda\pi^+$ of ABBHHM. ABBHHM's value, $0.74 \pm 0.34$ μb for $1.2\text{–}1.7$ GeV, is counted either evenly over that range or only above 1.42 GeV, the threshold of $\Sigma(1385)\mathrm{K}$. Much of this cross section goes through that channel ($\Sigma^+(1385)\mathrm{K}^0$: $0.68 \pm 0.48$ μb at $1.42\text{–}2.0$ GeV, ABBHHM's Table VII). $\mathrm{K}^+\Lambda\pi^0$ is not measured and was taken as $0.5\text{–}1$ times $\mathrm{K}^0\Lambda\pi^+$, and $\mathrm{K}^+\Sigma^0\pi^0$ and $\mathrm{K}^0\Sigma^0\pi^+$ as $\mathrm{K}^0\pi^0\Sigma^+$. M2 takes the middle of the resulting range, per species and with the weight $1/{E}$, as the steps of M3 do. This gives 0.43 μb of $\mathrm{K}^+$ and 0.51 μb of $\mathrm{K}^0$, 0.94 μb in all.
    - *M3 ($1.5\text{–}5$ GeV), ABBHHM.* The Aachen–Berlin–Bonn–Hamburg–Heidelberg–München collaboration ([Erbe et al. 1969](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.188.2060), Phys. Rev. 188, 2060) used a hydrogen bubble chamber in a bremsstrahlung beam of up to 5.8 GeV. The paper lists its 1312 events with strange particles by reaction (Table VI): 306 two-body, 725 with a hyperon and further particles, and 281 with a kaon pair. Corrected for unseen decays and normalised to the code's two-body steps (145 events per μb of $\int\sigma\thinspace dE/E$, for a photon flux $\propto 1/E$), the events with a hyperon and further particles correspond to $\int\sigma\thinspace dE/E = 7.1$ μb up to 5.8 GeV, and those with a kaon pair to $3.2\text{–}3.8$ μb. The paper's own cross sections of five reactions give $129\text{–}182$ events per μb. One multiplicity for the whole of M3 could give these totals, but not the rise of the yields across the type: it would make too many kaons at $1.5\text{–}2.5$ GeV and too few above.
    - *The two steps of M3* are set from the same data, as means over a step's photon energies with the weight $1/{E}$. For events with a kaon pair, the cross section of $\gamma\mathrm{p} \to \mathrm{p}\mathrm{K}^+\mathrm{K}^-$ (0.30 μb at $1.5\text{–}2.5$ GeV and 0.83 μb at $2.5\text{–}5$ GeV, Table V) is multiplied by the $\mathrm{K}\ \bar{\mathrm{K}}$ events per $\mathrm{p}\mathrm{K}^+\mathrm{K}^-$ event of Table VI. This ratio is 2.1 in the lower step (the reactions without a pion; those with one start at 1.8 GeV) and $4.1\text{–}4.9$ in the upper one, which gives 0.6 μb and $3.4\text{–}4.1$ μb. For events with a hyperon and further particles at $1.5\text{–}2.5$ GeV, the reactions with measured cross sections add up to 2.2 μb, $35\text{％}$ of the events: $\Lambda\mathrm{K}^0\pi^+$ and $\Lambda\mathrm{K}^+\pi^+\pi^-$ in ABBHHM's Fig. 29, $\Sigma^\pm\mathrm{K}^+\pi^\mp$ of SAPHIR (0.58 and 0.20 μb, against 0.44 and 0.18 μb from ABBHHM's two bins) and $\mathrm{K}^0\pi^0\Sigma^+$ of CBELSA/TAPS. The CBELSA/TAPS cross section is counted together with the third of their $\mathrm{K}^{\ast 0}\Sigma^+$ that decays into $\mathrm{K}^0\pi^0$, which their paper gives separately above 1.85 GeV. With the unmeasured three-body partners of these reactions ($1.0\text{–}1.5$ μb) and the other reactions with two pions (0.6 μb), they give $3.9\text{–}4.4$ μb, and at most 6.4 μb if every reaction rose as early as the measured ones. The rest of these events, after those of M2 and of the lower step, then gives $5.5\text{–}5.8$ μb above 2.5 GeV. ABBHHM's steps are the middle of each range, split into kaon species as Table VI's events: 2.76, 2.05, 0.30 and 0.34 μb of $\mathrm{K}^+$, $\mathrm{K}^0$, $\mathrm{K}^-$ and $\bar{\mathrm{K}}^0$ at $1.5\text{–}2.5$ GeV, and 5.32, 4.08, 1.95 and 1.83 μb at $2.5\text{–}5$ GeV. The code multiplies them by 1.21 and 1.32, from the events with a visible decay of SLAC and DESY (below). Up to 5.8 GeV its kaons are then $33\text{％}$ ($\mathrm{K}^+$), $37\text{％}$ ($\mathrm{K}^0$), $32\text{％}$ ($\mathrm{K}^-$) and $60\text{％}$ ($\bar{\mathrm{K}}^0$) above ABBHHM's. Its events with a hyperon and further particles are $30\text{％}$ above ABBHHM's, and those with a kaon pair $44\text{–}73\text{％}$, counting the start of M4, whose lower step stands for $5\text{–}15$ GeV.
    - *M3 and M4, SLAC and DESY.* The SLAC 82-inch bubble chamber, with photons of one energy, gives the cross section of events with a visible strange-particle decay: $4.4 \pm 0.9$, $8.1 \pm 0.5$ and $8.7 \pm 0.4$ μb at 1.44, 2.8 and 4.7 GeV ([Ballam et al. 1972](https://inspirehep.net/literature/67165), Phys. Rev. D 5, 545, Table V), and $9.8 \pm 0.4$ μb at 9.3 GeV ([Bingham et al. 1973](https://inspirehep.net/literature/81022), Phys. Rev. D 8, 1277, Table IV). With the same visibilities and ABBHHM's mix of hyperons, the visible decays of the code's kaons and of the hyperons that balance their strangeness give 3.6, 7.6, 7.1 and 9.4 μb. The code is thus $19\text{％}$ below at 1.44 GeV, where $81\text{％}$ is two-body, $6\text{％}$ and $18\text{％}$ below at 2.8 and 4.7 GeV, and $4\text{％}$ below at 9.3 GeV ($16\text{％}$ above with one step at the values measured at 20 GeV). The DESY streamer chamber gives the same in eight bins of tagged photons from 1.6 to 6.3 GeV ([Struczinski et al. 1976](https://inspirehep.net/literature/100089), Nucl. Phys. B 108, 45, Table 2, which its HEPData record leaves out). These values are not corrected for the decays the chamber did not see, so they are lower limits. Averaged over each bin with the weight $1/{E}$, the code's visible decays are $7\text{–}22\text{％}$ below them at $1.9\text{–}2.6$ and $3.25\text{–}5$ GeV, and $8\text{–}14\text{％}$ above in the other three bins. With no decay lost at all, they are still $6\text{–}15\text{％}$ below at $2.1\text{–}2.6$ and $3.25\text{–}5$ GeV. DESY needs two tracks for a vertex. If it could not count the events whose only charged particle from the vertex comes with a V (most two-body events among them), the gap would be wider: these events account for up to 0.6 μb of the code's visible decays above 2.6 GeV. From 2 to 5.8 GeV, DESY's events give $\int\sigma\thinspace dE/E = 8.2 \pm 0.3$ (stat.) $\pm 0.4$ (norm.) μb, 1.5 times ABBHHM's value and 0.95 times SLAC's. The code has 7.5 μb ($-8\text{％}$).
    - *The factors from the three experiments.* In none of the three data sets do the multiplicities of M3 repeat the two-body reactions: all need the two-body channels on top. ABBHHM and SLAC disagree with each other. Integrated over ABBHHM's photon spectrum, the SLAC cross sections give 1.36 times the 1185 events with a visible decay that ABBHHM found, and about 1.5 times above 2 GeV. Their normalisations do not differ by as much. SLAC's cross sections of $\gamma\mathrm{p} \to \mathrm{p}\mathrm{K}^+\mathrm{K}^-$ are $1.42 \pm 0.26$ and $0.69 \pm 0.15$ times ABBHHM's at 2.8 and 4.7 GeV ([Ballam et al.](https://inspirehep.net/literature/67165), Table VII). Its cross sections of the channels without strange particles agree with DESY's ([Struczinski et al. 1976](https://inspirehep.net/literature/100089), Table 3) to $0.98 \pm 0.06$ and $1.04 \pm 0.09$ for the three-prong channels, and DESY normalises to the published total cross sections ($\pm 5\text{％}$). So the difference lies in how each chamber found and counted the events with strange particles, not in the normalisation. DESY's events with a visible decay, uncorrected as they are, side with SLAC. The $\Lambda$ measured at 20 GeV speak for ABBHHM. $\Lambda$ come from the fragmentation of the target, at a rate that changes little with energy ([Abe et al. 1984](https://inspirehep.net/literature/190724), Sec. 3). ABBHHM's steps give 5.5 and 4.7 μb of them at 2.8 and 4.7 GeV, against $5.6 \pm 0.2$ μb at 20 GeV, while SLAC's visible decays would need 7.4, 7.8 and 6.8 μb at 2.8, 4.7 and 9.3 GeV. The code therefore takes ABBHHM's mix of reactions and kaon species, and the number of kaons from all three experiments. For each step of M3, an experiment gives a factor on the multi-pion kaons of ABBHHM's step: its events with a visible decay, less the two-body ones, over the visible decays of those kaons. The factors are 1.38 and 1.53 for SLAC, 1.37 and 1.45 for DESY (at least 1.21 and 1.32 with no decay lost), and 1 for ABBHHM. The code takes the middle of the range, 1.19 and 1.27, or, where that is lower, the least that DESY allows: 1.21 and 1.32 (`multibody_strangeness.py`). Up to 5.8 GeV, its kaons then give $25\text{％}$ more visible decays than ABBHHM found and $9\text{％}$ fewer than the SLAC cross sections, and from 2 to 5.8 GeV $8\text{％}$ fewer than DESY found. They come with 6.9, 6.1 and 6.3 μb of $\Lambda$ at 2.8, 4.7 and 9.3 GeV. If ABBHHM is right, the code makes about a quarter too many kaons at $2\text{–}15$ GeV. If SLAC and DESY are right, its visible decays are still $4\text{–}18\text{％}$ below SLAC's at $2.8\text{–}9.3$ GeV.
    - *The two steps of M4 ($5\text{–}15$ and $15\text{–}50$ GeV), from the measurement at 20 GeV* (`m4_steps.py`). The SLAC hybrid facility measured the strange particles of 284,000 hadronic events at 20 GeV ([Abe et al. 1985](https://inspirehep.net/literature/213727); HEPData [ins213727](https://www.hepdata.net/record/ins213727)). The values are in `tests/support/measured/strange_20gev.py`, and `m4_steps.py` checks them against the HEPData records of both papers: they match value for value. Out of the total 115 μb there are $9.663 \pm 0.272$ μb of $\mathrm{K}^0_\mathrm{S}$, $5.603 \pm 0.180$ μb of $\Lambda$ (with those from $\Sigma^0 \to \Lambda\gamma$) and $1.65 \pm 0.44$ μb of $\Sigma^0$, all corrected for the decays that are not seen. The kaons balance the strangeness of these, of the $\bar\Lambda$ and of the $\Xi$. The charged $\Sigma$ are not measured and are taken as $\Sigma^+ + \Sigma^- = 2\Sigma^0$. The preprint of the paper (SLAC-PUB-3634, Table 2) tells which of the kaons are $\mathrm{K}^0$, through the pairs of neutral strange particles in one event. The kaon that comes with a $\Lambda$ is a $\mathrm{K}^0$ in $0.40 \pm 0.02$ of the events (ABBHHM: 0.48). With ABBHHM's shares for the kaons of the charged $\Sigma$ and of the $\mathrm{K}\ \bar{\mathrm{K}}$ pairs, $\mathrm{K}^+, \mathrm{K}^0, \mathrm{K}^-, \bar{\mathrm{K}}^0$ $= 14.5, 10.9, 8.5, 8.5$ μb. That is 1.50 kaons per antikaon (Abe et al. measure $\mathrm{K}^{\ast +}(890)/\mathrm{K}^{\ast -}(890)$ $= 1.72 \pm 0.31$). These kaons give 0.95 μb of $\mathrm{K}^0_\mathrm{S}\ \mathrm{K}^0_\mathrm{S}$ pairs, against $0.973 \pm 0.040$ μb measured. The error of the $\Sigma^0$, $\Sigma^+ + \Sigma^- = 1.5\Sigma^0$ instead, or 0.1 extra $\mathrm{K}\ \bar{\mathrm{K}}$ pair per $\Lambda$ event change a species by up to $5\text{％}$. ABBHHM's shares of the kaons in the $\mathrm{K}\ \bar{\mathrm{K}}$ pairs, varied within their range, change it by up to $20\text{％}$ one at a time and $31\text{％}$ together. Each species is taken as linear in $\ln\epsilon_r$ between the code's kaons at $2.5\text{–}5$ GeV (7.7, 5.4, 2.6 and 2.4 μb, with the two-body ones) and those at 20 GeV. A step has the value at its middle, its mean for a photon field $\propto 1/\epsilon$. The two steps then have 6.9 and 10.7 μb of $\mathrm{K}^0_\mathrm{S}$. At 20 GeV the code is thus $11\text{％}$ above the measurement, on which the line lies (why two steps: [One step or two for M4](#why-m4-has-two-steps), below). Three checks test the line. First, it reaches the kaons, the antikaons and the $\mathrm{K}^0_\mathrm{S}$ of M5 ($50\text{–}500$ GeV), which are set independently, within $6\text{–}11\text{％}$. The single species agree within $18\text{％}$, as the line keeps the excess of $\mathrm{K}^+$ over $\mathrm{K}^0$ rising. Second, the code's $\Lambda$ at $2.5\text{–}5$ GeV, 6.3 μb, are $12\text{％}$ more than the $5.6 \pm 0.2$ μb measured at 20 GeV, where the fragmentation of the target should make about as many (Abe et al. 1984, Sec. 3). Third, $\mathrm{K}^0_\mathrm{S}$ production in $\pi^-\mathrm{p}$ collisions rises in the same way with the available energy $E_A = \sqrt{s} - m_a - m_b$. Abe et al. give $0.043 \pm 0.003$ $\mathrm{K}^0_\mathrm{S}$ per inelastic interaction at $E_A = 2.41$ GeV, and $0.091 \pm 0.004$ and $0.075 \pm 0.005$ at 4.31 and 4.89 GeV: a ratio of $0.52 \pm 0.04$. With the measured total cross sections, the line gives 0.56 between photons of 5.5 GeV and 16 GeV, which have $E_A = 2.41$ GeV and the mean of the other two.
    - *The energy of the kaons of M4.* A kaon's share of the nucleon energy is $\chi = (E^\ast - p^\ast_L)/\sqrt{s}$ in the centre-of-momentum frame ($p^\ast_L$ along the photon). The distributions of the $\mathrm{K}^0_\mathrm{S}$ in $x = 2p^\ast_L/\sqrt{s}$ and $p_T^2$ measured at 20 GeV ([Abe et al. 1984](https://inspirehep.net/literature/190724), Tables III and VI) give $\langle\chi\rangle = 0.112$. Those in $z = E_K/E_\gamma$ and $p_T^2$ (Tables V and VI) give 0.116. A tenth of the $\mathrm{K}^0_\mathrm{S}$ go towards the nucleon ($x \lt -0.2$) with $\langle\chi\rangle = 0.36$, and the others have $\langle\chi\rangle = 0.085$. The leading part of the code ($9\text{–}15\text{％}$ of the kaons at $\chi = 0.35$) corresponds to the former. For the others, 1.6 times the $\chi$ of the type's low-energy pions (0.112) is a third too much at 20 GeV. With the invariant distribution unchanged (Feynman scaling), $\langle\chi\rangle$ is 0.156 at the middle of the lower step and 0.100 at that of the upper one. The factors 1.8 and 0.93 give 0.154 and 0.100. All kaons of an interaction then take 0.0395 and 0.0393 of the nucleon energy. This rests on the measurement at 20 GeV: 42.3 μb of kaons, 0.353 per interaction of the type's 120 μb, $\times 0.112 = 0.0395$. The higher types keep the factor 1.6. The same scaling gives $\langle\chi\rangle = 0.060, 0.038$ and 0.022 at the middle of M5, M6 and M7, where the code has 0.059, 0.031 and 0.020. The measured distributions of 1984 fall short of the inclusive $\mathrm{K}^0_\mathrm{S}$ cross section of their sample by $2.6\text{％}$ (in $p_T^2$) and $0.6\text{％}$ (in $z$) (`m4_steps.py`).
    - *The code's steps* (`multibody_strangeness.py`, `m4_steps.py`): M3 has 5.8 μb and 12.4 μb of events with strangeness (0.8 μb and 5.0 μb with a $\mathrm{K}\ \bar{\mathrm{K}}$ pair), 1.21 and 1.32 times the middle of the ranges of ABBHHM's data. M4 has 19.5 μb and 27.6 μb, 11.1 μb and 19.1 μb of them with a $\mathrm{K}\ \bar{\mathrm{K}}$ pair. With the other types' factor 1.6 for the low energy fraction in both steps of M4, the $\mathrm{K}^0_\mathrm{S}$ of $15\text{–}50$ GeV would carry $41\text{％}$ too much energy. Abe et al. (1984) find about $20\text{％}$ more strange particles per event in $\gamma\mathrm{p}$ collisions at 20 GeV than in $\pi^\pm\mathrm{p}$ collisions at 18.5 GeV/c. This supports the $\mathrm{K}/\pi$ ratio of M5–M7.

They were checked against the preprints of Abe et al. ([SLAC-PUB-3162](https://inspirehep.net/literature/190724) and [SLAC-PUB-3634](https://inspirehep.net/literature/213727)) and their HEPData records.   - $\boldsymbol{\mathrm{pp}}$ **collisions** (`derivations/kaon_production.py`):
    - *AM³'s pions:* AM³'s code splits $F_\pi$ and the $\delta$ approximation in the pion energy. Pions above $K_\pi E_\mathrm{trans}$ come from $F_\pi$ with the protons above $E_\mathrm{trans}/5$, and those below from the $\delta$ approximation. Here $K_\pi$ is the fraction of $T_p$ the $\pi^0$ take, at the second grid energy above $E_\mathrm{trans}$. Its inelastic cross section for the charged pions, whose threshold uses the $\pi^\pm$ mass, is $0.06\text{–}0.27\text{％}$ below Kafexhiu et al.'s from 6.7 GeV up.
    - *From 100 GeV to $10^6$ GeV:* [Koers et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ph/0611219) fitted $10^3\text{–}10^6$ GeV. Over that range their spectra integrate to their own multiplicity fit (their eq. 15) within $12\text{％}$, and within $4\text{％}$ from $10^4$ GeV on. At 158 GeV their spectra give $\langle \mathrm{K}^+\rangle = 0.198$ and $\langle \mathrm{K}^-\rangle = 0.136$ per inelastic collision, against 0.227 and 0.130 measured by NA49 ([Anticic et al. 2010](https://arxiv.org/abs/1004.1889)). They deviate by up to $13\text{％}$ from the $\mathrm{K}^\pm$ multiplicities of NA49's compilation (Table 11). These multiplicities are NA49's measurement at $\sqrt{s} = 17.2$ GeV and its scaling of that to $\sqrt{s} = 23\text{–}63$ GeV with ISR ratios at $x_F = 0$. They also include NA49's extrapolation of the SPS and ISR data to 200 GeV. Koers et al.'s spectra deviate by up to $20\text{％}$ from the compilation's $\mathrm{K}^0_\mathrm{S}$ multiplicities ($\sqrt{s} = 14\text{–}546$ GeV). The two highest, UA5's at 200 and 546 GeV, come from $\bar{\mathrm{p}}\mathrm{p}$ collisions.
    - *Above $10^6$ GeV:* [Koers et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ph/0611219) consider that their fit "can be applied with some confidence" above $10^6$ GeV (their Sec. VII). Yet its parameters drift, so that the energy fraction of its pions reaches 1 at about $2.5 \times 10^9$ GeV (3.5 at $10^{11}$ GeV). Within its range, however, the $\mathrm{K}^+/\pi^0$ and $\mathrm{K}^0/\pi^0$ ratios at fixed $x \gtrsim 0.03$ are nearly independent of $E_p$. This is Feynman scaling of the ratio. For example, $\mathrm{K}^+/\pi^0 = 0.123\text{–}0.126$ at $x = 0.1$ and $0.26\text{–}0.28$ at $x = 0.9$ from $10^3$ to $10^6$ GeV. The $\mathrm{K}^-/\pi^0$ and $\bar{\mathrm{K}}^0/\pi^0$ ratios still rise by $13\text{–}14\text{％}$ at $x = 0.1$ and fall by $36\text{–}37\text{％}$ at $x = 0.9$ over that range. The energy fraction carried by the kaons is continuous at $E^\ast$, and their multiplicity drops by $1.2\text{–}1.3\text{％}$ (with QGSJET at most $0.2\text{％}$).
    - *Below 100 GeV:* Fesefeldt et al. derived their charged-kaon points from their cross sections for channels with two strange particles. They took the unmeasured $\mathrm{pp} \to \mathrm{K}^+\mathrm{K}^-\mathrm{X}$ equal to $\mathrm{pp} \to \mathrm{K}^0\bar{\mathrm{K}}^0\mathrm{X}$. They obtained the latter from their $\mathrm{K}^0_\mathrm{S}\thinspace\mathrm{K}^0_\mathrm{S}$ events, assuming equal cross sections for the two charge-conjugation states of $\mathrm{K}^0\bar{\mathrm{K}}^0$ (their p. 324). The multiplicity fit leaves out their charged-kaon points. Their $\mathrm{K}^-$ points lie $30\text{–}45\text{％}$ above it, and their $\mathrm{K}^+$ points $6\text{–}8\text{％}$. It also leaves out the two $\mathrm{K}^+$ points at $\sqrt{s} = 2.9$ GeV, which lie 7.4 and 7.7 times above the fitted form. It reproduces the remaining points to $8\text{％}$ ($\mathrm{K}^+$), $1\text{％}$ ($\mathrm{K}^-$) and $19\text{％}$ ($\mathrm{K}^0_\mathrm{S}$) rms in $\ln n$. The normalisation to Koers et al. at 100 GeV scales the fitted $\mathrm{K}^+$ multiplicity by 0.81 ($\mathrm{K}^-$ by 1.03, $\mathrm{K}^0_\mathrm{S}$ by 1.02). Below 100 GeV a $\mathrm{pp}$ collision makes fewer than 0.2 kaons of each kind, and below 10 GeV fewer than 0.03. Koers et al.'s spectra agree with the measured multiplicities within $13\text{％}$ at $\sqrt{s} = 17$ GeV ($\mathrm{K}^\pm$, 158 GeV).
    - *Energy:* from $E_p = 100$ GeV to $10^{12}$ GeV, all kaons together carry $4.5\text{–}6.2\text{％}$ of the proton energy ($5.2\text{％}$ in [Koers et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ph/0611219)'s Table I). That is $13\text{–}16\text{％}$ of the energy AM³'s pions carry. Both ranges hold for AM³'s default SIBYLL pion distribution. With QGSJET, which the extension above $10^6$ GeV then follows, they are $3.9\text{–}6.2\text{％}$ and $10\text{–}16\text{％}$. Above $10^{12}$ GeV the fraction keeps falling slowly, at the same share of the pion energy. It reaches $4.4\text{％}$ (SIBYLL) and $3.4\text{％}$ (QGSJET) at $4 \times 10^{13}$ GeV, the top of the default hadron grid. Below 100 GeV it falls faster ($3\text{％}$ at 50 GeV, $0.8\text{％}$ at 10 GeV).
  - **The code's kernels.** On AM³'s logarithmic grids $f_c$ depends only on the sum of the nucleon and photon bin indices. The rates are therefore Hankel products, computed together with AM³'s 51 pion channels. The kaons of nucleon bin $i$ are put at $\chi_{c,N}^K E_N$ and split between the two neighbouring bins with AM³'s weights (`number_split`, as AM³ does for its pions). The $\mathrm{pp}$ kaons are treated like AM³'s pions, but split in the proton energy. From protons above $E_t = 100$ GeV, the source is a matrix–vector product with the kernel $K{[i, j]}$ $= F_K(E_i/E_j, E_j)(E_i/E_j) c \sigma_\mathrm{inel}(E_j)$ on the hadron grid. The factor $E_i/E_j$ appears because AM³'s densities are per $\ln E$, $E\thinspace dN/dE$. Here $\kappa_K$ is the sum of $x F_K \Delta x$ over the same grid, so the kaon energy the protons lose is the energy in the kaon source. Kaons below the lowest hadron bin, 1 GeV, are neither made nor charged to the protons. For protons below 100 GeV, the $\tilde n_K$ kaons of each proton bin are shared between the two hadron bins around $E_{K}$, keeping their number and energy. Here too, kaons below 1 GeV, from protons below about 7 GeV, are neither made nor charged.

<a id="why-m4-has-two-steps"></a>
**One step or two for M4.** One step with the values measured at 20 GeV for the whole of M4 would reproduce that measurement exactly. But one measurement fixes the yields at one photon energy, not how they change over the factor of ten in photon energy that M4 spans. One step would keep the yields flat from 5 to 50 GeV, while all the other data have them rise. The bubble chambers find fewer strange particles up to 5.8 GeV and at 9.3 GeV. In $\pi^-\mathrm{p}$ collisions, $\mathrm{K}^0_\mathrm{S}$ production doubles between the available energies of 5.5 GeV and 16 GeV photons. M5, from 50 GeV on, has $1.5\text{–}2$ times the yields of that single step. The two steps keep the measurement, since the line through the kaons at $2.5\text{–}5$ GeV and those at 20 GeV passes through it. They also add the rise.

One step with the values measured at 20 GeV for the whole of M4 would have 0.121, 0.091, 0.070 and 0.070 kaons per interaction ($\mathrm{K}^+, \mathrm{K}^0, \mathrm{K}^-, \bar{\mathrm{K}}^0$). The low part would be at 1.1 times the $\chi$ of the low-energy pions. Against the data (`multibody_strangeness.py` and `m4_steps.py` compute both choices):

| | Two steps (the code) | One step with the values at 20 GeV |
|---|---|---|
| $\mathrm{K}^0_\mathrm{S}$ at 20 GeV (measured: 9.66 μb) | 10.7 μb ($+11\text{％}$) | 9.66 μb (exact) |
| ABBHHM up to 5.8 GeV: events with a $\mathrm{K}\ \bar{\mathrm{K}}$ pair | $+44 \ldots {+73}\text{％}$ | $+66 \ldots {+100}\text{％}$ |
| ABBHHM up to 5.8 GeV: $\mathrm{K}^+, \mathrm{K}^0, \mathrm{K}^-, \bar{\mathrm{K}}^0$ | $+33, +37, +32, +60\text{％}$ | $+41, +46, +52, +85\text{％}$ |
| SLAC: events with a visible strange-particle decay at 9.3 GeV | $-4\text{％}$ | $+16\text{％}$ |

The cost of the two steps is the $11\text{％}$ at 20 GeV, as the upper step stands for $15\text{–}50$ GeV. For a broad target photon spectrum the choice matters little. At the end of the 300 steps of `leptohadronic_all`, a build with the single step gives kaons of a species that differ by $-0.6 \ldots {+1.3}\text{％}$. It also gives up to $5\text{％}$ more neutrinos of a flavour ($8\text{％}$ of the $\bar\nu_\mu$ alone) in single bins near $10^{18}$ eV. It changes the total energies of the photons, $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ and neutrinos by at most $7 \times 10^{-5}$ (during the run by up to $1.3\text{％}$, $4.9\text{％}$, $9\text{％}$ and $1.7 \times 10^{-4}$). Photons in a narrow line at $\epsilon_r = 5\text{–}15$ GeV would make $1.3\text{–}1.5$ times as many kaons with the single step.

### Kaon decays

- **Matrix elements** (`derivations/kaon_decays.py`): $W_0 \pm W_\mathrm{pol}$ is the V−A trace with explicit Dirac matrices, exactly. $W_0$ is the [Chounet–Gaillard–Gaillard](https://doi.org/10.1016/0370-1573%2872%2990018-X) density (their eq. 6.9, checked against the paper) over the whole Dalitz plot, $W_0 = 4M_K\thinspace\rho$. The module also proves the reduction of $F_{R,L}$ to [Lipari et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/0704.0718)'s two-body formulas.
- **Energy** (the harness of `tests/unit/test_kaon_decays.py`, which checks it to $10^{-11}$): the products of the decays carry the kaon's energy.

**The code's decay kernels** (`kaon_decay_tables` in `src/am3_mojo/physics/decays.mojo`) are computed when the kernels are initialised, on whatever grid is set:
- **Quadrature in the kaon frame.** The Dalitz-line integrals use a 24-point Gauss–Legendre rule, which is exact because the densities are low-order polynomials along the line. The distribution in $E^\ast$ uses 256 Gauss–Legendre nodes after the substitution $E^\ast = m + (E_{\max} - m)\sin^2(\pi u/2)$. This substitution absorbs the square-root edges of $g$ at both ends: at $E^\ast = m$ for a massive product, and at $E_{\max}$ when both other products are massive. A three-body product is thus a sum of 256 two-body lab spectra: flat ones with weights $w_g = g\thinspace w$. For a muon of given helicity, they are flat ones with $w_g = g\thinspace w/2$ plus the helicity term $\tilde h$ with weight $w_G = \pm G\thinspace w/2$.
- **Exact binning.** Between two grid points $x_{k+1} \lt x \lt x_k$ ($x_k = e^{-k\Delta}$), the number and energy of one node's products are closed-form integrals over $x \in [x_a, x_b]$ (the overlap with the node's range):

  $$N = \frac{M}{2p^\ast}\left[w_g\thinspace (x_b - x_a) + w_G\left(\frac{E^\ast}{p^\ast}(x_b - x_a) - \frac{m^2}{p^\ast M}\ln\frac{x_b}{x_a}\right)\right],$$

  $$E = \frac{M}{2p^\ast}\left[w_g\thinspace \frac{x_b^2 - x_a^2}{2} + w_G\left(\frac{E^\ast}{p^\ast}\thinspace \frac{x_b^2 - x_a^2}{2} - \frac{m^2}{p^\ast M}(x_b - x_a)\right)\right].$$

  The products between $x_{k+1}$ and $x_k$ are shared between these two grid points linearly in $x$, which keeps both their number and their energy. The point $x_k$ receives $\left(E - x_{k+1}N\right)/(x_k - x_{k+1})$, and $x_{k+1}$ receives $\left(x_k N - E\right)/(x_k - x_{k+1})$.
- **Per step.** On a logarithmic grid the kernel depends only on the difference of the product and kaon bin indices. Each (species, product) pair is therefore a Toeplitz matrix, stored by its diagonal values. The 13 product sources are computed in parallel as SIMD Toeplitz products.
