# Differences from the C++ AM³

This file describes how am3_mojo differs from the C++ AM³ v1.2.0. The folder also holds the [comparison with the C++ code](../../README.md#reference-comparison): `setup_reference.sh` builds AM³ v1.2.0 and a copy with the fixes and [constants](#constants) of `am3_fixes.patch`. Each test file's docstring says what it checks.

## Bugs of AM³ v1.2.0 that am3_mojo fixes

Each fix has a regression test in `tests/unit/test_fixes.py`, unless its entry below names another file. The docstrings of these tests give the size of each bug. The patched C++ build has the same fixes, so that both codes solve the same equations. It leaves out only the fixes of the interface and the fix of the OpenMP race. The interface fixes are those of the call and argument checks, the getters, the settings changed after `init_kernels()` and the profiling flag. The race fix is left out because the reference builds are serial. The solver bugs change AM³'s results in common set-ups.

The solver solves each step of its semi-analytic regimes (3: cooling and escape, 4: cooling only) with the Green's function of the cooling equation. Its regime 2 (cooling by less than an e-fold per step) uses Crank–Nicolson steps with the weights of [Chang & Cooper](https://doi.org/10.1016/0021-9991%2870%2990001-X) (1970, J. Comput. Phys. 6, 1, eq. 18). Without diffusion, these weights take the flux between two cells from the upper cell. The solver has these bugs:
- Sign of the Green's function: The default source integral is `simple_integral_q`. It weights the injection with $\exp(+\int \alpha/\vert A\vert\thinspace dx)$ instead of the decaying $\exp(-\int \alpha/\vert A\vert\thinspace dx)$ ($\alpha$: escape and decay rate, $A$: cooling rate, $x = \ln E$). The RK4 variant (`set_solver_simpleqintegration(True)`) has the right sign. With the wrong sign, the cascade diverges at large time steps.
- Injection dropped: Where $\alpha/\vert A\vert \lt e^{-300}$, the same integral skips the injection along with the escape. The solver adds $10^{-150}$ to $\alpha$, so this can happen without escape or decay. The injection is then dropped in the $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ and photon solvers with cooling within about 1.4 ms, and in the hadron solvers with any cooling.
- Quadrature of the source integral: The solver uses regime 3 where two conditions hold. Cooling moves particles by more than an e-fold within a step, and escape is between 1% and 100 times the cooling rate. The source integral there is $\int_x^y q(x') \exp(-\int_x^{x'} \alpha/\vert A\vert\ dx'')$ $dx'/\vert A(x)\vert$, with $y$ the energy from which particles reach $x$ within the step and $q$ the injection rate.
  - AM³'s quadrature: AM³ sums $q(x') \exp(-\int_x^{x'+\Delta} \alpha/\vert A\vert)\ \Delta$ over the grid points $x'$ below $y$. The attenuation of each point includes the cell above it ($x' = x$ too). The rule is first order, and it leaves out the cell up to $y$.
  - Escape-dominated cells ($z = \Delta\thinspace \alpha/\vert A\vert \gtrsim 1$): even with the sign fixed, the densities come out about $z/(e^z - 1)$ times too low. Here $\Delta = 0.1$ is the step of the grid in $\ln E$.
  - Cooling-dominated cells: the densities are too high for falling source spectra and too low for rising ones, more so for steeper sources. This also happens at time steps of the escape time or shorter, wherever regime 3 is used.
  - am3_mojo integrates every cell exactly for the solver's splines (`simple_integral_q` in `src/am3_mojo/solver/solver.mojo`).
- No cooling in a window above much slower cooling: AM³ maps each bin of a regime-3/4 window to its energy when the step starts. The map uses the cooling time summed from the lowest of the 10 cells that pad the window below. Where those cells cool about $10^{15}$ times more slowly, or not at all, this sum swamps the window's cooling times in double precision. The window's cooling is then distorted or lost. am3_mojo gives the cells below a window the cooling rate of the window's first bin.
- Sign of the cooled particles in regime 4: The particles that cool into bin $x$ from $y$ are $n(y)\thinspace A(y)/A(x)$, but AM³ adds $-n(y)\thinspace A(y)/A(x)$. Here $n$ is the density. The sign error matters where regime 4 has a finite $y$, for example with fast energy-independent cooling. The error leaves the particles too little energy in the steady state.
- Escape above a cooling-only window: A window of regime 4 neglects escape, and AM³ solves the cells that pad it above without escape too. Where these cells hold bins in which escape is not negligible, the particles that cool into the window from there arrive unattenuated. In `test_cooling_only_window_below_escape_dominated_bins`, below a band in which escape becomes 1000 times faster than cooling, the electrons then come out 1.6 times their steady state. am3_mojo solves such a window as regime 3, with the escape of its padding cells and its own. It does so wherever escape in the padding is not negligible, whether its cells hold particles or not.
- Escape on the way down: Regime 3 attenuates the particles that cool from $y$ down to $x$ within the step by $\exp(-\int_x^y \alpha/\vert A\vert\thinspace dx)$. AM³ integrates this exponent with 5 RK4 steps from $x$ to $y$. The points of these steps fall differently across a jump of the escape. In `test_escape_on_the_way_into_a_cooling_only_window`, the escape jumps to as fast as the cooling. Below the jump, the electrons come out 0.88 to 1.04 times the exact density. The pattern repeats every 4 bins, the length of a step. am3_mojo integrates the exponent exactly for the solver's $\ln(\alpha/\vert A\vert)$, which is linear between the bins. It accumulates the exponent from the window's first bin. If the accumulation starts at a lower bin, fast escape in the cells below leaves no digits for the window's own escape (`test_escape_exponent_of_a_window_above_fast_escape`).
- Endless loop in the RK4 integral: If the RK4 variant widens its integration range again after narrowing it, the loop does not update its step. The loop then never ends.
- Range of the RK4 integral: Where the sink is weak, the RK4 variant integrates the injection up to $0.99\ x_\mathrm{max}$ instead of up to $y$. This limit is just below the top of the solver's window. The variant thus counts particles injected too far up to reach $x$ within the step.
- Last step of the RK4 integral: The RK4 variant sums its steps, and the sum can round past the top of its range. Where that range ends at the top of the solver's window, AM³ then raises an exception. The species is then emptied for the step. am3_mojo ends the last step at the top of the range (`test_spline_and_rk4_guards`).
- Sink of the RK4 integral: The RK4 variant leaves out the sink where $(y - x)\thinspace\alpha/\vert A\vert \lt 10^{-3}$, with $\alpha/\vert A\vert$ taken at $x$ alone. Where the escape rises between $x$ and $y$, as below escape-dominated bins, the variant then counts particles that escape before they reach $x$. In the set-up of `test_cooling_only_window_below_escape_dominated_bins` (with the fix above), the electrons come out 1.57 to 1.59 times their steady state. am3_mojo takes the largest $\alpha/\vert A\vert$ between $x$ and $y$.
- Particles that cool into a window from far above: The solver splits the grid into windows of one regime each. It solves them from the top down. AM³ feeds a window only from the 10 cells above it. It feeds Chang & Cooper windows at the old densities of these cells, and semi-analytic windows within these cells. It does not feed escape-dominated windows (regime 1) at all. Where the window above cools faster, the particles that cool into the window within the step are lost, with or without escape. am3_mojo pads the semi-analytic windows up to the energy from which particles reach them within the step, whether the cells there hold particles or not. It sums the cooling time as the windows do, with the solver's cooling rate log-linear between the bin centers. It adds the particles that cross into the other windows to their top cell (`cells_reached` and `crossing_count` in `src/am3_mojo/solver/solver.mojo`). Where the bins above cool far more slowly, the padding needs the time of the window's top bin itself (`test_particles_cooling_in_across_a_slow_band`). In `test_regime_boundary.py`, am3_mojo then keeps the electrons to $9 \times 10^{-4}$. In `tests/literature_checks/triplet/steady_states.py`, it keeps the triplet pairs to 0.1% ($3 \times 10^{15}$ photons cm⁻³, time step $0.1\ t_\mathrm{esc}$).
- Chang & Cooper densities read by the windows below: The Chang & Cooper steps take the flux between two cells from the upper one. In the steady state, a cell's density times its cooling rate is then the flux through its lower face. AM³'s windows below read these densities as values at the bin centers, and add the injection from there on. They thus count the injection between that face and the center twice. If a band of injection ends at the window's top, the particles below it come out 17% too many in a cooling-only window. In an escape-only window, they come out 15% too many. Under an injection falling as $E^{-2}$ across the boundary, they come out up to 4% too many (`test_cooling_only_window_below_a_chang_cooper_window`). am3_mojo reads the density of the mean of the fluxes through a cell's two faces. This reading makes these steady states exact. A cell reaches that relation only some cell crossings after the source starts. A source switched off before then leaves the window below too low for a few steps. Under an $E^{-2}$ injection, the window is 6% too low with this reading and 1% with AM³'s reading.
- Crank–Nicolson steps at large Courant numbers: The solver uses regime 2 where two conditions hold. Particles cool by less than an e-fold per step, and they escape at most 100 times faster than they cool. This regime thus covers Courant numbers $C = \vert A\vert\thinspace\Delta t/\Delta$ up to 10 and $\alpha\Delta t$ up to 100. Its Crank–Nicolson steps keep the densities from going negative only where $C + \alpha\Delta t \le 2$. [Chang & Cooper](https://doi.org/10.1016/0021-9991%2870%2990001-X) prove non-negative densities only for fully implicit steps (their eqs. 19–21). Above this limit, the densities oscillate from step to step. In `test_chang_cooper_window_at_large_courant_numbers` ($C = 7$, $\alpha\Delta t = 50$), the electrons at $10^{10}$ eV are 1.92, 0.15, 1.79, 0.27, … times their steady state. The exact solution reaches this steady state within the first step. AM³ sets the negative densities to zero. This clipping creates particles. A sharp pulse that flows into the top cell of a Chang & Cooper window (see the fix above) undershoots there in the next step. A clipped pair component also no longer adds up with the others to all pairs. With steps of $10^4$ s, the components then add up only to within $9 \times 10^{-2}$ in one bin (`tests/unit/test_window_top_undershoot.py`). am3_mojo sets the weight $\theta$ of the implicit half of the step in each cell from its rates. The weight is Crank–Nicolson's $\theta = 1/2$ where the cell's diagonal $1 - (C + \alpha\Delta t)/2$ of the explicit half is non-negative. Elsewhere, it is the least implicit $\theta$ for which $1 - (1 - \theta)(C + \alpha\Delta t)$ is non-negative. The flux between two cells takes the $\theta$ of the upper cell, from which the particles cool. The flux uses this weight in both cells, so that the step conserves the particles. The step is then linear in the sources. The cells below a window that its matrix holds then do not change its steps (`test_chang_cooper_steps_linear_in_the_sources`, `test_chang_cooper_window_above_fast_escape`).
- Injection integral of regime 4: AM³ accumulates the integral of the injection from the bottom of the window. It restarts the integral where $q\thinspace dx / \mathrm{sum} \lt 10^{-15}$. The bins just below each restart keep almost no significant digits of their injection. The particles of a bin come from above it, so am3_mojo accumulates the integral from the top of the window.
- Particles in the top bins of a grid: The solver leaves out the top bin of every grid. It also leaves out the empty bins above the highest bin with particles. AM³ makes this empty window at least 6 bins wide. A spectrum that reaches the 6 highest evolved bins thus loses their particles every step. Because the top bin's rates are zero, AM³'s semi-analytic regimes also evolve the highest evolved bin as if it did not cool. In am3_mojo, the empty window ends at the first bin with particles, and the top bin keeps its rates. Of the four scenarios, only `exotic` reaches the top of its grid.
- Cooling time across a cell: A semi-analytic window maps each bin $x$ to the energy $y$ from which its particles cool within the step. To do so, it inverts the cooling time summed over the cells. AM³ sums each cell's time with 10 RK4 steps and inverts the sum linearly in that time. The RK4 steps overestimate the time where the cooling time grows steeply across a cell. Where it grows $e^{25}$ times, the error is 1.2% (`test_cooling_time_across_a_steep_cell`). In the top cell of some windows, the summed RK4 steps round past the window's top. The RK4 sum then leaves out the last step. Where the cooling time grows steeply across a cell, the linear inversion puts $y$ next to $x$. This happens, for example, above a band of fast extra cooling. The bin then keeps its particles and injection without cooling. In `test_fast_cooling_below_a_slow_cell`, the electrons grow without bound. The logarithm of the solver's cooling rate is linear across each cell. am3_mojo sums and inverts the cooling time exactly in each cell for this rate (`compute_y` in `src/am3_mojo/solver/solver.mojo`).
- Windows shorter than 6 bins: A window ends where the regime changes, but only after 6 bins. A shorter escape-only window (regime 1) thus solves up to 5 bins below it without their cooling. A shorter cooling-only window (regime 4) solves them without their escape. In `test_short_cooling_window_above_escape_dominated_bins`, 3 escape-dominated bins below 3 fast-cooling ones gain the injection of every step without escaping. A short window of regime 3 takes in up to 5 bins below it too. Where these bins hardly cool, their cooling times swamp the window's own in the summed cooling time. The window then does not cool. These bins also do not keep the particles that cool out of the window into them. In `test_cooling_window_above_bins_that_do_not_cool`, electrons cool by inverse Compton on photons at 10 MeV in no field. Their lowest bin with cooling then does not cool, and 8% of the electrons are lost. A short Chang & Cooper window (regime 2) solves up to 5 bins below it with its matrix, at their Courant numbers. In `test_chang_cooper_window_above_fast_cooling_bins`, 3 bins below the window that cool by 20 e-folds per step then hold 0.38 times the exact density after one step. The window starts at the highest bin with particles. A source $10^{60}$ times weaker above it thus moves the window, and changes those 3 bins by 160%. In am3_mojo, every window ends where the regime changes.

The simulation has these bugs:
- Quantum-synchrotron cooling of heavy particles: With `set_process_quantum_syn_cooling(True)`, AM³ multiplies the synchrotron cooling of every charged species by $1/(1 + \sqrt{2}\thinspace \chi^{2/3})^2$. Here $\chi$ is the quantum parameter, defined below. This factor interpolates between the classical limit and the $\chi^{-4/3}$ quantum limit of Landau & Lifshitz ([Berestetskii, Lifshitz & Pitaevskii](https://doi.org/10.1016/C2009-0-24486-2), §90, eq. 90.27). The section Kept as in AM³ discusses the accuracy of this factor. For a particle of mass $m$ and charge $e$ in the field $B$, the quantum parameter is $\chi = \gamma B/B_\mathrm{cr}$ with $B_\mathrm{cr} = m^2c^3/(e\hbar) \propto m^2$. AM³, however, computes it as $\gamma B/m$ for all species. This value is $m/m_e$ times too large for protons, pions and muons (and am3_mojo's kaons). It thus overstates their quantum suppression. The pion and muon cooling breaks of the neutrino spectra in strong fields are therefore wrong. The $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ are unaffected. am3_mojo scales $\chi$ by $m_e/m$.
- Series of the inverse-Compton cooling functions: Below $z = 0.1$, AM³ evaluates the cooling functions $f_1(z)$ and $f_2(z)$ by Taylor series ([Jones 1965](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.137.B1306), eqs. 14–15). See also Numerics below. AM³ keeps only the first three terms of Jones' series (his Table I). These three terms are too large near $z = 0.1$. At that point, AM³ switches to the closed forms. The inverse-Compton cooling of every species is therefore too fast on photons just below $\varepsilon\bar\gamma = 0.1$. At $\varepsilon\bar\gamma = 0.1$, the cooling jumps back. Here $\varepsilon$ is in units of the particle's rest energy, and $\bar\gamma$ is defined under Numerics. am3_mojo uses Jones' series to $z^{10}$ (with exact coefficients).
- Dilogarithm of the inverse-Compton cooling functions: From $z = 0.1$ on, the cooling functions $f_1(z)$ and $f_2(z)$ hold the dilogarithm $\mathrm{Li}_ 2(-2z)$. AM³'s solver integrates the dilogarithm with 20 RK4 steps from its value at $-0.1$ (given to 9 digits) down to $-20$. Below that point, it takes an asymptotic series. The integrated value is $3 \times 10^{-4}$ off at $-20$. As a result, $f_1$ and $f_2$ are up to $4.6 \times 10^{-5}$ and $1.9 \times 10^{-5}$ off just below $z = 10$, where they jump (`test_inverse_compton_cooling_functions_with_the_dilogarithm`). am3_mojo takes the dilogarithm to rounding (`polylog` in `src/am3_mojo/utils/specfun.mojo`, as for the photon loss below).
- Dilogarithm of the inverse-Compton photon loss: A photon of energy $\varepsilon$ (in units of $m_e c^2$) scatters off $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ of Lorentz factor $\gamma$. The scattering has the Klein–Nishina cross section averaged over the angles. The closed form of this cross section holds the dilogarithm $\mathrm{Li}_ 2(-2L)$, with $L = 2\gamma\varepsilon$. AM³'s `polylog` in `math.cc` sums 49 terms of the power series of $\mathrm{Li}_ 2$ for $L \lt 0.5$. It takes 99 rectangles of the integral for $0.5 \lt L \lt 5$ and $-\ln^2(2L)/2 - 1.6$ above. It is up to 1% off at the grid's values of $L$ and jumps by 2.2% at $L = 5$. At $L = 0.5$, it gives −1.6 for $\mathrm{Li}_ 2(-1) = -\pi^2/12$. The photons' loss rates are thus up to 1.8% too large at $L = 1.1$ and 0.9% too small at $L = 5.4$. am3_mojo evaluates $\mathrm{Li}_ 2$ to rounding (`polylog` in `src/am3_mojo/utils/specfun.mojo`).
- Integral of $K_{5/3}$ in the quantum-synchrotron kernel: The $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ emission of quantum synchrotron radiation holds an integral from $x$ to infinity. Its integrand is the modified Bessel function $K_{5/3}(t)$. AM³'s `int_bessel_k53` (`math.cc`) sums 100 rectangles in $\ln t$ from $x$ to 10. This sum is 1.9–3.1% too large. Above $x = 10$, it takes $(1.253314 + 0.139338/x)\thinspace e^{-x}/\sqrt{x}$. This form is 5.4% too small just above 10, where AM³'s integral jumps. The $1/x$ term of the asymptotic series has the coefficient $\sqrt{\pi/2}\thinspace 55/72 = 0.957$. Below $x = 10^{-3}$, AM³ uses a series with rounded coefficients and without an $x^{4/3}$ term. In the classical limit, AM³'s emission thus carries 1.019 times the energy that the cooling takes (`test_quantum_synchrotron_emission_takes_the_energy_of_the_cooling`). am3_mojo takes the integral to $3 \times 10^{-14}$ (`test_integral_of_k53`). Above $x = 10^{-3}$, it applies the trapezoidal rule to the integral of $e^{-x\cosh u}\cosh(5u/3)/\cosh u$ over $u \ge 0$. This integral form follows from [DLMF 10.32.9](https://dlmf.nist.gov/10.32.E9). The rule converges exponentially here. Below that value, it sums the series with exact coefficients (`int_bk53m` and `int_bk53s` in `src/am3_mojo/utils/specfun.mojo`).
- Muons of pion decays: In a $\pi^\pm$ decay, the muon takes a fraction $x$ of the pion's energy, between $r = (m_\mu/m_\pi)^2$ and 1. The fraction has one distribution for each helicity ([AM³ paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.13371), after [Lipari et al. 2007](https://arxiv.org/abs/0704.0718)). The distribution of the left-handed $\mu_\mathrm{L}^+$ is largest at $x = 1$. AM³'s kernel leaves out this point, so a decay makes too few muons, with too little energy. This deficit also lowers the neutrinos and $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ of muon decays. am3_mojo integrates the distributions over the bin of each grid point (`PionDecay.initialize_kernels` in `src/am3_mojo/physics/decays.mojo`), so that a decay makes one muon.
- $\boldsymbol{\mathbf{e}^\pm}$ **of muon decays.** In the V−A decay of a muon, the $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ have the spectrum of the $\nu_\mu$-type neutrino. This spectrum depends on the muon's helicity. For the $\mathrm{e}^\pm$, the [AM³ paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.13371) and code take $\tfrac{4}{3}(1 - x^3)$ for every muon ("omitting chiralities"). This form is the spectrum of one helicity. Most of the muons of pion decays are $\mu_\mathrm{L}^+$ and $\mu_\mathrm{R}^-$. Their $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ therefore take 0.40 of the muon's energy instead of 0.30. am3_mojo gives each helicity its spectrum (`MuonDecay.Calc_MUtoE` in `src/am3_mojo/physics/decays.mojo`).
- Photons of $\pi^0$ decays: The two photons of a $\pi^0$ decay are spread evenly in energy, from 0 to the pion's energy $E$. For the pions of grid point $j$, AM³ takes $E = (E_j + E_{j+1})/2$, the mean of two grid energies. It fills the kernel with the values of this spectrum at the photon grid points. The photons of a decay then carry 1.048 times the pion's energy, and their number is 1.9967. am3_mojo takes $E = E_j$ and gives each photon bin the decays whose photons lie in it. A decay then makes 2 photons. They carry $\cosh(\Delta/2) = 1.00125$ times the pion's energy, with $\Delta = 0.1$ the bin width in $\ln E$. This factor is the mean energy of an even spread over a bin, in units of the energy of its grid point (`test_pi0_decay_photons_carry_the_energy_of_the_pions`).
- Maximum-energy estimates: With `set_estimate_max_energies(True)`, the electron and proton estimates read one bin past the grid when acceleration wins everywhere. If the memory past the grid reads 0, the injection becomes NaN. With positrons merged into electrons, the electron estimate reads the cooling of the electron solver. This solver is then not evolved. The estimates also leave out the extra cooling of `set_t_pair_extra_cooling` and `set_t_proton_extra_cooling`. The cooling of the $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ and protons includes this extra cooling. With an extra cooling time of 1 s above $10^{11}$ eV in 1 G, the $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ estimate stays at $5.0 \times 10^{13}$ eV. But acceleration balances the cooling at $9.1 \times 10^{12}$ eV (`test_estimated_max_energies_with_extra_cooling`). am3_mojo takes the cooling of the solver that evolved the $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ in the step before. After a merged step, this is the merged solver (`test_estimated_electron_emax_when_the_merge_is_switched`). It also takes the extra cooling. If acceleration wins everywhere, it takes the energy one bin above the grid's top.
- Positrons merged for a few steps: With `set_process_merge_positrons_into_electrons(True)`, AM³ evolves only the summed pairs. It keeps the $\mathrm{e}^-$ and $\mathrm{e}^+$ of the last step before the merge. When the merge is switched off again, the next step evolves these kept particles and sums them into the pairs. The pairs thus lose what the merged steps changed. In `test_positrons_merged_for_a_few_steps`, they lose 61%. During the merge, `get_electrons()` and `get_positrons()` do not change. am3_mojo splits the merged pairs of each bin after every merged step, in the ratio of the bin's $\mathrm{e}^-$ and $\mathrm{e}^+$ before the step. If the bin had none, the pairs count as electrons.
- Photons with `parse_sed` off: The $\mathrm{pp}$ $\pi^0$ photons have a direct treatment by default. With this default and `set_process_parse_sed(False)`, AM³ drops the photons of these $\pi^0$ from the total photon spectrum.
- Decay products of injected particles with `parse_sed` on (the default): AM³ then computes two totals as the sums of their photo-pion and $\mathrm{pp}$ components. These totals are the $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ of muon decays and the photons of $\pi^0$ decays. The components leave out the decay products of injected muons, charged pions and $\pi^0$. am3_mojo computes the totals from all particles, and the components only split them.
- External injection of muons and neutrinos: AM³ evolves an injection set with `set_injection_rate_mu_plus_left` as $\mu_\mathrm{R}^-$. It evolves one set with `set_injection_rate_mu_minus_right` as $\mu_\mathrm{L}^+$. It adds the injection of `set_injection_rate_electron_neutrinos` to all four neutrino species. It ignores the injections of the other three neutrino setters. am3_mojo injects each into its own species.
- Photons on the hadron grid after `update_energy_grid`: `update_energy_grid` computes the shift between the photon and hadron grids. For most lowest photon energies $E_{\gamma,\mathrm{min}}$, every power of ten among them, this shift is one bin too large. The constructor, and so the default grid, has the correct shift. With the wrong shift, the photo-hadronic processes see every photon at $e^{-0.1}$ of its energy. This error slows photo-pion production most near threshold. The photons that hadrons emit also land one bin too high. am3_mojo computes the shift as the constructor does.
- Grid points and optimization limits: For an energy from a grid's first point on, `eV_to_…_grid_index` gives the grid point at or below it. AM³ gives some grid points' own energies the point below (14 of the 340 lepton points of the default grid). A power-law injection whose minimum energy is such a point's then starts one bin lower. `update_energy_grid` computes the grids from its three energies in the same way. For some grid points' energies, a grid then ends two bins below the maximum energy, or starts one bin above its minimum energy. AM³ also rounds the indices of the optimization limits up to even numbers. A limit then lies one bin (10.5%) higher on photon grids with an odd number of bins below $m_e c^2$. `update_energy_grid` computes the limits of the new grid from those of the old grid. So each call that changes this number from even to odd, or back, moves the limits up one bin. A limit that a grid cuts also stays cut on a later, larger grid. am3_mojo gives a grid point's energy its own index, and a limit the grid point at or below it. It keeps the energies that the setters received, and `update_energy_grid` computes the limits from them (`test_grid_index_of_a_grid_point`, `test_grids_from_the_energies_of_grid_points`, `test_optimisation_limits_on_another_grid`, `test_optimisation_limits_that_a_grid_cut`).
- Protons of the $\mathrm{pp}$ $\delta$ approximation: Below the transition energy of this approximation, a pion of energy $E_\pi$ comes from the protons at $E_\pi/K_\pi + m_p$. Here $K_\pi$ is the pion multiplicity at the transition, and $m_p$ is the proton mass. AM³ reads the protons there from the two grid points above that energy, not the two around it. It thus extrapolates downward. It also takes $K_\pi$ at the second grid point above the transition, not the first. Pions below about 10 GeV, and their muons and neutrinos, then come out too low. They are up to 1% too low for protons whose number per unit energy falls as $E^{-2}$, and up to 3% for $E^{-3}$. Here $E$ is the proton energy. Just below a sharp lower edge of the protons, the extrapolated density is negative. am3_mojo interpolates between the grid points around each energy.
- Transition energy of the $\mathrm{pp}$ $\delta$ approximation at a grid point: AM³ compares each energy of the hadron grid with the transition energy exactly. A transition energy set to a grid point's energy can differ from it in the last bit. The point then counts as below the transition, for 52 of 87 grid points from 55 GeV on. The $\mathrm{pp}$ loss rate of the point's bin changes by up to 0.9%. am3_mojo takes a grid point within $10^{-9}$ of the transition energy as lying at it (`test_pp_transition_energy_at_a_grid_point`).
- Photon spectrum of $\mathrm{pp}$ collisions below 100 GeV: [Kafexhiu et al. 2014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.7369) give the $\pi^0$ photon spectrum as a peak value times a shape. Their Table V gives the shape of SIBYLL and of QGSJET for proton kinetic energies above 100 GeV, and that of Geant4 below. AM³ switches the peak value at 100 GeV, but the shape at 50 GeV. Between 50 and 100 GeV, it thus gives the peak of Geant4 the shape of SIBYLL or QGSJET. Protons at 67 GeV then make photons with 1.44 (SIBYLL, the default) and 1.03 (QGSJET) times the energy of Geant4's. am3_mojo switches both at 100 GeV (`test_pp_photons_of_sibyll_and_qgsjet_below_100_gev`).
- Stale decay sources: AM³ does not reset the decay sources of a step when a decay is off. With pion decay switched off during a run, it keeps injecting the muons of the last step with pion decay. With muon decay off, it keeps injecting the $\nu_e$ and $\bar\nu_e$ of the last step with muon decay. With pion decay off and muon decay on, it adds the muon-decay $\nu_\mu$ to the total $\nu_\mu$ sources of all earlier steps. With the hadronic processes switched off and `parse_sed` off, it keeps injecting the $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ of the last step's muon decays. AM³ gives the $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ of muon decays to the photo-pion and $\mathrm{pp}$ pair components only if pion decay is on too. These components thus miss the pairs from the muons that remain after pion decay is switched off. AM³'s solvers also take the decay switches at `init_kernels()`. If a pion or muon decay is switched later, the switch changes only its products. am3_mojo recomputes the sources every step, and its decay sinks follow the switches.
- Cooling switches during a run: AM³'s solvers take the synchrotron, inverse-Compton and quantum-synchrotron cooling switches at `init_kernels()`. If a cooling is switched off later, it keeps cooling the particles, but their emission stops. If a cooling is switched on later, it does not cool them. `set_mag_field` builds the synchrotron kernels only for the emission and synchrotron self-absorption switches that are on at its call. The $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ kernel then stays empty, and those of the hadrons keep an earlier field. The $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ kernel also stays quantum or classical as `set_process_quantum_syn` was at the call. am3_mojo's solvers follow the cooling switches every step. am3_mojo also builds every synchrotron kernel for the field before the next step. Its $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ kernel follows `set_process_quantum_syn`.
- Cooling rates of a step without cooling: A solver computes its synchrotron and inverse-Compton cooling rates only in a step with cooling. With the field at zero and no inverse Compton, AM³'s cooling-time getters and maximum-energy estimates thus read the rates of the last step with cooling. They also read these rates after both $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ cooling switches are turned off during a run (with the fix above). In `test_cooling_rates_of_a_step_without_cooling`, the estimate then puts the maximum electron energy at the bottom of the grid. This low maximum energy ends the electron injection. am3_mojo sets the rates of a step without cooling to zero.
- Loss rates of processes switched off: AM³ computes some loss rates only while their process is on. These are the proton losses of Bethe–Heitler pair production, photo-pion production and $\mathrm{pp}$ collisions, and the photon losses of photo-pion production and inverse Compton. When a process is switched off, AM³ keeps the rates of its last step. AM³'s timescale getters then report these rates. The proton maximum-energy estimate also reads them in the first step after the process is switched on again. In `test_estimated_proton_emax_after_pp_cooling_is_switched_on_again`, the $\mathrm{pp}$ density drops from $10^{13}$ to $10^8$ cm⁻³ while $\mathrm{pp}$ cooling is off. The estimate then reads the loss at $10^{13}$ cm⁻³: $8.9 \times 10^{16}$ eV instead of $1.2 \times 10^{18}$ eV. AM³ also computes the rates of synchrotron self-absorption with the $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ synchrotron off, though these rates act only with the synchrotron on. am3_mojo sets the loss rates of a process that is off to zero, and computes those of synchrotron self-absorption only with the $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ synchrotron on.
- Kernels of the switches at `init_kernels()`: AM³ builds each photo-pion and Bethe–Heitler kernel only with its emission or cooling switch on at `init_kernels()`. The re-injection of the nucleons is part of their photo-pion cooling and uses the emission kernel. If the emission is off at `init_kernels()` and the cooling is on, the nucleons that interact therefore vanish. In `test_photopion_cooling_with_its_emission_off_at_init`, the nucleons then lose 10% of their energy per step instead of 1.8%. Photo-pion cooling switched on later creates nucleons, and Bethe–Heitler cooling switched on later makes pairs without proton losses. The same holds for the $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ inverse-Compton emission and photon-loss kernels, and for the step tables of the hadrons' inverse Compton. If a hadron emission is switched on later, it puts its photons at the energies of their targets. am3_mojo builds these kernels whatever the switches. Because its $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ cooling follows the switches (above), the $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ cool by inverse Compton only with the kernels of `init_kernels()`.
- γγ pairs without their photon loss: The pairs of γγ pair production take their number from the photon loss rates. AM³ computes these rates only with the photon loss (`set_process_annihilation_cooling`) on. If the photon loss is off from the start, AM³ makes no pairs. If the photon loss is switched off during a run, the pairs come from the rates of its last step. The pairs also come from these old rates in the first step after the process itself is switched off and on again. am3_mojo computes the rates for the emission too, and takes them as zero while the process is off, as at `init_kernels()`. am3_mojo builds both kernels whatever the switches.
- Integration limits of the γγ pair kernel: `set_optimize_annihilation_pair_emission(False)` switches off the eight-bin approximation. The pairs of Lorentz factor $\gamma$ then sum the kernel over the energies of two photons. The lower limits of these energies depend on $\gamma$. AM³ computes the lowest energy of the first photon as $\gamma(1 - \beta)/2$, which cancels at large $\gamma$. Here $\beta$ is the lepton's speed in units of $c$. The limit is 74% too large at $\gamma = 8.8 \times 10^7$, and zero from $9.5 \times 10^7$ on, where its logarithm is $-\infty$. For a first photon of energy $x \gt 1$ in units of $m_e c^2$, the lowest energy of the second one is $\gamma - x + (f + 1/f)/2$, with $f = 2x - \gamma(1 + \beta)$. Where $x$ and $\gamma$ are large, this limit cancels to a value of the rounding, zero or below. For photons $n \propto \varepsilon^{-0.6}$, the pairs come out up to 7.2% too few at 45 TeV (`test_gamma_gamma_full_kernel_limits_at_large_lorentz_factors`). am3_mojo takes $\gamma(1 - \beta)$ as $1/(\gamma(1 + \beta))$. It takes the second limit as $\gamma(1 - \beta)/2 + 1/(2f)$, which is equal to the difference above. The sum can start at a first photon just below the lowest energy. For such a photon, AM³ takes the logarithm of a negative number and converts the NaN to an integer. This conversion is undefined, and it gives 0 on arm64. am3_mojo returns 0.
- Energy of the γγ pairs of the full kernel: The full kernel gives the pairs their spectrum. AM³ then scales the pairs to the energy of the absorbed photons, but it sums this energy over the photons above $m_e c^2$ only. The pairs thus miss the energy of the softer photons. For photons from 0.1 to 10 MeV whose number per unit energy falls as $E^{-2}$, the pairs get 0.939 of the absorbed energy. am3_mojo sums over all absorbed photons, and the pairs get 0.988 (`test_gamma_gamma_pairs_against_the_absorbed_photons` in `tests/unit/test_kept_approximations.py`).
- Target photons of photo-pion production: The products of photo-pion production take only the target photons below `optimize_photopion_target_photon_max` (511 keV by default). But AM³'s nucleon loss takes all target photons. The nucleons that interact with photons above the limit therefore vanish. For protons at $10^{12}$ eV on photons at 2 MeV, 4% of the protons vanish per step. am3_mojo leaves these interactions out of the nucleon and photon losses too.
- Split of the photo-pion products between two grid points: The energy of a product mostly lies between two grid points, and AM³ splits the product between them. Let $f$ be the fraction of a bin from the lower point to the product, and $\Delta$ the bin width in $\ln E$. The share $(e^{f\Delta} - 1)/(e^\Delta - 1)$ for the upper point keeps the product's number and energy. AM³ takes $f + f(1 - f)\Delta/2$, the weight of an interpolation between the two points. This weight books the product too high in energy by $f(1 - f)\Delta^2$ of it, up to 0.25%. am3_mojo takes the share that keeps the energy, also for the kaons and charm hadrons that it adds (`test_number_split_keeps_number_and_energy`).
- Bethe–Heitler pairs of energetic collisions: The Bethe–Heitler pair spectrum integrates over $\omega$, the photon energy in the proton's frame (in units of $m_e c^2$). The integral goes up to $2\gamma_p\varepsilon$. Here $\gamma_p$ is the proton's Lorentz factor and $\varepsilon$ the photon's energy in units of $m_e c^2$. AM³'s cross section ends at $\omega = 600$ (see Kept as in AM³), but AM³ spreads its 5 integration steps over the whole range. Above $2\gamma_p\varepsilon \approx 600$, the steps sample the part below 600 ever more coarsely. Above about 8000, they do not sample it at all. The protons then lose energy without making pairs. am3_mojo integrates up to the smaller of $2\gamma_p\varepsilon$ and 600, with the last integration point at that limit itself. (If the last point is the sum of the steps, it rounds to just above 600 in a tenth of the bins. The cross section is zero there, and the pairs of these bins lose 5–11%.)
- Components after their emission stops: AM³ evolves the pair components of Bethe–Heitler, γγ and $\mathrm{pp}$ pair production only with their emission on. The same holds for the pion, muon and neutrino components of the photo-pion and $\mathrm{pp}$ interactions. After a switch-off, these components stay as they were, but the totals escape and decay. The components then no longer add up to the totals. am3_mojo evolves the components while they hold or get particles.
- Power-law injection off the grid: AM³ normalizes a power-law injection by its integral over the grid. If the spectrum has no bin on the grid, for example with its lowest energy above the grid's top, the integral is zero. Every bin of the injection is then NaN, and the solver leaves the species unchanged. am3_mojo injects nothing in this case.
- Hard or steep power-law injection: AM³ computes the shape of a power-law injection as it is, and then multiplies it by the luminosity density over its integral. For a steep or hard spectrum, the shape overflows, for example with index −60 or 400. Bins of the injection are then infinite or NaN, and the solver leaves the electrons without particles. Without a cutoff, at index −20, the factor underflows to 0 instead, and the electrons get no injection. am3_mojo computes the shape relative to its largest bin, from its logarithm (`test_power_law_injection_of_a_hard_or_steep_spectrum`).
- Quantum synchrotron without a field: With `set_process_quantum_syn(True)` and no field, AM³'s $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ synchrotron kernel is $\infty \times 0$ = NaN. The solver then leaves the photons unchanged. am3_mojo emits no synchrotron photons in this case.
- Stale emission sources: If the $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ synchrotron or inverse-Compton emission is switched off during a run, AM³ keeps adding the photons of its last step. After the hadronic processes are switched off, AM³ evolves the photon components of hadronic origin with the sources of their last step. am3_mojo sets these sources to zero.
- Reads and writes past the end of arrays:
  - `PionDecay::initialize_kernels` reads `egrid_had[j+1]` past the grid for the top hadron bin. In some fresh processes, this read made the total photon spectrum zero for whole runs. Hadron inverse-Compton cooling reads photon bins past the photon grid for the top 49 hadron bins. In some `AM3` objects, this read gave far too short or negative cooling times. am3_mojo uses the next grid point and empty bins instead.
  - The 8 getters of the emission timescales read arrays that `init_kernels()` allocates. Before that call, these getters crash AM³. am3_mojo allocates the arrays with the object.
  - If pion decay is off, AM³ reads the $\nu_\mu$ sources of pion decays on the neutrino grid from arrays of the hadron grid's length.
  - The hadron grid can become larger than the first one, for example with `update_energy_grid(1e-4, 1e2, 1e23)`. The shifted grid of the $\mathrm{pp}$ kernels then keeps the first size, and so do the three arrays of the photon-convolved photo-pion kernels. AM³ writes these arrays past their end. With photo-pion production on, the C++ code then crashes in `init_kernels()`.
  - $\mathrm{pp}$ collisions: the transition energy of their $\delta$ approximation can lie within one bin of the top of the hadron grid. AM³ then reads past the multiplicity table (for a transition energy above the top, see below).
  - $\mathrm{pp}$ collisions can be switched on after an `init_kernels()` without them, on a smaller grid than that of an earlier call with them. The loop of their $\delta$ approximation then runs to the transition energy of that earlier grid, past the arrays. am3_mojo ends the loop at the top of the grid.
  - $\mathrm{pp}$ collisions with the transition energy of their $\delta$ approximation below about 12 GeV: this energy leaves the approximation no pion bins. AM³ then starts the pions of the kernel at a negative bin, before the arrays. Below about 1.1 GeV, AM³ starts them at `(int)(-inf)`. am3_mojo starts them at the first bin.
  - γγ pair production with its eight-bin approximation on at `init_kernels()` and switched off later (`set_optimize_annihilation_pair_emission(False)`): AM³ reads the full pair kernel. But AM³ did not allocate this kernel. am3_mojo builds the full kernel in the next step.
  - Optimization limits beyond the end of a grid (for example `set_optimize_photopion_target_photon_max(1e30)`) give an index past its end. am3_mojo clamps them to the grid. For the minimum proton energy of the Bethe–Heitler pairs, the index past the end means no protons. AM³ rounds indices up to even ones. When the top bin's index is odd, AM³ thus gives the index past the end to a minimum at or above the top bin. The getter of this minimum and `update_energy_grid` then read past the grid. am3_mojo gives the index past the end to every minimum from the energy of the next grid point above the grid on. am3_mojo's getter then returns that energy.
  - Target-photon limits of the inverse-Compton, Bethe–Heitler and photo-pion kernels can be lowered after `init_kernels()`. AM³ then multiplies the kernels of `init_kernels()` by the shorter photon vector, past its end. With the photo-pion limit lowered, the photons of `blazar_simple` vanished in some runs. am3_mojo rebuilds the kernels for the new limits (see Kernel settings after `init_kernels()`).
- OpenMP data race: The OpenMP build of AM³ races on `+=` in the photo-pion source terms. am3_mojo's parallel sections are race-free and deterministic (`tests/unit/test_threads.py`).
- Neutrino timescales: `get_t_neutrino_escape` and `get_t_neutrino_extra_cooling` return the whole neutrino grid. AM³ returns only the hadron-grid length.
- Emission timescale of the $\pi^0$ photons: AM³'s `get_t_photon_emission_from_pi0` divides the photons by the sum of the photo-pion and $\mathrm{pp}$ components of their source. That sum leaves out injected $\pi^0$. With `parse_sed` off, the sum is zero, and the timescale is at least $10^{100}$ s. am3_mojo divides by the source that the photons evolve with.
- Photo-pion timescale of the photons: AM³'s `get_t_photon_photopion` returns the photons' loss rate on the photon grid of the hadrons. The energies of this grid lie 0.21 bin below those of `get_egrid_photons()`. In `test_photopion_photon_loss_timescale_on_the_photon_grid`, the result of the getter is thus 4.8% off in the median bin and 22% off at 462 keV. am3_mojo returns the rate on the photon grid, because the photons evolve with that rate.
- Timescales of the hadrons after a step without them: With `set_process_hadronic(False)`, AM³ skips the hadronic part of a step. But AM³'s getters still report the rates of the last step with the hadronic part. These are the `get_t_…` of the protons' synchrotron, inverse-Compton, Bethe–Heitler, photo-pion and $\mathrm{pp}$ losses, the pions' and muons' cooling and the photons' photo-pion loss. am3_mojo reports no rate (1e200 s) after such a step. The rates stay in its arrays, because the proton maximum-energy estimate reads them in the first step after the processes are switched on again.
- Decay timescales with a decay off: AM³'s `get_t_pion_decay` and `get_t_muon_decay` report the decay time whatever `set_process_pion_decay` and `set_process_muon_decay` are. With a decay off, the evolution applies none. am3_mojo then reports no rate (1e200 s), also for the neutrons, kaons and charm hadrons that it adds. For these three, it also reports none while their decay tables are missing. Their decay then does not act although it is switched on, after an `init_kernels()` that built no tables (`test_decay_timescales_follow_the_decay_switches`, `test_decay_timescales_without_the_decay_tables`).
- Emission timescales of the $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ with merged positrons: With `set_process_merge_positrons_into_electrons(True)`, AM³'s `get_t_pair_emission_from_…` divide the electrons. But AM³ then does not evolve the electrons. am3_mojo divides all pairs by the sources of the electrons and the positrons.
- Density setters with merged positrons: With `set_process_merge_positrons_into_electrons(True)`, AM³ evolves the pairs. But `set_current_densities_electrons` and `set_current_densities_positrons` do not change the pairs. The particles that these setters set do not reach the pairs, also when they are set before the merge is switched on. am3_mojo then sets the pairs to the sum of the $\mathrm{e}^-$ and $\mathrm{e}^+$ (`test_density_setters_with_merged_positrons`).
- Components after `parse_sed` is switched on again: Steps with `set_process_parse_sed(False)` evolve only the totals. So the components keep the values of the last step before these steps. When the switch is turned on again, AM³ evolves the components on from there. The components then no longer add up to the totals. In that case, am3_mojo sets the components to zero, so that they count the particles from the next step on (`test_components_after_parse_sed_is_switched_on_again`).
- Optimization getters: AM³'s `get_optimize_…` energy getters return the grid's units, $m_e c^2$ or GeV, but their setters take eV. If a limit is set to the value that its getter returns, the limit thus moves. For example, the photo-pion target-photon limit moves from 511 keV to 1 eV. am3_mojo's getters return eV.
- Timescales read back: A `get_t_…` getter of an escape or extra-cooling rate returns $10^{200}$ s for a rate of zero. For the $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ and photons, whose rates are in code units, it returns $2.7 \times 10^{177}$ s. AM³'s setters turn these values into a small rate, and the getter then returns half of them. In am3_mojo, a setter that receives a getter's values keeps the rates (`test_timescale_setters_take_back_what_the_getters_return`).
- Solver settings after `init_kernels()`: The solvers take the thresholds of `set_solver_threshold_…` and the source integral of `set_solver_simpleqintegration` when they are built. AM³ ignores later settings, although their getters report them. am3_mojo's solvers follow the settings every step.
- Kernel settings after `init_kernels()`: AM³ builds its kernels with the settings at `init_kernels()`. These are the $\mathrm{pp}$ transition energy and pion models (`set_pp_transition_energy_to_delta_approx`, `set_pp_model_charged_pions`, `set_pp_model_neutral_pions`). The settings also include the optimization limits of the photo-pion, Bethe–Heitler and inverse-Compton kernels. AM³ ignores later settings, although their getters report them. For example, $\mathrm{pp}$ pion model 1 changes the pions by up to 48%. A photo-pion target-photon limit raised to $10^8$ eV adds half the neutrinos. If the setting comes after `init_kernels()`, neither effect occurs (`test_kernel_settings_after_init`). If a setting of a set of kernels changes, am3_mojo rebuilds that set in the next step.
- Uninitialized flag: `profile_timing` is uninitialized in AM³. In am3_mojo, it defaults to `False`.

Calls that AM³ does not check, where am3_mojo raises an exception:
- `evolve_step()` before `init_kernels()` (AM³ crashes), or after `update_energy_grid()` without a new `init_kernels()` (AM³ runs with the kernels of the old grid). The same call also raises after the hadronic processes or photo-pion production are switched on, if they were off at `init_kernels()` (AM³ crashes).
- `AM3.__init__()` on an object that is initialized already (AM³ accepts the call). Such a call would put a new simulation over the old one without freeing its kernels and arrays. Create a new object instead.
- `update_energy_grid()` outside $10^{-100}$ eV $\le E_{\gamma,\mathrm{min}} \lt 511$ keV, $10^{-100}$ eV $\le E_{\nu,\mathrm{min}} \lt 1$ GeV and $10^{10}$ eV $\le E_\mathrm{max} \lt 10^{300}$ eV. AM³ accepts, for example, negative energies. It also accepts minima so small that the number of bins overflows. With $\mathrm{pp}$ collisions on and the default transition energy (next item), `init_kernels()` needs $E_\mathrm{max} \ge 110$ GeV (`test_grid_for_the_default_pp_transition_energy`).
- `init_kernels()` with $\mathrm{pp}$ collisions on and the transition energy of their $\delta$ approximation (`set_pp_transition_energy_to_delta_approx`) above the top of the hadron grid. AM³ then crashes in the first `evolve_step()`. If such a value is set after `init_kernels()`, the next `evolve_step()` raises too.
- A $\mathrm{pp}$ pion model that does not exist, passed to `set_pp_model_charged_pions` (models 0 and 1) or `set_pp_model_neutral_pions` (models 0 to 3). AM³ takes any number. Its `init_kernels()` then prints that it uses model 0, while the getter returns the number.
- An array of the wrong length passed to a setter (`set_current_densities_…`, `set_injection_rate_…`, `set_t_…`). AM³ copies as many values as the grid has, past the end of a shorter array.
- An array of complex numbers, strings, booleans or Python objects passed to one of these setters, and a list that mixes booleans with numbers. AM³ converts any array that numpy can convert. It drops the imaginary part of a complex number, and it reads the string "1.5" as 1.5 and `True` among numbers as 1 (`test_arrays_that_are_not_real_numbers_are_rejected`).
- `True`, `False` or a numpy boolean (also a 0-d array of one) passed to a setter of a number, to `update_energy_grid()` or to an `eV_to_…_grid_index()` method. AM³ takes them as 1 and 0. For example, `set_pp_model_charged_pions(True)` selects model 1, and `set_mag_field(True)` sets a field of 1 G (`test_numbers_that_are_booleans_are_rejected`).
- A complex number passed there. AM³ takes a numpy complex number as its real part, with only a warning: `set_mag_field(np.complex128(3+4j))` sets a field of 3 G (`test_numbers_that_are_complex_are_rejected`).
- A value that is not finite (NaN or infinity) passed to a setter of a number or an array. That includes the time step, the field, the timescales, the escape fractions, the densities, the injection parameters, the optimization limits and the solver thresholds. A negative time step or field raises too (a field of −0.0 as well). With a negative field, AM³'s synchrotron kernels are NaN, and the photons do not change. AM³ takes these values. A NaN time step empties every species, and a NaN field, escape or expansion timescale empties the $\mathrm{e}^\pm$. A NaN optimization limit falls to the bottom of its grid.
- A timescale below $10^{-200}$ s (also of 0 or below) passed to `set_escape_timescale`, `set_expansion_timescale` or a `set_t_…` array setter. The same holds for an escape fraction below 0 or above $10^{100}$. AM³ takes these values. An escape time of 0 empties its bins, and a negative timescale or fraction makes the particles grow instead of escaping or cooling. With an escape timescale of $-10^4$ s, the electrons of `test_timescales_that_are_not_positive_are_rejected` reach 68 times those of $10^4$ s after 40 steps. Down to $5.6 \times 10^{-309}$ s, the rate $1/t$ is finite, but the solver's sums of such rates overflow (`test_timescales_too_short_for_the_solver_are_rejected`).
- Power-law injection parameters out of range (`set_powerlaw_injection_parameters_…`): a volume of 0 or below, a negative luminosity or a negative cutoff steepness. A minimum, break or maximum energy of 0 or below is out of range too. AM³ takes these values. A negative volume or luminosity gives a negative injection, and a break energy of 0 gives a NaN injection. A negative steepness cuts the spectrum below the maximum energy instead of above it.
- A negative $\mathrm{pp}$ target density (`set_pp_target_proton_density`) and an acceleration factor of 0 or below (`set_electron_accel_factor`, `set_proton_accel_factor`). The same holds for a negative value in an array of densities or injection rates (`set_current_densities_…`, `set_injection_rate_…`). AM³ takes these values. A negative target density makes the protons' $\mathrm{pp}$ loss timescales negative, and it gives no pions. An acceleration factor of $-1$ puts the estimated maximum energy at the bottom of the grid. An acceleration factor of 0 gives NaN acceleration timescales without a field. A negative density stays negative where nothing acts on it.
- An energy of 0 or below, or one that is not finite, passed to `eV_to_…_grid_index`. AM³ converts its logarithm to an integer without a check. The result is the smallest `int` for 0, 0 for a negative energy or NaN, and the largest for infinity.
- An energy below the first point of the grid, passed to `eV_to_…_grid_index`. Up to one bin below that point, AM³ gives the index 0. Further below, it gives a negative index, which numpy counts from the end of an array. `eV_to_had_grid_index(1e8)` gives −23, which reads the proton bin at $4.3 \times 10^{21}$ eV (`test_grid_index_below_the_grid`).
- `evolve_step()` with a source, or escape or cooling rates, that are not finite. Such values come, for example, from a power-law injection whose luminosity over volume overflows, or from a field whose square overflows (`set_mag_field(1e160)`). The call also raises when its solution holds a NaN. AM³ leaves the species unchanged where the source is not finite, and sets the NaN densities of the others to zero. am3_mojo raises within the step, after the species before it were advanced.

## Effect of the fixes on the four scenarios

The patched C++ AM³ is compared with the original one at the last checkpoint, and with builds with single fixes reverted. These comparisons show which fixes matter:
- `blazar_simple`: the particles that cool into a window from far above, even at a hundredth of the default time step. This fix changes the Bethe–Heitler and photo-pion $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ components, and the total $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ where these pairs outnumber the injected electrons. The muon fix raises the muons and neutrinos, the $\nu_e$ most, and the photons of the high-energy bump, far below the peak. The fix of the $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ of muon decays moves the photo-pion $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ to lower energies. The other fixes act only in the far tails.
- `leptohadronic_all`: the solver fixes, the quantum-synchrotron one, the integral of $K_{5/3}$, the two muon fixes and the protons of the $\mathrm{pp}$ $\delta$ approximation. The particles that cool into a window from far above change the $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ and photons. This fix changes the $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ components most at low energies, where some components were empty in the original. The muon fixes raise the muons and neutrinos at all energies, the $\nu_e$ most, and move the photo-hadronic and $\mathrm{pp}$ $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ to lower energies. Near their cutoffs, pions, muons and neutrinos also change through the quadrature of the source integral. The particles that cool into a Chang & Cooper window from an escape-dominated one raise the protons near their cutoff by up to 7%. These particles also raise the neutrons and charm hadrons by 2–3%. The protons of the $\mathrm{pp}$ $\delta$ approximation raise the $\mathrm{pp}$ pions and muons below about 10 GeV, by up to 7% at its top. Crank–Nicolson steps at large Courant numbers change the $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ below 5 MeV by up to 2% and the photons by up to 0.7%. AM³'s integral of $K_{5/3}$ gives the quantum-synchrotron emission 1.9% too much energy. The pair cascade of the dense source amplifies this error. The fix of the integral changes the $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ by up to 30% at 0.62 MeV and the photons by up to 9%. The energies of the pairs and photons drop by 8.6% and 6.6%. Near their cutoff, the protons change by up to 15%. Below their peak, around $10^{13}$ eV, the neutrinos change by up to 9%.
- `ssc_external`: mainly the maximum-energy estimate with merged $\mathrm{e}^\pm$. With the fix, the electron injection ends at the estimated cutoff instead of the grid top. The fix thus removes the $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ and photons above the cutoff and changes the spectra below it too.
- `exotic` (a grid that `update_energy_grid` ends at $3 \times 10^{14}$ eV): the solver fixes, the photons on the hadron grid and the two muon fixes. The external injections and the decay products of injected particles matter too. So do the protons of the $\mathrm{pp}$ $\delta$ approximation (pions and muons up by 2%). Pions, muons and neutrons gain energy, mostly from the protons in the top bins of the grid, which AM³ emptied. The photons on the hadron grid raise the photo-hadronic yields, and the photons that hadrons emit land one bin lower. The steps at large Courant numbers change the photons and the $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ by up to 0.3%.

The series of the inverse-Compton cooling functions changes the scenarios only slightly, most at the $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ cutoff of `ssc_external`. The integration of the Bethe–Heitler pairs up to $\omega = 600$ changes the Bethe–Heitler pairs of `blazar_simple`, `leptohadronic_all` and `exotic`. This fix changes these pairs by up to 2–5% in single bins, and their photons by up to 3–5%. The target photons of photo-pion production and the cooling time across a cell change the scenarios by less than $10^{-4}$. The Chang & Cooper densities read by the windows below change `blazar_simple` by $3 \times 10^{-5}$, `leptohadronic_all` by $7 \times 10^{-4}$ and `ssc_external` by 0.2%. This fix changes `exotic` by up to 0.7% (its injected pairs, at $3 \times 10^{-10}$ of their peak).

The windows shorter than 6 bins do not change the scenarios, nor do the cooling rates of a step without cooling. The loss rates of processes switched off, the escape on the way down and the sink of the RK4 integral do not change them either. The dilogarithm of the inverse-Compton photon loss changes `blazar_simple` by $5 \times 10^{-10}$, `leptohadronic_all` by up to $3 \times 10^{-5}$ and `exotic` by $1.4 \times 10^{-6}$. The dilogarithm of the cooling functions changes the fluxes by up to $1.4 \times 10^{-6}$ (`exotic`) and the cooling timescales by up to $1.5 \times 10^{-5}$. The steps at large Courant numbers change `blazar_simple` by less than $10^{-4}$, and `ssc_external` only in 3 bins at its cutoff (0.7%).

The integration limits of the γγ pair kernel change only `exotic`, the one scenario without the eight-bin approximation. They change its γγ pairs by up to 0.31% at 5 MeV, through their normalization to the photons absorbed, and by 0.23% above 10 TeV. Its $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ change by $6 \times 10^{-4}$ and its photons by $5 \times 10^{-5}$ ($2.8 \times 10^{-3}$ far below the peak). `ssc_external` merges its positrons from the start: the split of the merged pairs gives it electrons equal to its pairs instead of zeros.

The grid points and optimization limits change only the two scenarios that set limits on a grid of `update_energy_grid`. `ssc_external` changes by $3 \times 10^{-7}$. In `exotic`, the limits of `set_had_optimizer_on` lie one bin lower, at or below the 1 TeV and 1 MeV that it sets. Its Bethe–Heitler pairs rise by 8%, and its photo-pion neutrinos fall by 6%.

Four fixes are compared with builds of am3_mojo instead (added processes off), each with one fix reverted. The photons of $\pi^0$ decays change the three scenarios with hadrons. Their $\pi^0$ photons lose 4.5% (`blazar_simple`), 6.2% (`leptohadronic_all`) and 4.1% (`exotic`) of their energy, and up to 46% in single bins at their top end. In `leptohadronic_all`, all photons lose 3.4% of their energy and the γγ pairs 4.9%, and the $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ at 0.62 MeV fall by 15%. The split of the photo-pion products lowers the energy of the photo-pion pions and muons by 0.3–1.1%. It changes them by up to 4.5% in single bins of `blazar_simple`, and by up to 10% at the top of the grid of `exotic`. It also raises the neutrons at 150 GeV by 5–6%. The photon spectrum of $\mathrm{pp}$ collisions below 100 GeV changes only `leptohadronic_all`. Its $\mathrm{pp}$ $\pi^0$ photons lose 2.4% of their energy, and up to 10% in single bins. Its other fluxes change by up to 0.12%. The energy of the γγ pairs of the full kernel changes `exotic` by up to $4 \times 10^{-6}$. None of the four fixes changes `ssc_external`, which has no hadrons. The transition energy of the $\mathrm{pp}$ $\delta$ approximation does not change the scenarios. Its default, 100 GeV, is not the energy of a grid point.

## Interface

- Module name: The module is `am3_mojo` (AM³: `am3`). `am3_mojo.AM3_API_VERSION` is `"1.0.1"`, the version string of that AM³ (`am3.__version__`).
- Booleans: Switch getters return `bool`, as in AM³, and so does `get_verbosity_level()` (AM³: a float, 0.0 or 1.0). Switch setters accept only `True`/`False` (AM³ also accepts any number and `None`) and raise an exception for any other value. Docstrings and the printed switch table say `True`/`False`.
- Exceptions: am3_mojo raises errors as Python `Exception`, with AM³'s message where AM³ has one. AM³ raises `ValueError` or `RuntimeError` instead (or pybind11's `TypeError` for an argument of the wrong type).
- Docstrings: am3_mojo keeps AM³'s docstrings but corrects those that misstate the code. The default time step is $10^{-3}$ of the escape timescale at construction, $10^3$ s (AM³: 0.01 of the escape timescale). This default does not follow `set_escape_timescale`. The minimum energy of the inverse-Compton photons is used whether or not `set_lep_optimizer_on` is called. It is 7.8 meV by default (AM³: only with `set_lep_optimizer_on`, 1 meV). The same holds for the maximum target-photon energies of inverse Compton and Bethe–Heitler pair production, and for the minimum proton energy of the latter. These are 511 keV and 1.1 TeV by default (AM³: only with `set_lep/had_optimizer_on`, 1 MeV and 1 TeV). AM³'s docstring of the Bethe–Heitler photon limit also describes a parameter of the γγ kernel. The photo-pion target-photon limit is 511 keV by default (AM³: 1 MeV). The `set_optimize_…_grid` switches always read as `True`, and the full grids are used (see Kept as in AM³). `get_powerlaw_injection_parameters_…` return the eight parameters of their setters, from the volume (AM³ names six, from the luminosity). With positrons merged, `get_electrons()` and `get_positrons()` hold the split pairs (AM³: not updated). The matrix threshold defaults to 10 (AM³: 100). AM³'s docstring of `set_process_pp_emission_pi0_into_cascade` swaps its two treatments. `init_kernels()` ends with `clear_am3()`. The escape rates follow `set_escape_timescale` only from its call on, so a fresh object has no escape (AM³: not stated). `clear_am3()` also zeroes the escape and extra-cooling arrays and the rates that the processes computed. It also resets the estimated maximum energies (AM³: the densities and injection rates). `set_process_parse_sed` says how the components restart (see Components after `parse_sed` is switched on again). `get_electron_neutrinos()` and `get_muon_neutrinos()` say that they leave out the anti-neutrinos (AM³: not stated). `set_lep_optimizer_on` and `set_had_optimizer_on` take no argument (AM³: "Set to 0 (off) / 1 (on)"). The γγ approximation has one name, eight-bin (AM³: "14-bin" in its docstring and "8bin" in its output). The volume of `set_powerlaw_injection_parameters_…` is in cm³ (AM³: cm⁻³). The `get_t_pair_emission_from_…` getters say what they divide, and the decay-time getters say that they follow the decay switches (see Kept as in AM³ and Decay timescales with a decay off).
- GSL errors: GSL domain errors abort the AM³ process. am3_mojo reports these errors from the solver.

## Numerics: same equations, not the same rounding

am3_mojo evaluates AM³'s equations in plain double precision with the platform libm, and lets the compiler fuse multiply-adds and vectorize. Its results are deterministic and identical for any thread count. Against the patched C++ AM³, the four scenarios agree to about $10^{-7}$ ([the comparison](../../README.md#reference-comparison)). In two places, AM³'s result depends on the last bit of a floating-point number:
- $\boldsymbol{\gamma\gamma}$ **pair kernel lookup tables.** AM³ reads $A_0(h)$ and related functions of the kernel from tables of step 0.01 with `int(h / 0.01)`, without interpolation. Rounding can thus move `h` to the neighboring entry. am3_mojo keeps the tables, and so AM³'s accuracy, but can pick a different neighbor than the C++ build. The effect of a different neighbor on the `exotic` pair spectra is below $3 \times 10^{-8}$.
- Inverse-Compton cooling near $\gamma = 1$: The cooling function $F(\varepsilon, \gamma)$ $= \gamma$ $[f_1(\varepsilon\bar\gamma) - f_1(\varepsilon/\bar\gamma)]$ $- \varepsilon$ $[f_2(\varepsilon\bar\gamma) - f_2(\varepsilon/\bar\gamma)]$ ([Jones 1965](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.137.B1306), eqs. 13–15) subtracts nearly equal numbers when $\gamma \approx 1$. Here $\bar\gamma = \gamma + \sqrt{\gamma^2 - 1}$, and $\varepsilon$ is in units of the particle's rest energy. In both codes, this subtraction cancels most of the digits of the cooling function. At $\gamma = 1$, both codes take $\bar\gamma = \gamma + \sqrt{\gamma^2 - 1 + 10^{-10}}$. Both codes evaluate the series of $f_1$ and $f_2$ in the same form (the fix above). But rounding still moves the cooling timescale of the lowest bin ($\gamma = 1$) a little. The fluxes are unaffected, because the solver keeps that bin empty.

AM³ also computes the inverse-Compton emission of the $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ in single precision. It divides the photons by their sum and multiplies them with the emission kernel as 32-bit numbers. am3_mojo takes double precision. The scattered photons of the two codes agree to about $10^{-7}$. AM³ loses the target photons below $10^{-38}$ of the photon sum, where the normal numbers of single precision end. Targets at $10^{-40}$ and $10^{-43}$ of the sum scatter $5 \times 10^{-6}$ and 0.5% less energy than in am3_mojo. Targets at $10^{-46}$ of the sum scatter none (`tests/reference/test_single_precision.py`).

## Misprints of the AM³ paper

am3_mojo writes every formula as the papers' equation, in plain arithmetic ([Gao et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.05306) and [Klinger et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.13371)). Where the papers give no equation (the quantum suppression of synchrotron cooling), am3_mojo writes the formula as the C++ code has it. Where the AM³ paper has misprints, am3_mojo follows the C++ code:

- In the inverse-Compton loss, the cooling functions $f_1$ and $f_2$ have $-2\ \mathrm{Li}_ 2(-2z)$ instead of $+2\ \mathrm{Li}_ 2(-2z)$ and $+\mathrm{Li}_ 2(-2z)$. They go to zero only linearly as $z \to 0$, as $8{z}$ and $6{z}$ instead of $(8/9)z^3$ and $(4/3)z^2$. The printed functions thus miss the Thomson limit. Here $\mathrm{Li}_ 2$ is the dilogarithm. The loss rate has $n_\gamma(\varepsilon)/(\gamma^2\varepsilon)$ where its photon density per unit $\varepsilon$ needs $n_\gamma(\varepsilon)/(\gamma^2\varepsilon^2)$. The C++ code has the correct forms of [Jones 1965](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.137.B1306) (eqs. 14–15, and eq. 13 apart from its $1/\beta$, see Kept as in AM³). The same section also writes $\sigma_T \propto m_X^{-4}$ for the $m_X^{-2}$ of its own definition. It takes $\varepsilon$ in units of $m_e c^2$, but the formula needs units of the particle's rest energy (as the code has it). Here $m_X$ is the mass of the particle.
- The inelastic $\mathrm{pp}$ cross section (of [Kafexhiu et al. 2014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.7369)) has the threshold factor $[1 - (T_p/E_\mathrm{th})^{1.9}]^3$, which is negative above the threshold. Kafexhiu et al.'s eq. 1 and the code have $[1 - (E_\mathrm{th}/T_p)^{1.9}]^3$. Here $T_p$ is the proton's kinetic energy and $E_\mathrm{th}$ its value at the threshold.
- The quantum-synchrotron emission kernel has $\xi = 3/(2F_B\gamma)$ for $(3/2)F_B\gamma$, which its own $x = \varepsilon/(\gamma\xi)$ and the argument $2\varepsilon/(3\gamma^2F_B)$ of $F_\xi$ require. It also has $K_{5/3}(z)$ for $K_{2/3}(z)$ in the second term of $F_\xi$ ($z x \xi^2 = y^2/(1 - y)$ with $y = \varepsilon/\gamma$, the factor of $K_{2/3}$ in the quantum-synchrotron spectrum). The code has $(3/2)F_B\gamma$ and $K_{2/3}$. The code also averages the kernel over the pitch angle, but the paper does not mention this average. The kernel's prefactor $\sqrt{3}\thinspace\alpha m_e c^2$ is an energy, and the code's rate has $m_e c^2/h$ in its place. Here $F_{B}$ is the magnetic field in units of the critical field and $F_\xi$ the paper's spectral function. $K_\nu$ is a modified Bessel function, $\alpha$ the fine-structure constant and $h$ the Planck constant.
- The synchrotron kernel has a factor $(m_X/m_e)^2$ too many for the particles heavier than $\mathrm{e}^\pm$. Here $\sigma_T$ and $B_\mathrm{crit}$ are those of the particle, as the paper defines them. With this factor, a proton radiates about $3.4 \times 10^6$ times the energy that its synchrotron cooling removes. The asymptotic form of $\hat R(z)$ for $2z \gt 10$ has $99/{165}$ for $99/{162}$, the coefficient of its asymptotic series. Here $\hat R$ is the kernel's synchrotron function. The code has neither misprint.
- The $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ inverse-Compton kernel $\tilde R(u, v)$ (printed alike by [Gao et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.05306)) has $u$ and $v$ interchanged and $(u - 1)^3$ for $(1 - v)^2$. Gao et al. also leave the photon's energy out of $u$. The paper cites the kernel as eq. 2.49 of [Blumenthal & Gould](https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.42.237). That equation defines the variables of their kernel, eq. 2.48. In the paper, the total rate (the integral of the kernel) lacks the factor $\sigma_T$. The paper's variables are $u = 2\gamma\varepsilon_i$ and $v = \varepsilon_o/\gamma$, where $\varepsilon_i$ and $\varepsilon_o$ are the photon energies before and after the scattering. With these variables, the code has

  $$\tilde R = [6(1 - v)(v/u) \ln(v/[2u(1 - v)]) - 3(v/u - 2 + 2v)(v/u + 1 + v^2/2 - v)]/[4u\gamma(1 - v)^2].$$
- The step cross section of the hadrons' inverse Compton has $H(1 - \tfrac{1}{2}\gamma\varepsilon m_e/m_X)$. This form lets a photon take up to twice the particle's energy. The code cuts at $\gamma\varepsilon m_e/m_X = 1/2$, that is $H(1 - 2\gamma\varepsilon m_e/m_X)$. Here $H$ is the Heaviside step function.

## Kept as in AM³

- AM³ evolves all pion components with its $\pi^+$ solver. am3_mojo gives these components solvers with the same configuration.
- The quantum suppression of synchrotron cooling, $1/(1 + \sqrt{2}\thinspace \chi^{2/3})^2$. This factor has the classical limit and the $\chi^{-4/3}$ scaling of the quantum limit of [Berestetskii, Lifshitz & Pitaevskii](https://doi.org/10.1016/C2009-0-24486-2) (§90, eq. 90.27). At small $\chi$, however, the factor starts as $1 - 2\sqrt{2}\thinspace \chi^{2/3}$ instead of their $1 - (55\sqrt{3}/16)\thinspace \chi$ (eq. 90.26). AM³ computes $\chi$ with $m_e c^2/B_\mathrm{cr}$ rounded to $1.2 \times 10^{-8}$ eV/G.
- The ultra-relativistic forms of the continuous losses. Synchrotron cooling is $\propto \gamma^2$ (exact: $p^2 = \gamma^2\beta^2$). The inverse-Compton loss $-\dot\gamma$ $= (3/16)\ \sigma_T c \int d\varepsilon\ n(\varepsilon)$ $F(\varepsilon, \gamma)/(\gamma^2 \varepsilon^2)$ is $\beta$ times the exact Klein–Nishina loss rate in an isotropic photon field. Here $n$ is per unit $\varepsilon$, $\varepsilon$ is in units of the particle's rest energy, and $\sigma_T$ is that of the particle, $\propto m^{-2}$. $F$ is the cooling function above. The exact rate has $\gamma p$ instead of $\gamma^2$ in the denominator, as in eq. 13 of [Jones](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.137.B1306), with $\beta\gamma^2 = \gamma p$. $F$ itself is exact. Both ultra-relativistic forms differ from the exact rates by more than 1% for $\gamma \lesssim 7$ (inverse Compton, $\beta \lt 0.99$) and $\gamma \lesssim 10$ (synchrotron, $\gamma^2/(\gamma^2 - 1) \gt 1.01$). At $\gamma = 3$, they differ by -6% and +12.5%. In the lowest bin that the solver keeps ($\gamma = 1.22$), $\beta = 0.57$. $F$ is negative for photons more energetic than the particle. The particle then gains energy from the photons. Both codes set $F$ to zero there, because their solvers treat only losses.
- The Bethe–Heitler pair spectrum ends where the photon energy in the proton's frame reaches 600 $m_e c^2$. The [AM³ paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.13371) takes this energy as a conservative limit of the Born approximation that it uses. The protons' Bethe–Heitler energy loss has no such limit, so collisions above this limit take energy from the protons without making pairs. On photons of one energy, the pairs get 72% of the protons' loss at $2\gamma_p\varepsilon = 760$, 12% at 2500 and 0.75% at 19000. On a broad power-law photon field, the pairs get at least 96% of the protons' loss up to protons of $4 \times 10^{18}$ eV. They get 83–96% at $4 \times 10^{19}$ eV and 9–12% at $4 \times 10^{20}$ eV. This field reaches from 1 meV to 511 keV, with slopes from 1.5 to 2.5 per unit energy.
- `get_photons_pi0_decay()` is the sum of its photo-pion and $\mathrm{pp}$ components. This sum leaves out the photons of injected $\pi^0$ (`set_injection_rate_pi0`), but the total photons hold them.
- The optimization limits of Bethe–Heitler pair production and inverse Compton restrict only the products. The Bethe–Heitler pairs take the protons above `optimize_bethe_heitler_incoming_protons_min` (1.1 TeV by default) and the photons below `optimize_bethe_heitler_target_photon_max` (511 keV). The protons' loss takes all protons and photons. The inverse-Compton photons take the targets below `optimize_compton_target_emax` (511 keV), but the $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ cooling and the photon loss take all photons. Collisions outside the limits thus take energy from the protons, $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ and photons without making pairs or photons. If the losses leave out these collisions, the Bethe–Heitler loss of protons below 1.1 TeV disappears. So does the $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ cooling on photons above 511 keV. am3_mojo restricts the losses only with the photo-pion limit (see Target photons of photo-pion production).
- The target-photon limit of photo-pion production (`optimize_photopion_target_photon_max`) cuts the photons on the photon grid of the hadrons. The limit thus acts at 0.979 times the energy that its getter reports.
- The `optimize_*_grid` switches are C++ `bool`s that always read as `True`. Their "every 2nd grid point" code paths are unreachable in AM³, and am3_mojo does not implement them.
- The source integral of the solver's regime 4 (cooling only). For each bin, $y$ is the energy from which its particles cool within the step. Both semi-analytic regimes take the density at that energy from the line between the grid points around it. Regime 3 integrates the source exactly, because the source is linear between grid points. Regime 4 takes the source's integral up to $y$ from the line between its integrals up to those grid points. In the cell that holds $y$, this interpolation differs from the exact integral by $\Delta\thinspace\Delta q\thinspace u(1-u)/2$. Here $\Delta$ is the cell's width in $\ln E$, $\Delta q$ the change of the source across it and $u$ the fraction of the cell below $y$. In a steady state with constant rates, this error cancels that of the density at $y$. Regime 4 is then exact there, and regime 3 is not. After one step from empty, regime 3 is exact and regime 4 is not. For a source in one bin, regime 4 gives 3.0 and 0.86 times the exact density after one step. These factors apply in the two bins whose $y$ falls beside the source bin, and the errors fade within 5 steps. In the steady state, regime 3 gives those bins 0.89 and 1.11 times the exact density. Elsewhere, both regimes are exact to $5 \times 10^{-5}$ or better, apart from the escape that regime 4 leaves out.
- The photon spectrum of $\mathrm{pp}$ collisions beyond its range. [Kafexhiu et al. 2014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.7369) give this spectrum for proton energies up to 1 PeV, and AM³ uses it on the whole grid. Take protons whose number per unit energy falls as $E^{-2}$ over a decade. With SIBYLL (the default), their $\pi^0$ photons get 0.49 of the energy that the protons lose in $\mathrm{pp}$ collisions from $10^{12}$ eV. They get 0.53 from $10^{14}$ eV, 0.71 from $10^{16}$ eV, 1.00 from $10^{17}$ eV and 1.61 from $10^{18}$ eV. With Geant4, they get 0.48 from $10^{14}$ eV, 1.11 from $10^{16}$ eV and 4.42 from $10^{18}$ eV. Far beyond the range, the photons alone thus get more energy than the protons lose (`test_pp_photons_beyond_the_range_of_their_parameterization` in `tests/unit/test_kept_approximations.py`, as the tests of the next three items).
- The decay products of a step. The decay source of a step is $n\thinspace\Delta t/\tau$. Here $n$ is the density of the parents at the end of the step, $\Delta t$ the time step and $\tau$ the decay time. This source is exact in a steady state. It is also exact over a whole pulse of injection: once the parents are gone, every injected parent has made its products. During an injection, the parents and their products together count more particles than were injected, for example pions and their muons. After the first step, they count $(1 - e^{-x})(1 + 1/x)$ times as many, with $x = \Delta t/\tau$. That is 1.26 times at $\Delta t = \tau$ and 1.10 times at $\Delta t = 10\tau$ (`test_decay_products_of_an_injection`). Parents that are there when a step starts, for example set with `set_current_densities_pion_plus`, decay within the step. If the step is much longer than $\tau$, none are left at its end, and they make no products. Pions from $10^{11}$ to $10^{13}$ eV, with $\tau$ from 20 μs to 2 ms, make no muons in a step of 1 s. In 1000 steps of 1 ms, 2.35% of them make a muon, and in 1000 steps of 10 μs, 88% do (`test_decay_products_come_from_the_parents_left_after_a_step`). The pions, muons, neutrons, kaons and charm hadrons all decay in this way.
- The pairs of the eight-bin γγ approximation (the default). It gives the $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ of an absorbed photon to 14 lepton bins, in 15 equal shares. These bins lie between 0.09 and 1 times the photon's energy. It makes no pairs of absorbed photons below 1.9 MeV. For photons from 0.1 to 10 MeV whose number per unit energy falls as $E^{-2}$, the pairs get 0.726 of the absorbed energy. Each absorbed photon gives 0.52 $\mathrm{e}^\pm$. From 1 keV to 1 GeV, these figures are 0.991 and 0.81. With the full kernel (`set_optimize_annihilation_pair_emission(False)`), they are 0.988 and 0.95, and 1.000 and 0.84. The full kernel is scaled to the energy of the absorbed photons, not to their number (`test_gamma_gamma_pairs_against_the_absorbed_photons`).
- The inverse-Compton emission of the hadrons. The hadrons emit with AM³'s step cross section, and cool with the exact function of [Jones](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.137.B1306). The energy of the photons thus differs from the energy that the hadrons lose. Take protons from $10^{10}$ to $10^{20}$ eV whose number per unit energy falls as $E^{-2}$, and target photons of the same slope. On targets from 1 meV to 0.1 eV, the photons get 1.256 times the energy lost. They get 0.705 times it on targets from 1 eV to 1 keV, and 1.002 times it on targets from 0.1 meV to 100 keV (`test_proton_compton_emission_against_cooling`). The pions and muons emit and cool in the same way, as do the kaons and charm hadrons that am3_mojo adds.
- The emission timescales of the $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ (`get_t_pair_emission_from_…`). With positrons tracked apart, they divide the electrons by the electrons' part of a source. With `set_process_parse_sed(False)`, the steps evolve only the totals. The timescales of the photo-pion and $\mathrm{pp}$ pairs then report no source.
