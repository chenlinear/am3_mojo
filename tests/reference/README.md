# Differences from the C++ AM³

This file details how am3_mojo differs from the C++ AM³ v1.2.0, which the main README summarises under [Differences from the C++ original](../../README.md#differences-from-the-c-original). The folder also holds the [comparison with the C++ code](../../README.md#reference-comparison): `setup_reference.sh` builds AM³ v1.2.0 and a copy with the fixes and [constants](#constants) of `am3_fixes.patch`. Each test file's docstring says what it checks.

## Bugs of AM³ v1.2.0 that am3_mojo fixes

Each fix has a regression test in `tests/unit/test_fixes.py`, whose docstrings give the set-up and size of each bug. The patched C++ build has the same fixes, so that both codes solve the same equations. It leaves out only those of the interface (call and argument checks, optimisation limits, getters, the profiling flag) and of the OpenMP race, as the reference builds are serial. The solver bugs change AM³'s results in common set-ups.

*The solver* solves each step of its semi-analytic regimes (3: cooling and escape, 4: cooling only) with the Green's function of the cooling equation. Its regime 2 (cooling by less than an e-fold per step) uses Crank–Nicolson steps with the weights of [Chang & Cooper](https://doi.org/10.1016/0021-9991%2870%2990001-X) (1970, J. Comput. Phys. 6, 1, eq. 18). Without diffusion, these take the flux between two cells from the upper cell. The solver has these bugs:
- **Sign of the Green's function.** The default source integral is `simple_integral_q`. It weights the injection with $\exp(+\int \alpha/\vert A\vert\thinspace dx)$ instead of the decaying $\exp(-\int \alpha/\vert A\vert\thinspace dx)$ ($\alpha$: escape and decay rate, $A$: cooling rate, $x = \ln E$). The RK4 variant (`set_solver_simpleqintegration(True)`) has the right sign. With the wrong sign, the cascade diverges at large time steps.
- **Injection dropped.** Where $\alpha/\vert A\vert \lt e^{-300}$, the same integral skipped the injection along with the escape. As the solver adds $10^{-150}$ to $\alpha$, this can happen without escape or decay: with cooling within about 1.4 ms in the $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ and photon solvers, and with any cooling in the hadron solvers.
- **Quadrature of the source integral.** Regime 3 is used where cooling moves particles by more than an e-fold within a step and escape is between 1% and 100 times the cooling rate. The source integral there is $\int_x^y q(x') \exp(-\int_x^{x'} \alpha/\vert A\vert\ dx'')$ $dx'/\vert A(x)\vert$, with $y$ the energy from which particles reach $x$ within the step and $q$ the injection rate.
  - **AM³'s quadrature:** a sum of $q(x') \exp(-\int_x^{x'+\Delta} \alpha/\vert A\vert)\ \Delta$ over the grid points $x'$ below $y$. Each point is attenuated by the cell above it ($x' = x$ too). The rule is first order, and it leaves out the cell up to $y$.
  - **Escape-dominated cells** ($z = \Delta\thinspace \alpha/\vert A\vert \gtrsim 1$, with $\Delta = 0.1$ the step of the grid in $\ln E$): even with the sign fixed, the densities come out about $z/(e^z - 1)$ times too low.
  - **Cooling-dominated cells:** the densities are too high for falling source spectra and too low for rising ones, more so for steeper sources. This also happens at time steps of the escape time or shorter, wherever regime 3 is used.
  - **am3_mojo** integrates every cell exactly for the solver's splines (`simple_integral_q` in `src/am3_mojo/solver/solver.mojo`).
- **No cooling in a window above much slower cooling.** AM³ maps each bin of a regime-3/4 window to its energy at the start of the step through the cooling time summed from the lowest of the 10 cells that pad the window below. Where those cells cool about $10^{15}$ times more slowly, or not at all, this sum swamps the window's cooling times in double precision: the window's cooling is distorted or lost. am3_mojo gives the cells below a window the cooling rate of the window's first bin.
- **Sign of the cooled particles in regime 4.** The particles that cool into bin $x$ from $y$ are $n(y)\thinspace A(y)/A(x)$; AM³ adds $-n(y)\thinspace A(y)/A(x)$. Here $n$ is the density. This matters where regime 4 has a finite $y$, e.g. with fast energy-independent cooling, and leaves the particles too little energy in the steady state.
- **Endless loop in the RK4 integral.** When the RK4 variant widens its integration range again after narrowing it, the loop does not update its step and never ends.
- **Range of the RK4 integral.** Where the sink is weak, the RK4 variant integrates the injection up to just below the top of the solver's window ($0.99\ x_\mathrm{max}$) instead of up to $y$. It thus counts particles injected too far up to reach $x$ within the step.
- **Particles that cool into a window from far above.** The solver splits the grid into windows of one regime each and solves them from the top down. AM³ feeds a window only from the 10 cells above it: at their old densities (Chang & Cooper windows), within those cells (semi-analytic windows) or not at all (escape-dominated windows, regime 1). Where the window above cools faster, the particles that cool into the window within the step are lost, with or without escape. am3_mojo pads the semi-analytic windows up to the energy from which particles reach them within the step. It adds the particles that cross into the other windows to their top cell (`cells_reached` and `crossing_count` in `src/am3_mojo/solver/solver.mojo`). In `test_regime_boundary.py` it then keeps the electrons to $9 \times 10^{-4}$, and in `tests/literature_checks/triplet/steady_states.py` the triplet pairs to 0.1% ($3 \times 10^{15}$ photons cm⁻³, time step $0.1\ t_\mathrm{esc}$).
  - With the fix, a sharp pulse that flows into a Chang & Cooper window reaches its top cell, which Crank–Nicolson undershoots in the next step: [Chang & Cooper](https://doi.org/10.1016/0021-9991%2870%2990001-X) prove non-negative densities (their eqs. 19–21) only for fully implicit steps. AM³ sets negative densities to zero, so a clipped pair component no longer adds up with the others to all pairs. In the set-up of `test_pair_components_add_up_with_triplet` at a time step of $10^4$ s, they add up only to within $9 \times 10^{-2}$ in one bin (`test_window_top_undershoot.py`). At the test's 3000 s, and in the four scenarios, no density goes negative.
- **Injection integral of regime 4.** AM³ accumulates the integral of the injection from the bottom of the window, and restarts it where $q\thinspace dx / \mathrm{sum} \lt 10^{-15}$. The bins just below each restart keep almost no significant digits of their injection. The particles of a bin come from above it, so am3_mojo accumulates the integral from the top of the window.
- **Particles in the top bins of a grid.** The solver leaves out the top bin of every grid and the empty bins above the highest one with particles. AM³ holds that empty window to at least 6 bins, so a spectrum that reaches the 6 highest evolved bins loses their particles every step. As the top bin's rates are zero, AM³'s semi-analytic regimes also evolve the highest evolved bin as if it did not cool. In am3_mojo the empty window ends at the first bin with particles, and the top bin keeps its rates. Of the four scenarios, only `exotic` reaches the top of its grid.

*The simulation:*
- **Quantum-synchrotron cooling of heavy particles.** With `set_process_quantum_syn_cooling(True)`, AM³ multiplies the synchrotron cooling of every charged species by $1/(1 + \sqrt{2}\thinspace \chi^{2/3})^2$, where $\chi$ is the quantum parameter (below). This factor interpolates between the classical limit and the $\chi^{-4/3}$ quantum limit of Landau & Lifshitz ([Berestetskii, Lifshitz & Pitaevskii](https://doi.org/10.1016/C2009-0-24486-2), §90, eq. 90.27). Its accuracy is discussed under Kept as in AM³. For a particle of mass $m$ and charge $e$ in the field $B$, the quantum parameter is $\chi = \gamma B/B_\mathrm{cr}$ with $B_\mathrm{cr} = m^2c^3/(e\hbar) \propto m^2$. AM³, however, computes it as $\gamma B/m$ for all species. This overstates it by $m/m_e$ for protons, pions and muons (and am3_mojo's kaons), and so overstates their quantum suppression. The pion and muon cooling breaks of the neutrino spectra in strong fields are therefore off. The $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ are unaffected. am3_mojo scales $\chi$ by $m_e/m$.
- **Series of the inverse-Compton cooling functions.** Below $z = 0.1$, AM³ evaluates the inverse-Compton cooling functions $f_1(z)$ and $f_2(z)$ by Taylor series ([Jones 1965](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.137.B1306), eqs. 14–15; see Numerics below). It keeps only the first three terms of Jones' series (his Table I), which are too large near $z = 0.1$. At that point, AM³ switches to the closed forms. The inverse-Compton cooling of every species is therefore too fast on photons just below $\varepsilon\bar\gamma = 0.1$ ($\varepsilon$ in units of the particle's rest energy, $\bar\gamma$ as under Numerics), and it jumps back at $\varepsilon\bar\gamma = 0.1$. am3_mojo uses Jones' series to $z^{10}$ (with exact coefficients).
- **Muons of pion decays.** In a $\pi^\pm$ decay the muon takes a fraction $x$ of the pion's energy, between $r = (m_\mu/m_\pi)^2$ and 1, with a distribution for each helicity ([AM³ paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.13371), after [Lipari et al. 2007](https://arxiv.org/abs/0704.0718)). That of the left-handed $\mu_\mathrm{L}^+$ is largest at $x = 1$, which AM³'s kernel leaves out, so a decay makes too few muons, with too little energy. This also lowers the neutrinos and $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ of muon decays. am3_mojo integrates the distributions over the bin of each grid point (`PionDecay.initialize_kernels` in `src/am3_mojo/physics/decays.mojo`), so that a decay makes one muon.
- $\boldsymbol{\mathbf{e}^\pm}$ **of muon decays.** In the V−A decay of a muon, the $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ have the spectrum of the $\nu_\mu$-type neutrino, which depends on the muon's helicity. For the $\mathrm{e}^\pm$, the AM³ paper and code take $\tfrac{4}{3}(1 - x^3)$ for every muon ("omitting chiralities"), the spectrum of one helicity. The $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ of the $\mu_\mathrm{L}^+$ and $\mu_\mathrm{R}^-$, most of the muons of pion decays, therefore take 0.40 of the muon's energy instead of 0.30. am3_mojo gives each helicity its spectrum (`MuonDecay.Calc_MUtoE` in `src/am3_mojo/physics/decays.mojo`).
- **Maximum-energy estimates.** With `set_estimate_max_energies(True)`, the electron and proton estimates read one bin past the grid when acceleration wins everywhere, and the injection becomes NaN when that memory reads 0. With positrons merged into electrons, the electron estimate reads the cooling of the electron solver, which is then not evolved. am3_mojo uses the cooling of the merged $\mathrm{e}^\pm$, and the energy one bin above the grid's top when acceleration wins everywhere.
- **Photons with `parse_sed` off.** With `set_process_parse_sed(False)` and the default direct treatment of the $\mathrm{pp}$ $\pi^0$ photons, AM³ drops the photons of these $\pi^0$ from the total photon spectrum.
- **Decay products of injected particles with `parse_sed` on** (the default). AM³ then takes the $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ of muon decays and the photons of $\pi^0$ decays as the sums of their photo-pion and $\mathrm{pp}$ components, which leave out the decay products of injected muons, charged pions and $\pi^0$. am3_mojo computes the totals from all particles; the components only split them.
- **External injection of muons and neutrinos.** AM³ evolves an injection set with `set_injection_rate_mu_plus_left` as $\mu_\mathrm{R}^-$, and one set with `set_injection_rate_mu_minus_right` as $\mu_\mathrm{L}^+$. It adds that of `set_injection_rate_electron_neutrinos` to all four neutrino species and ignores those of the other three neutrino setters. am3_mojo injects each into its own species.
- **Photons on the hadron grid after `update_energy_grid`.** For most lowest photon energies $E_{\gamma,\mathrm{min}}$, every power of ten among them, `update_energy_grid` computes the shift between the photon and hadron grids one bin too large, unlike the constructor (and so the default grid). The photo-hadronic processes then see every photon at $e^{-0.1}$ of its energy, which slows photo-pion production most near threshold, and the photons that hadrons emit land one bin too high. am3_mojo computes the shift as the constructor does.
- **Stale decay sources.** AM³ does not reset the decay sources of a step when a decay is off. With pion decay switched off during a run, it keeps injecting the muons of the last step with pion decay, and with muon decay off the $\nu_e$ and $\bar\nu_e$. With pion decay off and muon decay on, it adds the muon-decay $\nu_\mu$ to the total $\nu_\mu$ sources of all earlier steps. am3_mojo recomputes the sources every step.
- **Reads and writes past the end of arrays:**
  - `PionDecay::initialize_kernels` reads `egrid_had[j+1]` past the grid for the top hadron bin, which made the total photon spectrum zero for whole runs in some fresh processes. Hadron IC cooling reads photon bins past the photon grid for the top 49 hadron bins, which got absurdly short or negative cooling times in some AM3 objects. am3_mojo uses the next grid point and empty bins instead.
  - The 8 getters of the emission timescales read arrays that `init_kernels()` allocates, so they crash AM³ before it; am3_mojo allocates them with the object.
  - With pion decay off, the $\nu_\mu$ sources of pion decays are read on the neutrino grid from arrays of the hadron grid's length.
  - On a hadron grid larger than the first one (e.g. `update_energy_grid(1e-4, 1e2, 1e23)`), the shifted grid of the $\mathrm{pp}$ kernels and the three arrays of the photon-convolved photo-pion kernels keep the first size and are written past their end. With photo-pion production on, the C++ then crashes in `init_kernels()`.
  - $\mathrm{pp}$ collisions: a transition energy of their $\delta$ approximation within one bin of the top of the hadron grid reads past the multiplicity table (above the top, see below).
  - Optimisation limits beyond the end of a grid (e.g. `set_optimize_photopion_target_photon_max(1e30)`) give an index past its end; am3_mojo clamps them to the grid.
  - Target-photon limits of the inverse-Compton, Bethe–Heitler and photo-pion kernels lowered after `init_kernels()` make AM³ multiply the kernels of `init_kernels()` by the shorter photon vector, past its end. With the photo-pion limit lowered, the photons of `blazar_simple` vanished in some runs. am3_mojo takes the photons above a lowered limit as zero.
- **OpenMP data race.** The OpenMP build of AM³ races on `+=` in the photo-pion source terms. am3_mojo's parallel sections are race-free and deterministic.
- **Neutrino timescales.** `get_t_neutrino_escape` and `get_t_neutrino_extra_cooling` return the whole neutrino grid. AM³ returns only the hadron-grid length.
- **Uninitialised flag.** `profile_timing` is uninitialised in AM³. In am3_mojo it defaults to `False`.

*Calls that AM³ does not check,* where am3_mojo raises an exception:
- `evolve_step()` before `init_kernels()` (AM³ crashes), after `update_energy_grid()` without a new `init_kernels()` (AM³ runs with the kernels of the old grid), or after switching on hadronic processes that were off at `init_kernels()` (AM³ crashes).
- `update_energy_grid()` outside $0 \lt E_{\gamma,\mathrm{min}} \lt 511$ keV, $0 \lt E_{\nu,\mathrm{min}} \lt 1$ GeV and $10^{10}$ eV $\le E_\mathrm{max} \lt 10^{300}$ eV (AM³ accepts, e.g., negative energies).
- `init_kernels()` with $\mathrm{pp}$ collisions on and the transition energy of their $\delta$ approximation (`set_pp_transition_energy_to_delta_approx`) above the top of the hadron grid (AM³ crashes in the first `evolve_step()`).
- An array of the wrong length passed to a setter (`set_current_densities_…`, `set_injection_rate_…`, `set_t_…`). AM³ copies as many values as the grid has, past the end of a shorter array.

## Effect of the fixes on the four scenarios

Comparing the patched with the original C++ AM³ at the last checkpoint, and builds with single fixes reverted, shows which fixes matter:
- **`blazar_simple`:** the particles that cool into a window from far above, even at a hundredth of the default time step. They change the Bethe–Heitler and photo-pion $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ components, and the total $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ where these pairs outnumber the injected electrons. The muon fix raises the muons and neutrinos, the $\nu_e$ most, and the photons of the high-energy bump, far below the peak. The fix of the $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ of muon decays moves the photo-pion $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ to lower energies. The other fixes act only in the far tails.
- **`leptohadronic_all`:** the solver fixes, the quantum-synchrotron one and the two muon fixes. The particles that cool into a window from far above change the $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ and photons, the $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ components most at low energies, where some were empty in the original. The muon fixes raise the muons and neutrinos at all energies, the $\nu_e$ most, and move the photo-hadronic and $\mathrm{pp}$ $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ to lower energies. Near their cutoffs, pions, muons and neutrinos also change through the quadrature of the source integral.
- **`exotic`** (a grid that `update_energy_grid` ends at $3 \times 10^{14}$ eV): the solver fixes, the photons on the hadron grid, the two muon fixes, the external injections and the decay products of injected particles. Pions, muons and neutrons gain energy, mostly from the protons in the top bins of the grid, which AM³ emptied. The photons on the hadron grid raise the photo-hadronic yields, and the photons that hadrons emit land one bin lower.
- **`ssc_external`:** mainly the maximum-energy estimate with merged $\mathrm{e}^\pm$. With the fix, the electron injection ends at the estimated cutoff instead of the grid top, which removes the $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ and photons above it and changes the spectra below it too.

The series of the inverse-Compton cooling functions changes the scenarios only slightly, most at the $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ cutoff of `ssc_external`.

## Constants

am3_mojo takes every physical constant and every particle mass and mean life from [PDG 2026](https://pdg.lbl.gov/2026/), its Table 1.1 (CODATA 2022) and its particle listings. They are in `src/am3_mojo/utils/constants.mojo`, which cites the table entry or listing page of each. AM³'s own values are rounded or older:

| elementary charge [esu] | $4.803 \times 10^{-10}$ | $4.8032047 \times 10^{-10}$ |

AM³ also rounds the code units of the leptons and photons (length $\sqrt{\sigma_T}$, time $\sqrt{\sigma_T}/c$, density and rate) one by one, so that they do not quite fit together. am3_mojo derives them from the Thomson cross section $\sigma_T$ and the speed of light $c$, as it does the field and frequency units of AM³'s synchrotron kernels. The patched C++ build has am3_mojo's values (the hunks of `am3_fixes.patch` marked `am3_mojo PDG`). Only AM³'s $\pi^0$ mean life ($8.52 \times 10^{-17}$ s, PDG 2026: $8.43 \times 10^{-17}$ s) and the neutron mean life of its neutron solver (879.5 s) stay, as AM³ does not use them.

## Interface

- **Module name.** The module is `am3_mojo` (AM³: `am3`). `am3_mojo.AM3_API_VERSION` is `"1.0.1"`, the version string of that AM³ (`am3.__version__`).
- **Booleans.** Switch getters return `bool`, as in AM³, and so does `get_verbosity_level()` (AM³: a float, 0.0 or 1.0). Switch setters accept only `True`/`False` (AM³ also accepts any number and `None`) and raise an exception for any other value. Docstrings and the printed switch table say `True`/`False`.
- **Exceptions.** Errors are raised as Python `Exception`, with AM³'s message where it has one. AM³ raises `ValueError` or `RuntimeError` instead (or pybind11's `TypeError` for an argument of the wrong type).
- **GSL errors.** GSL domain errors abort the AM³ process; am3_mojo reports them from the solver.

## Numerics: same equations, not the same rounding

am3_mojo evaluates AM³'s equations in plain double precision with the platform libm, and lets the compiler fuse multiply-adds and vectorise. Its results are deterministic and identical for any thread count. Against the patched C++ AM³, `blazar_simple` and `ssc_external` agree to about $10^{-7}$ ([the comparison](../../README.md#reference-comparison)). In three places, AM³'s result depends on the last bit of a floating-point number, so that `leptohadronic_all` and `exotic` differ by up to $2 \times 10^{-3}$:
- $\boldsymbol{K_{5/3}}$ **integral** (`int_bessel_k53`, in the quantum-synchrotron kernel). AM³'s loop `y = yi; while (y < yf) { …; y += 0.01 * (yf - yi); }` is meant as 100 steps, but rounding in the accumulated `y` adds a 101st step at the upper limit for some arguments. am3_mojo always takes 100 steps. The integral then differs by less than 1% (`test_special_functions.py`), and the `leptohadronic_all` spectra by up to $10^{-3}$.
- $\boldsymbol{\gamma\gamma}$ **pair kernel lookup tables.** AM³ reads $A_0(h)$ and related functions of the kernel from tables of step 0.01 with `int(h / 0.01)`, without interpolation, so rounding can move `h` to the neighbouring entry. am3_mojo keeps the tables, and so AM³'s accuracy, but can pick a different neighbour than the C++ build. This changes the far tail of the `exotic` pair spectra by up to $2 \times 10^{-3}$.
- **Inverse-Compton cooling near $\gamma = 1$.** The cooling function $F(\varepsilon, \gamma)$ $= \gamma$ $[f_1(\varepsilon\bar\gamma) - f_1(\varepsilon/\bar\gamma)]$ $- \varepsilon$ $[f_2(\varepsilon\bar\gamma) - f_2(\varepsilon/\bar\gamma)]$ ($\bar\gamma = \gamma + \sqrt{\gamma^2 - 1}$, $\varepsilon$ in units of the particle's rest energy; [Jones 1965](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.137.B1306), eqs. 13–15) subtracts nearly equal numbers when $\gamma \approx 1$, so both codes lose most of its digits there. At $\gamma = 1$, both take $\bar\gamma = \gamma + \sqrt{\gamma^2 - 1 + 10^{-10}}$. Both evaluate the series of $f_1$ and $f_2$ in the same form (the fix above), but rounding still moves the cooling timescale of the lowest bin ($\gamma = 1$) a little. The fluxes are unaffected, because the solver keeps that bin empty.

## Misprints of the AM³ paper

am3_mojo writes every formula as the papers' equation, in plain arithmetic ([Gao et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.05306); [Klinger et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.13371)). Where the papers give none (the quantum suppression of synchrotron cooling), it is written as the C++ code has it. Where the AM³ paper has misprints, am3_mojo follows the C++ code:

- In the inverse-Compton loss, the cooling functions $f_1$ and $f_2$ have $-2\ \mathrm{Li}_ 2(-2z)$ instead of $+2\ \mathrm{Li}_ 2(-2z)$ and $+\mathrm{Li}_ 2(-2z)$. They go to zero only linearly as $z \to 0$ (as $8{z}$ and $6{z}$ instead of $(8/9)z^3$ and $(4/3)z^2$, so they miss the Thomson limit). Here $\mathrm{Li}_ 2$ is the dilogarithm. The loss rate has $n_\gamma(\varepsilon)/(\gamma^2\varepsilon)$ where its photon density per unit $\varepsilon$ needs $n_\gamma(\varepsilon)/(\gamma^2\varepsilon^2)$. The C++ code has the correct forms, those of [Jones 1965](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.137.B1306) (eqs. 14–15, and eq. 13 apart from its $1/\beta$, see Kept as in AM³). The same section also writes $\sigma_T \propto m_X^{-4}$ for the $m_X^{-2}$ of its own definition. It takes $\varepsilon$ in units of $m_e c^2$, where the formula needs it in units of the particle's rest energy (as the code has it). Here $m_X$ is the mass of the particle.
- The inelastic $\mathrm{pp}$ cross section (of [Kafexhiu et al. 2014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.7369)) has the threshold factor $[1 - (T_p/E_\mathrm{th})^{1.9}]^3$, which is negative above the threshold. Kafexhiu et al.'s eq. 1 and the code have $[1 - (E_\mathrm{th}/T_p)^{1.9}]^3$. Here $T_p$ is the proton's kinetic energy and $E_\mathrm{th}$ its value at the threshold.
- The quantum-synchrotron emission kernel has $\xi = 3/(2F_B\gamma)$ for $(3/2)F_B\gamma$, which its own $x = \varepsilon/(\gamma\xi)$ and the argument $2\varepsilon/(3\gamma^2F_B)$ of $F_\xi$ require. It also has $K_{5/3}(z)$ for $K_{2/3}(z)$ in the second term of $F_\xi$ ($z x \xi^2 = y^2/(1 - y)$ with $y = \varepsilon/\gamma$, the factor of $K_{2/3}$ in the quantum-synchrotron spectrum). The code has $(3/2)F_B\gamma$ and $K_{2/3}$, and averages the kernel over the pitch angle, which the paper does not mention. The kernel's prefactor $\sqrt{3}\thinspace\alpha m_e c^2$ is an energy, and the code's rate has $m_e c^2/h$ in its place. Here $F_{B}$ is the magnetic field in units of the critical field and $F_\xi$ the paper's spectral function. $K_\nu$ is a modified Bessel function, $\alpha$ the fine-structure constant and $h$ the Planck constant.
- The synchrotron kernel has a factor $(m_X/m_e)^2$ too many for the particles heavier than $\mathrm{e}^\pm$, with $\sigma_T$ and $B_\mathrm{crit}$ of the particle as the paper defines them. With this factor, a proton would radiate about $3.4 \times 10^6$ times the energy its synchrotron cooling removes. The asymptotic form of $\hat R(z)$ for $2z \gt 10$ has $99/{165}$ for $99/{162}$, the coefficient of its asymptotic series ($\hat R$ is the kernel's synchrotron function). The code has neither misprint.
- The $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ inverse-Compton kernel $\tilde R(u, v)$ (printed alike by [Gao et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.05306)) has $u$ and $v$ interchanged and $(u - 1)^3$ for $(1 - v)^2$. Gao et al. also leave the photon's energy out of $u$. The paper cites the kernel as [Blumenthal & Gould](https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.42.237)'s eq. 2.49, which defines the variables of their kernel, eq. 2.48. In the paper, the total rate (the integral of the kernel) lacks the factor $\sigma_T$. With the paper's variables $u = 2\gamma\varepsilon_i$ and $v = \varepsilon_o/\gamma$, where $\varepsilon_i$ and $\varepsilon_o$ are the photon energies before and after the scattering, the code has

  $$\tilde R = [6(1 - v)(v/u) \ln(v/[2u(1 - v)]) - 3(v/u - 2 + 2v)(v/u + 1 + v^2/2 - v)]/[4u\gamma(1 - v)^2].$$
- The step cross section of the hadrons' inverse Compton has $H(1 - \tfrac{1}{2}\gamma\varepsilon m_e/m_X)$, which lets a photon take up to twice the particle's energy; the code cuts at $\gamma\varepsilon m_e/m_X = 1/2$, i.e. $H(1 - 2\gamma\varepsilon m_e/m_X)$. Here $H$ is the Heaviside step function.

## Kept as in AM³

- AM³ evolves all pion components with its $\pi^+$ solver; am3_mojo gives them solvers with the same configuration.
- The quantum suppression of synchrotron cooling, $1/(1 + \sqrt{2}\thinspace \chi^{2/3})^2$. It has the classical limit and the $\chi^{-4/3}$ scaling of the quantum limit of [Berestetskii, Lifshitz & Pitaevskii](https://doi.org/10.1016/C2009-0-24486-2) (§90, eq. 90.27). At small $\chi$, however, it starts as $1 - 2\sqrt{2}\thinspace \chi^{2/3}$ instead of their $1 - (55\sqrt{3}/16)\thinspace \chi$ (eq. 90.26). AM³ computes $\chi$ with $m_e c^2/B_\mathrm{cr}$ rounded to $1.2 \times 10^{-8}$ eV/G.
- The ultra-relativistic forms of the continuous losses. Synchrotron cooling is $\propto \gamma^2$ (exact: $p^2 = \gamma^2\beta^2$). The inverse-Compton loss $-\dot\gamma$ $= (3/16)\ \sigma_T c \int d\varepsilon\ n(\varepsilon)$ $F(\varepsilon, \gamma)/(\gamma^2 \varepsilon^2)$ ($n$ per unit $\varepsilon$, $\varepsilon$ in units of the particle's rest energy) is $\beta$ times the exact Klein–Nishina loss rate in an isotropic photon field. Here $\sigma_T$ is that of the particle, $\propto m^{-2}$, and $F$ is the cooling function above. The exact rate has $\gamma p$ instead of $\gamma^2$ in the denominator, as in [Jones](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.137.B1306)' eq. 13, with $\beta\gamma^2 = \gamma p$. $F$ itself is exact. Both ultra-relativistic forms differ from the exact rates by more than 1% for $\gamma \lesssim 7$ (inverse Compton, $\beta \lt 0.99$) and $\gamma \lesssim 10$ (synchrotron, $\gamma^2/(\gamma^2 - 1) \gt 1.01$). At $\gamma = 3$, they differ by -6% and +12.5%. In the lowest bin the solver keeps ($\gamma = 1.22$), $\beta = 0.57$. $F$ is negative for photons more energetic than the particle. The particle then gains energy from the photons. Both codes set $F$ to zero there, as their solvers treat only losses.
- The `optimize_*_grid` switches are C++ `bool`s that always read as `True`. Their "every 2nd grid point" code paths are unreachable in AM³ and are not implemented.
