# Differences from the C++ AM³

This file describes how am3_mojo differs from the C++ AM³ v1.2.0, which the main README summarises under [Differences from the C++ original](../../README.md#differences-from-the-c-original). The folder also holds the [comparison with the C++ code](../../README.md#reference-comparison): `setup_reference.sh` builds AM³ v1.2.0 and a copy with the fixes of `am3_fixes.patch`, and each test file's docstring says what it compares.

## Bugs of AM³ v1.2.0 that am3_mojo fixes

Each fix has a regression test in `tests/unit/test_fixes.py`, whose docstring gives the set-up and the size of the bug. The C++ build of the comparisons has the same fixes (`am3_fixes.patch`), so that both codes solve the same equations. It leaves out only the fixes of the interface (the checks of calls and arguments, the optimisation limits, the getters and the profiling flag) and of the OpenMP race, as the reference builds are serial. The solver bugs change AM³'s results in common set-ups.

*The solver* solves each step of its semi-analytic regimes (3: cooling and escape, 4: cooling only) with the Green's function of the cooling equation. Its regime 2 (cooling by less than an e-fold per step) uses Crank–Nicolson steps and the weights of [Chang & Cooper](https://doi.org/10.1016/0021-9991%2870%2990001-X) (1970, J. Comput. Phys. 6, 1, eq. 18). With no diffusion (AM³ sets the diffusion coefficient to $10^{-100}$), these weights take the flux between two cells from the upper one. The solver has these bugs:
- **Sign of the Green's function.** The default source integral is `simple_integral_q`. It weights the injection with $\exp(+\int \alpha/\vert A\vert\thinspace dx)$ instead of the decaying $\exp(-\int \alpha/\vert A\vert\thinspace dx)$ ($\alpha$: escape and decay rate, $A$: cooling rate, $x = \ln E$). The RK4 variant (`set_solver_simpleqintegration(True)`) has the right sign. At large time steps the cascade diverges. At $dt = 0.1\ R/c$, AM³ gives protons of $7 \times 10^{66}$ and pairs of $2 \times 10^{258}$ ($E\thinspace dN/dE$ in cm⁻³), whereas the RK4 variant and am3_mojo give $3 \times 10^6$ and $3\text{–}5 \times 10^9$. These values are for a leptohadronic set-up with $B = 100$ G, $R = 3 \times 10^{14}$ cm and $\mathrm{pp}$ collisions, after 30 steps.
- **Injection dropped.** Where $\alpha/\vert A\vert \lt e^{-300}$, the same integral skipped the injection along with the escape. The solver adds $10^{-150}$ to $\alpha$ in its internal units, so this happens where there is no escape or decay. In the $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ and photon solvers, which count time in units of $2.7 \times 10^{-23}$ s, the cooling must also take less than about 1.4 ms. In the hadron solvers, which count in seconds, any cooling is enough. The regression test has cooling in 10 μs and escape only below $10^{11}$ eV. In a single step, the 6 bins just below that energy lost $46\text{–}90\text{％}$ of their electrons to this bug (those further down less).
- **Quadrature of the source integral.** Regime 3 is used where cooling moves particles by more than an e-fold within a step and escape is between $1\text{％}$ and 100 times the cooling rate. The source integral there is $\int_x^y q(x') \exp(-\int_x^{x'} \alpha/\vert A\vert\ dx'')$ $dx'/\vert A(x)\vert$, with $y$ the energy from which particles reach $x$ within the step.
  - **AM³'s quadrature:** a sum of $q(x') \exp(-\int_x^{x'+\Delta} \alpha/\vert A\vert)\ \Delta$ over the grid points $x'$ below $y$. Each point is attenuated by the cell above it ($x' = x$ too). The rule is first order, and it leaves out the cell up to $y$.
  - **Escape-dominated cells** ($z = \Delta\thinspace \alpha/\vert A\vert \gtrsim 1$, with $\Delta = 0.1$ the step of the grid in $\ln E$): even with the sign fixed, the densities come out about $z/(e^z - 1)$ times too low. In a steady state at a time step much longer than the escape time, they are $7 \times 10^{-4}$ of the exact density just above regime 1 (escape 100 times faster than cooling), 0.08 at 1 GeV ($\vert A\vert/\alpha = 0.026$), 0.50 at 3 GeV and 0.85 at 10 GeV. This set-up has electrons with synchrotron cooling in 1 G, escape in $10^4$ s, injection $\propto E^{-1}$ per $\ln E$ and $dt = 10^8$ s.
  - **Cooling-dominated cells:** the densities are too high for falling source spectra and too low for rising ones, more so for steeper sources. This also happens at time steps of the escape time or shorter, wherever regime 3 is used.
  - **am3_mojo** integrates every cell accurately for the solver's splines, in which $\ln(\alpha/\vert A\vert)$ and $q$ are linear between grid points (`simple_integral_q` in `src/am3_mojo/solver/solver.mojo`). It agrees with the exact steady state of that test to $2 \times 10^{-3}$. It also agrees to $10^{-3}$ with the exact solution of one step, with energy-independent cooling and $\alpha/\vert A\vert$ from 0.05 to 50, also for a source rising as $E^2$ per $\ln E$ across the window. In this one-step test, AM³'s quadrature (with the sign fixed) was off by $+9\text{％}$ to $-96.5\text{％}$.
- **No cooling in a window above much slower cooling.** The solver maps each bin of a regime-3/4 window to the energy its particles had at the start of the step. It does so through the cooling time accumulated from the lowest of the 10 cells that pad the window below. Where those cells cool about $10^{15}$ times more slowly than the window, or not at all (below the grid), this sum swamps the window's cooling times in double precision. The window's cooling is then distorted, and from $10^{17}$ on lost: every bin is evolved as if it did not cool. A bin's solution involves only the bins above it, so am3_mojo gives the cells below a window the cooling rate of the window's first bin.
- **Sign of the cooled particles in regime 4.** The particles that cool into bin $x$ from $y$ are $n(y)\thinspace A(y)/A(x)$; AM³ adds $-n(y)\thinspace A(y)/A(x)$. This matters where regime 4 has a finite $y$, e.g. with fast energy-independent cooling. Protons with cooling times of 0.3, 0.5 and $0.9\ dt$ and escape in $100\ dt$ have $6\text{％}$, $23\text{％}$ and $49\text{％}$ too little energy in the steady state.
- **Endless loop in the RK4 integral.** When the RK4 variant has narrowed its integration range and widens it again, the widening loop never updates its step. The loop therefore never ends.
- **Range of the RK4 integral.** Where the sink is weak, the RK4 variant integrates the injection up to $0.99\ x_\mathrm{max}$ instead of up to $y$. This limit lies just below the top $x_\mathrm{max}$ of the solver's window. So the RK4 variant counts particles injected too far up to reach $x$ within the step. The error is $+4\text{％}$ in one step of three cooling times, with an energy-independent cooling time and escape in 20 cooling times. In a steady state, $y$ is the window's top, and only the cells above $0.99\ x_\mathrm{max}$ were missing.
- **Particles that cool into a window from far above.** The solver splits the grid into windows of one regime each and solves them from the top down. It pads the windows of regimes 2 to 4 with 10 cells above them (those of regime 1 get none). AM³'s Chang & Cooper windows of regime 2 took the old densities of these cells, without inflow at their top, and its escape-dominated windows of regime 1 took no inflow at all. Its semi-analytic windows of regimes 3 and 4 looked at most 10 cells up for the energy $y$ the particles had at the start of the step. Where the window above cools faster, the particles that cool into the window within the step were dropped (AM³'s `Solver.cc` has an inflow term for this commented out). In steady states of electrons with synchrotron cooling and escape, $3\text{–}23\text{％}$ of them were missing, and with cooling alone up to $28\text{％}$ (`tests/solver_checks/regime_boundary.py`). Electrons that cool down to where their cooling stops lost $72\text{％}$. The pairs of triplet pair production, which pile up just above its threshold, lost $3\text{％}$ (at a time step of $0.1\ t_\mathrm{esc}$; `tests/solver_checks/triplet_steady_states.py`, $3 \times 10^{15}$ photons cm⁻³). am3_mojo pads the semi-analytic windows up to the energy from which particles reach them within the step. It adds the particles that cross into a Chang & Cooper or escape-dominated window during the step to the window's top cell, counted along the characteristics (`cells_reached` and `crossing_count` in `src/am3_mojo/solver/solver.mojo`). These set-ups then keep their electrons to $8 \times 10^{-4}$, and the triplet pairs to $0.1\text{％}$. In `blazar_simple`, AM³'s photo-pion and Bethe–Heitler $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ components are $25\text{–}28\text{％}$ off at the default time step, and still $21\text{–}22\text{％}$ off at a hundredth of it. With the fix, they agree with the result at a hundredth of the time step to $2\text{％}$. At the last checkpoint of `leptohadronic_all`, the $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ rise by up to $26\text{％}$ and the photons by up to $9\text{％}$. These values are $28\text{％}$ and $7\text{％}$ at a tenth of the time step, and $46\text{％}$ and $30\text{％}$ at step 200. With the fix, a sharp pulse of particles that flows into a Chang & Cooper window reaches the window's top cell, which Crank–Nicolson undershoots in the next step. The proof by [Chang & Cooper](https://doi.org/10.1016/0021-9991%2870%2990001-X) that their scheme keeps densities non-negative (their eqs. 19–21) is for fully implicit steps. AM³ sets negative densities to zero, so a clipped pair component no longer adds up with the others to all pairs. In the set-up of `test_pair_components_add_up_with_triplet` at a time step of $10^4$ s, they add up only to $9 \times 10^{-2}$ in one bin (`tests/solver_checks/window_top_undershoot.py`). The test itself takes a time step of 3000 s, at which no density goes negative. In the four scenarios no density goes negative, with or without the added processes (counted with a build that reports each one).
- **Injection integral of regime 4.** Regime 4 takes the particles injected between $x$ and $y$ from a cumulative integral of $q$. AM³ accumulates it from the bottom of the window and restarts it where $q\thinspace dx / \mathrm{sum} \lt 10^{-15}$. The bins just below each restart kept almost no significant digits of their injection, and those where $q$ is more than about 47 orders of magnitude below its peak none. The particles of a bin come from above it, so am3_mojo accumulates the integral from the top of the window.
- **Particles in the top bins of a grid.** The solver leaves out the top bin of every grid, and the bins above the highest bin with particles (regime 0). AM³ held that window of empty bins to the minimum window length of 6 bins, so a spectrum that reached the 6 highest bins the solver evolves lost their particles every step. This hid a second bug: the top bin's cooling and escape rates are set to zero, so the semi-analytic regimes evolved the highest evolved bin as if it did not cool. With only the first bug fixed, the photons in the top bins grew $10^4$ to $10^7$ times per step at the large time step above, in both codes. In am3_mojo, the window of empty bins ends at the first bin with particles, and the top bin keeps its rates. Of the four scenarios, only `exotic` reaches the top of its grid (below).

*The simulation:*
- **Quantum-synchrotron cooling of heavy particles.** With `set_process_quantum_syn_cooling(True)`, AM³ multiplies the synchrotron cooling of every charged species by $1/(1 + \sqrt{2}\thinspace \chi^{2/3})^2$. This factor interpolates between the classical limit and the $\chi^{-4/3}$ quantum limit of Landau & Lifshitz ([Berestetskii, Lifshitz & Pitaevskii](https://doi.org/10.1016/C2009-0-24486-2), §90, eq. 90.27). Its accuracy is discussed under Kept as in AM³. The quantum parameter is $\chi = \gamma B/B_\mathrm{cr}$ with $B_\mathrm{cr} = m^2c^3/(e\hbar) \propto m^2$, but AM³ computes it as $\gamma B/m$ for all species. This overstates it by $m/m_e$ for protons, pions and muons (and am3_mojo's kaons), and so overstates their quantum suppression. The pion and muon cooling breaks of the neutrino spectra in strong fields are therefore off. The $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ are unaffected. am3_mojo scales $\chi$ by $m_e/m$.
- **Series of the inverse-Compton cooling functions.** Below $z = 0.1$, AM³ evaluates the inverse-Compton cooling functions $f_1$ and $f_2$ by Taylor series ([Jones 1965](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.137.B1306), eqs. 14–15; see Numerics below). It keeps only the first three terms of Jones' series (his Table I), which are $2.4\text{％}$ ($f_1$) and $1.2\text{％}$ ($f_2$) too large at $z = 0.1$. At that point, AM³ switches to the closed forms. The inverse-Compton cooling of every species is therefore too fast on photons just below $\varepsilon\bar\gamma = 0.1$ ($\varepsilon$ in units of the particle's rest energy). It is too fast by $2.4\text{％}$ for $\gamma \gtrsim 14$ ($2.5\text{％}$ at $\gamma = 10$, $3.3\text{％}$ at $\gamma = 1.5$ and $4.8\text{％}$ at $\gamma = 1.22$, the lowest $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ bin the solver keeps; $0.3\text{％}$ at $\varepsilon\bar\gamma = 0.05$). At $\varepsilon\bar\gamma = 0.1$, the cooling jumps back. am3_mojo uses Jones' series to $z^{10}$ (with exact coefficients), accurate to $2 \times 10^{-5}$ at $z = 0.1$.
- **Maximum-energy estimates.** With `set_estimate_max_energies(True)`, the estimates of the electron and proton maximum energies read one bin past the grid when acceleration wins everywhere. The injection becomes NaN when that memory reads 0. With positrons merged into electrons, the electron estimate reads the cooling rates of the electron solver, which is not evolved in that mode. Without adiabatic cooling (the default), acceleration then wins everywhere. am3_mojo uses the cooling of the merged $\mathrm{e}^\pm$, and the energy one bin above the grid top when acceleration wins everywhere.
- **Photons with `parse_sed` off.** With `set_process_parse_sed(False)`, AM³ drops the photons of $\pi^0$ decays from $\mathrm{pp}$ collisions from the total photon spectrum. It does so under the default direct treatment of the $\mathrm{pp}$ $\pi^0$ photons (`set_process_pp_emission_pi0_into_cascade(False)`).
- **Decay products of injected particles with `parse_sed` on.** `set_process_parse_sed(True)` is the default. With it, AM³ takes the $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ of muon decays and the photons of $\pi^0$ decays as the sums of their photo-pion and $\mathrm{pp}$ components. These sums leave out the decay products of injected muons, charged pions and $\pi^0$. With $\mu^-$ injected for 20 steps (and no hadrons), the $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ and photons come out $4 \times 10^{-34}$ and $10^{-8}$ times those with `parse_sed` off. am3_mojo computes the totals from all muons and $\pi^0$, and the components only split them.
- **External injection of muons and neutrinos.** AM³ evolves an injection set with `set_injection_rate_mu_plus_left` as $\mu_\mathrm{R}^-$, and one set with `set_injection_rate_mu_minus_right` as $\mu_\mathrm{L}^+$. It adds the set with `set_injection_rate_electron_neutrinos` to all four neutrino species. It ignores those of `set_injection_rate_electron_anti_neutrinos`, `set_injection_rate_muon_neutrinos` and `set_injection_rate_muon_anti_neutrinos`. am3_mojo injects each into its own species.
- **Photons on the hadron grid after `update_energy_grid`.** The photo-hadronic processes and the synchrotron and inverse-Compton emission of hadrons use the photons on the hadron grid (energies $1\ \mathrm{GeV} \times e^{n\Delta}$). These photons are interpolated from the photon grid at a shift of $\ln(1\ \mathrm{GeV}/m_e c^2)/\Delta \approx 75.8$ bins. For $79\text{％}$ of the values of $E_{\gamma,\mathrm{min}}$, `update_energy_grid` computed the whole-bin part of that shift one bin larger than the constructor does. These values include every power of ten from $10^{-21}$ eV up. The default grid is right. The photo-hadronic processes then saw every photon at $e^{-0.1}$ of its energy. A proton thus had the photo-hadronic rates of one with $e^{-0.1}$ of its energy. Photo-pion production was $8\text{％}$ slower in our test, $10\text{–}18\text{％}$ for photon spectra $\propto \varepsilon^{-2}$ to $\varepsilon^{-3}$, and more near threshold. The photons that hadrons emit also landed one bin ($e^{0.1}$) too high. am3_mojo computes the shift as the constructor does.
- **Stale decay sources.** AM³ does not reset the decay sources of a step when a decay is off. With pion decay switched off during a run, it keeps injecting the muons of the last step with pion decay. With muon decay switched off, it does the same with the $\nu_e$ and $\bar\nu_e$. With pion decay off and muon decay on, it adds the muon-decay $\nu_\mu$ to the total $\nu_\mu$ sources of all earlier steps. am3_mojo recomputes the sources every step.
- **Reads and writes past the end of arrays:**
  - `PionDecay::initialize_kernels` reads `egrid_had[j+1]` past the grid for the top hadron bin, which can spoil the $\pi^0$-decay kernel. In 8 of 95 fresh processes with photo-pion production, the C++ AM³ gave a total photon spectrum of zero for the whole run (11 of 40 with the set-up of `blazar_simple`, none of 52 with the patch). Hadron IC cooling reads photon bins past the photon grid, as the kernels of the top 49 hadron bins reach past it. In AM3 objects created after another one in the same process (5 of 45 such objects), the top 30 hadron bins got cooling times of $10^{-187}$ to $10^{-18}$ s, or negative ones, and wrong spectra where particles reach them. am3_mojo uses the next grid point and empty bins instead.
  - The 8 getters of the emission timescales (`get_t_photon_emission_from_…`, `get_t_pair_emission_from_…`) read arrays that `init_kernels()` allocates. Called before it, they crash AM³. am3_mojo allocates these arrays with the object.
  - With pion decay off, the $\nu_\mu$ sources of pion decays are read on the neutrino grid from arrays of the hadron grid's length.
  - Some arrays keep the size of the first grid but are written on a larger hadron grid (e.g. `update_energy_grid(1e-4, 1e2, 1e23)`). These are the shifted energy grid of the $\mathrm{pp}$ kernels (`egrid_shifted`) and the three arrays of the photon-convolved photo-pion kernels. With photo-pion production on, the C++ then crashes in `init_kernels()`.
  - $\mathrm{pp}$ collisions: a transition energy of their $\delta$ approximation (a proton energy) within one bin of the top of the hadron grid reads past the multiplicity table. (Above the top, AM³ crashes even with this fixed. am3_mojo raises an exception, see below.)
  - Optimisation limits beyond the end of a grid (e.g. `set_optimize_photopion_target_photon_max(1e30)`) give an index past its end. am3_mojo clamps them to the grid.
  - Target-photon limits of the inverse-Compton, Bethe–Heitler and photo-pion kernels can be lowered after `init_kernels()` (`set_optimize_compton_target_emax`, `set_optimize_bethe_heitler_target_photon_max`, `set_optimize_photopion_target_photon_max`). AM³ then multiplies the kernels of `init_kernels()` by the shorter photon vector, past its end. In our tests, with the photo-pion limit lowered to 3 keV, the photons of `blazar_simple` vanished within 30 steps in some runs and not in others. The memory read past the end decides which. am3_mojo takes the photons above a lowered limit as zero.
- **OpenMP data race.** The OpenMP build of AM³ races on `+=` in the photo-pion source terms. am3_mojo's parallel sections are race-free and deterministic.
- **Neutrino timescales.** `get_t_neutrino_escape` and `get_t_neutrino_extra_cooling` return the whole neutrino grid. AM³ returns only the hadron-grid length.
- **Uninitialised flag.** `profile_timing` is uninitialised in AM³. In am3_mojo it defaults to `False`.

*Calls that AM³ does not check,* where am3_mojo raises an exception:
- `evolve_step()` in three cases. Before `init_kernels()`, AM³ crashes. After `update_energy_grid()` without a new `init_kernels()`, AM³ runs with the kernels of the old grid. After switching the hadronic processes on when they were off at `init_kernels()`, AM³ crashes.
- `update_energy_grid()` outside $0 \lt E_{\gamma,\mathrm{min}} \lt 511$ keV, $0 \lt E_{\nu,\mathrm{min}} \lt 1$ GeV and $10^{10}$ eV $\le E_\mathrm{max} \lt 10^{300}$ eV (AM³ accepts, e.g., negative energies).
- `init_kernels()` with $\mathrm{pp}$ collisions on and the transition energy of their $\delta$ approximation (`set_pp_transition_energy_to_delta_approx`) above the top of the hadron grid (AM³ crashes in the first `evolve_step()`).
- An array of the wrong length passed to a setter (`set_current_densities_…`, `set_injection_rate_…`, `set_t_…`). AM³ copies as many values as the grid has, past the end of a shorter array.

## Effect of the fixes on the four scenarios

On the four scenarios of the tests, these fixes change the original AM³'s results as follows (patched vs original C++, at the last checkpoint):
- **`blazar_simple`** (the particles that cool into a window from far above): the Bethe–Heitler and photo-pion $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ components change by up to $34\text{％}$ and $39\text{％}$ around $10^{10}$ eV. They contain $6\text{％}$ and $35\text{％}$ more particles. The total $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ change by up to $34\text{％}$ at $4 \times 10^9\text{–}1.1 \times 10^{10}$ eV, and by up to $10\text{％}$ around 1 MeV, near their peak in $E^2\thinspace dN/dE$. In the first range, the Bethe–Heitler and photo-pion pairs outnumber the tail of the injected electrons (below $10^{-5}$ of the $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ peak in $E^2\thinspace dN/dE$). Around 1 MeV, the cooled electrons flow into an escape-dominated window ($0.7\text{％}$ more $\mathrm{e}^\pm$, $0.2\text{％}$ more energy). The photons change by up to $1.4 \times 10^{-4}$ where they are above $10^{-4}$ of their peak in $E^2\thinspace dN/dE$ ($0.7\text{％}$ in their far radio tail). Of the other fixes, the series of the inverse-Compton cooling functions acts there by less than $10^{-7}$. The injection integral of regime 4 acts by less than $10^{-7}$ above $10^{-4}$ of the peaks, and by up to $2\text{％}$ at $10^{-10}\text{–}10^{-12}$ of them.
- **`leptohadronic_all`** (the solver fixes and the quantum-synchrotron one): the $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ spectrum changes by up to $28\text{％}$ ($9\text{％}$ in energy). The photons change by up to $11\text{％}$ ($3\text{％}$ in energy). Both change mostly through the particles that cool into a window from far above. That fix alone raises them by up to $26\text{％}$ and $9\text{％}$, at 0.6 MeV and 0.03 eV. The $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ components by origin change more at low energies ($\gamma \lesssim 100$). Between $\gamma \approx 10$ and 60, the $\mathrm{pp}$, Bethe–Heitler and photo-pion components are $71\text{–}90\text{％}$ higher. Below $\gamma \approx 10$, they are higher by $39\text{–}74\text{％}$, $74\text{–}94\text{％}$ and $94\text{–}121\text{％}$. Around $\gamma \approx 67$, the $\mathrm{pp}$ component is 12 times higher, and the other two, empty there in the original, appear. In all, the three gain $51\text{％}$, $79\text{％}$ and $96\text{％}$ of their particles. Pions, muons and neutrinos change by up to $36\text{％}$, $35\text{％}$ and $35\text{％}$ near their cutoffs. These changes come mostly from the quadrature of the source integral. That fix alone lowers the pion, muon and neutrino spectra by up to $33\text{％}$, $30\text{％}$ and $31\text{％}$ from $5 \times 10^{17}$, $3 \times 10^{17}$ and $2 \times 10^{17}$ eV up. Above those energies, the spectra are below $7\text{％}$, $0.03\text{％}$ and $0.15\text{％}$ of their peaks. The quantum-synchrotron fix alone changes pions, muons and neutrinos near the cutoffs by $3.5\text{％}$, $4.9\text{％}$ and $2.8\text{％}$ (where they are above $10^{-12}$ of their peaks). The inflow into the escape-dominated (here: decay-dominated) windows raises the muons by up to $11\text{％}$ around $10^{15}$ eV and the pions by up to $9\text{％}$ around $3 \times 10^{16}$ eV.
- **`exotic`** runs on a grid that `update_energy_grid` ends at $3 \times 10^{14}$ eV. The fixes that act here are the solver fixes, the photons on the hadron grid, the external injections and the decay products of injected particles. Where the original's spectra are above $10^{-3}$ of their peaks in $E^2\thinspace dN/dE$, the photo-pion and Bethe–Heitler $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ components rise by up to 2.6 and 1.7 times. Their energy rises by $22\text{％}$ and $10\text{％}$. There, the $\gamma\gamma$ component changes by $-14\text{％}$ to $+8\text{％}$ ($9\text{％}$ less energy), and the total $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ by up to $-6\text{％}$ (less than $0.1\text{％}$ in energy). The photons change there by $-15\text{％}$ to $+7\text{％}$ ($4.5\text{％}$ less energy), and the neutrinos by up to $+61\text{％}$ at $2 \times 10^{13}$ eV ($0.1\text{％}$ in energy). The pions and muons gain $8\text{％}$ of their energy, and the neutrons $14\text{％}$. Most of the gain of pions and muons comes from the protons in the top bins of the grid, which AM³ emptied. The fixes of the top bins alone give $+8\text{％}$, and $+1\text{％}$ in photon energy. The fix of the photons on the hadron grid raises the photo-hadronic yields. On its own, it gives the photo-pion pions $+7\text{％}$ and the neutrons $+8\text{％}$ in energy. It also lowers the photon energy by $6\text{％}$, because the photons that hadrons emit land one bin lower. The fixes of the external injections and of the decay products of injected particles change the spectra by less than $10^{-4}$ there.
- **`ssc_external`** (mainly the maximum-energy estimate with merged $\mathrm{e}^\pm$): the electron injection ends at the estimated cutoff ($6 \times 10^{11}$ eV) instead of the grid top. This removes the $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ and photons above the cutoff and, increasingly towards it, below it. The $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ fall by $49\text{％}$ at $1.4 \times 10^{11}$ eV and by $85\text{％}$ at the cutoff. The photon energy changes by $-25\text{％}$. The shorter injection also changes the spectra at lower energies by $-48\text{％}$ to $+20\text{％}$. The $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ in the injection range change by $+20\text{％}$, and the cooled $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ below it by $-27$ to $-48\text{％}$. The photons at $0.2\text{–}10$ keV change by down to $-43\text{％}$, and the radio and millimetre photons by up to $+18\text{％}$.

The series of the inverse-Compton cooling functions alone changes `leptohadronic_all` by up to $0.2\text{％}$, `exotic` by up to $0.4\text{％}$ and `ssc_external` by up to $1\text{％}$ at any checkpoint. These figures are for the total $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ and photons, in the bins above $10^{-4}$ of the peak in $E^2\thinspace dN/dE$. The series fix also changes one bin at the $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ cutoff of `ssc_external` by $20\text{％}$ ($1.4 \times 10^{12}$ eV, $4 \times 10^{-5}$ of the peak in energy).

## Interface

- **Module name.** The module is `am3_mojo` (AM³: `am3`). `am3_mojo.AM3_API_VERSION` is `"1.0.1"`, the version string of that AM³ (`am3.__version__`).
- **Booleans.** Switch getters return `bool`, as in AM³, and so does `get_verbosity_level()` (AM³: a float, 0.0 or 1.0). Switch setters accept only `True`/`False` (AM³ also accepts any number and `None`) and raise an exception for any other value. Docstrings and the printed switch table say `True`/`False`.
- **Exceptions.** Errors are raised as Python `Exception`, with AM³'s message where it has one. AM³ raises `ValueError` or `RuntimeError` instead (or pybind11's `TypeError` for an argument of the wrong type).
- **GSL errors.** GSL domain errors abort the AM³ process; am3_mojo reports them from the solver.

## Numerics: same equations, not the same rounding

am3_mojo evaluates AM³'s equations in plain double-precision arithmetic, with the platform libm. The compiler is free to fuse multiply-adds and to vectorise, and am3_mojo does not reproduce the rounding of the C++ build. Its results are deterministic and identical for any thread count. They agree with the C++ AM³ with the same fixes (`am3_fixes.patch`) to about $10^{-7}$ or better in most energy bins. In three places, AM³'s result depends on the last bit of a floating-point number. There the four scenarios differ by up to $2 \times 10^{-3}$, in far tails of the spectra. With quantum synchrotron, the first of them moves most bins by about $10^{-5}$ (see [the comparison](../../README.md#reference-comparison)).
- $\boldsymbol{K_{5/3}}$ **integral** (`int_bessel_k53`, used by the quantum-synchrotron kernel). AM³'s loop `y = yi; while (y < yf) { …; y += 0.01 * (yf - yi); }` is meant as 100 steps. Because of rounding in the accumulated `y`, it takes a 101st step for about half of all arguments, which adds a term at the upper limit. am3_mojo always takes the 100 steps. This changes the integral by up to $0.4\text{％}$ and the `leptohadronic_all` spectra by up to $1.0 \times 10^{-3}$ ($1.1 \times 10^{-3}$ with quantum synchrotron on in $B = 1\ \mathrm{kG}$). The C++ build fuses the loop step into a multiply-add. A copy of the C++ loop that does the same would agree with the C++ code to $2.5 \times 10^{-7}$ in `leptohadronic_all` and $2 \times 10^{-7}$ at 1 kG, but am3_mojo does not make one.
- $\boldsymbol{\gamma\gamma}$ **pair kernel lookup tables.** AM³ reads $A_0(h)$ and related functions from tables of step 0.01 with `int(h / 0.01)`, without interpolation. When rounding moves `h` across a table boundary, the neighbouring entry is used. am3_mojo keeps AM³'s tables, so its own results are as accurate as AM³'s, but they can pick a different neighbour than the C++ build does. The effect is up to $2 \times 10^{-3}$ in the far tail of the `exotic` pair spectra (below about $10^{-8}$ of their peak in $E\thinspace dN/dE$ and $10^{-4}$ in $E^2\thinspace dN/dE$). With the full kernel on the default grid, it is a few $10^{-3}$, there also near the peaks of the $\gamma\gamma$-pair components.
- **Inverse-Compton cooling near $\gamma = 1$.** The cooling function $F(\varepsilon, \gamma)$ $= \gamma$ $[f_1(\varepsilon\bar\gamma) - f_1(\varepsilon/\bar\gamma)]$ $- \varepsilon$ $[f_2(\varepsilon\bar\gamma) - f_2(\varepsilon/\bar\gamma)]$ ($\bar\gamma = \gamma + \sqrt{\gamma^2 - 1}$, $\varepsilon$ in units of the particle's rest energy; [Jones 1965](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.137.B1306), eqs. 13–15) subtracts nearly equal numbers when $\gamma \approx 1$. Then 14 of its 16 digits cancel, so both codes get it only to about $10\text{％}$ there. At $\gamma = 1$, both take $\bar\gamma = \gamma + \sqrt{\gamma^2 - 1 + 10^{-10}}$. There the error is up to $14\text{％}$ in the photon bins of the default grid where $F \gt 0$. It reaches $40\text{％}$ in the last of them, next to the change of sign. Both codes evaluate the series of $f_1$ and $f_2$ in the same form (the fix above), so they agree there. The cooling timescale of the lowest bin ($\gamma = 1$) differs by up to $2 \times 10^{-6}$ in the scenarios where the photon fields agree. For photons just below the change of sign of $F$, the difference is $10^{-5}$. The fluxes are unaffected, because the solver keeps that bin empty.

## Misprints of the AM³ paper

Every formula is written in plain arithmetic, as the equation of the papers ([Gao et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.05306); [Klinger et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.13371)). Where the papers give none (the quantum suppression of synchrotron cooling), it is written as the C++ code has it. Where the AM³ paper has misprints, am3_mojo follows the C++ code:

- In the inverse-Compton loss, the cooling functions $f_1$ and $f_2$ have $-2\ \mathrm{Li}_ 2(-2z)$ instead of $+2\ \mathrm{Li}_ 2(-2z)$ and $+\mathrm{Li}_ 2(-2z)$. They go to zero only linearly as $z \to 0$ (as $8{z}$ and $6{z}$ instead of $(8/9)z^3$ and $(4/3)z^2$, so they miss the Thomson limit). The loss rate has $n_\gamma(\varepsilon)/(\gamma^2\varepsilon)$ where its photon density per unit $\varepsilon$ needs $n_\gamma(\varepsilon)/(\gamma^2\varepsilon^2)$. The C++ code has the correct forms, those of [Jones 1965](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.137.B1306) (eqs. 14–15, and eq. 13 apart from its $1/\beta$, see Kept as in AM³). The same section also writes $\sigma_T \propto m_X^{-4}$ for the $m_X^{-2}$ of its own definition. It takes $\varepsilon$ in units of $m_e c^2$, where the formula needs it in units of the particle's rest energy (as the code has it).
- The inelastic $\mathrm{pp}$ cross section (of [Kafexhiu et al. 2014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.7369)) has the threshold factor $[1 - (T_p/E_\mathrm{th})^{1.9}]^3$, which is negative above the threshold. Kafexhiu et al.'s eq. 1 and the code have $[1 - (E_\mathrm{th}/T_p)^{1.9}]^3$.
- The quantum-synchrotron emission kernel has $\xi = 3/(2F_B\gamma)$ for $(3/2)F_B\gamma$, which its own $x = \varepsilon/(\gamma\xi)$ and the argument $2\varepsilon/(3\gamma^2F_B)$ of $F_\xi$ require. It also has $K_{5/3}(z)$ for $K_{2/3}(z)$ in the second term of $F_\xi$ ($z x \xi^2 = y^2/(1 - y)$ with $y = \varepsilon/\gamma$, the factor of $K_{2/3}$ in the quantum-synchrotron spectrum). The code has $(3/2)F_B\gamma$ and $K_{2/3}$, and averages the kernel over the pitch angle, which the paper does not mention. The kernel's prefactor $\sqrt{3}\thinspace\alpha m_e c^2$ is an energy. The code's rate has $m_e c^2/h$ in its place.
- The synchrotron kernel has a factor $(m_X/m_e)^2$ too many for the particles heavier than $\mathrm{e}^\pm$, with $\sigma_T$ and $B_\mathrm{crit}$ of the particle as the paper defines them. With this factor, a proton would radiate about $3.4 \times 10^6$ times the energy its synchrotron cooling removes. The asymptotic form of $\hat R(z)$ for $2z \gt 10$ has $99/{165}$ for $99/{162}$, the coefficient of its asymptotic series. The code has neither.
- The $\mathrm{e}^\pm$ inverse-Compton kernel $\tilde R(u, v)$ (printed alike by [Gao et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.05306)) has $u$ and $v$ interchanged and $(u - 1)^3$ for $(1 - v)^2$. Gao et al. also leave the photon's energy out of $u$. The paper cites the kernel as [Blumenthal & Gould](https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.42.237)'s eq. 2.49, which defines the variables of their kernel, eq. 2.48. In the paper, the total rate (the integral of the kernel) lacks the factor $\sigma_T$. The code has

  $$\tilde R = [6(1 - v)(v/u) \ln(v/[2u(1 - v)]) - 3(v/u - 2 + 2v)(v/u + 1 + v^2/2 - v)]/[4u\gamma(1 - v)^2].$$
- The step cross section of the hadrons' inverse Compton has $H(1 - \tfrac{1}{2}\gamma\varepsilon m_e/m_X)$, which lets a photon take up to twice the particle's energy; the code cuts at $\gamma\varepsilon m_e/m_X = 1/2$, i.e. $H(1 - 2\gamma\varepsilon m_e/m_X)$.

## Kept as in AM³

- AM³ evolves all pion components with its $\pi^+$ solver; am3_mojo gives them solvers with the same configuration.
- The quantum suppression of synchrotron cooling, $1/(1 + \sqrt{2}\thinspace \chi^{2/3})^2$. It has the classical limit and the $\chi^{-4/3}$ scaling of the quantum limit. However, against the exact intensity of [Berestetskii, Lifshitz & Pitaevskii](https://doi.org/10.1016/C2009-0-24486-2) (§90, eq. 90.25), it makes the cooling too slow by $2\text{％}$ at $\chi = 10^{-3}$ and $7\text{–}10\text{％}$ for $0.01 \le \chi \le 0.3$. It does so by $5\text{–}6\text{％}$ for $1 \le \chi \le 10$ and $10\text{％}$ as $\chi \to \infty$ (it tends to $\chi^{-4/3}/2$, their eq. 90.27 to $0.556\ \chi^{-4/3}$). At small $\chi$ it starts as $1 - 2\sqrt{2}\thinspace \chi^{2/3}$ instead of $1 - (55\sqrt{3}/16)\thinspace \chi$ (eq. 90.26). It therefore suppresses the cooling by $0.6\text{％}$ at $\chi = 10^{-4}$, where the true suppression is $0.06\text{％}$. AM³ computes $\chi$ with $1.2 \times 10^{-8}$ eV/G for $m_e c^2/B_\mathrm{cr} = 1.158 \times 10^{-8}$ eV/G, $3.7\text{％}$ high (`tests/literature_checks/derivations/am3_kernels.py`).
- The ultra-relativistic forms of the continuous losses. Synchrotron cooling is $\propto \gamma^2$ (exact: $p^2 = \gamma^2\beta^2$). The inverse-Compton loss $-\dot\gamma$ $= (3/16)\ \sigma_T c \int d\varepsilon\ n(\varepsilon)$ $F(\varepsilon, \gamma)/(\gamma^2 \varepsilon^2)$ ($n$ per unit $\varepsilon$, $\varepsilon$ in units of the particle's rest energy) is $\beta$ times the exact Klein–Nishina loss rate in an isotropic photon field. Here $\sigma_T$ is that of the particle, $\propto m^{-2}$, and $F$ is the cooling function above. The exact rate has $\gamma p$ instead of $\gamma^2$ in the denominator, as in [Jones](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.137.B1306)' eq. 13, with $\beta\gamma^2 = \gamma p$. $F$ itself is exact. It is checked against a direct integration of the Klein–Nishina cross section to $10^{-13}$ (`tests/literature_checks/derivations/am3_cooling.py`). With the series fix, both codes evaluate it to $5 \times 10^{-5}$ for $\gamma \gtrsim 7$, $5.5 \times 10^{-5}$ at $\gamma \approx 4.5$ and $1.6 \times 10^{-4}$ at $\gamma \approx 2.7$. The largest errors lie just below $\varepsilon\bar\gamma = 10$, where their 20-step quadrature of $\mathrm{Li}_ 2(-2z)$ is least accurate before they switch to its asymptotic series. In the last photon bin before $F$ changes sign, the error is up to $3.3 \times 10^{-4}$ for $1.6 \lesssim \gamma \lesssim 2.5$ and $6 \times 10^{-4}$ at $\gamma \approx 1.5$. Both ultra-relativistic forms differ from the exact rates by more than $1\text{％}$ for $\gamma \lesssim 7$ (inverse Compton, $\beta \lt 0.99$) and $\gamma \lesssim 10$ (synchrotron, $\gamma^2/(\gamma^2 - 1) \gt 1.01$). At $\gamma = 3$, they differ by $-6\text{％}$ and $+12.5\text{％}$. In the lowest bin the solver keeps ($\gamma = 1.22$), $\beta = 0.57$. $F$ is negative where the photons are more energetic than the particle ($\varepsilon \gt 0.87\gamma$ at $\gamma = 3$, $\varepsilon \gt 0.98\gamma$ at $\gamma = 10$, tending to $\varepsilon \gt \gamma$ at large $\gamma$; $\varepsilon \gtrsim (4/3)(\gamma^2 - 1)$ as $\gamma \to 1$). The particle then gains energy from the photons. Both codes set $F$ to zero there, as their solvers treat only losses.
- The `optimize_*_grid` switches are C++ `bool`s that always read as `True`. Their "every 2nd grid point" code paths are unreachable in AM³ and are not implemented.
